# Mineral County, Montana: disclosure litigation research draft

**As of 2026-10-26 UTC; retrieved 2026-10-26. Unpublished research notes, not legal advice.** This distinguishes the 2022 Jasper/Kilby mandamus proceedings, the later Funke/Allard dispute, and the independent *Patrick/Ovitt/Green* supervisory-control decision. “Brady cop” is a colloquial label, not a court-imposed status. A duty to disclose, admissibility, officer qualification, and suppression are different questions. Case captions are used where necessary; no victim or witness contact information is reproduced.

## Bottom line and evidence hierarchy

* **Signed primary order located:** Montana Supreme Court, *Patrick et al. v. Montana Fourth Judicial District Court*, **OP 24-0341**, dated August 13, 2024 (court-hosted PDF, scanned signatures). It reverses the district court's suppression reversals in **State v. Patrick, DC-23-35 (TK-595-2022-0001683); State v. Ovitt, DC-23-34 (TK-595-2022-0001772); State v. Green, DC-23-36 (TK-595-2022-0000884)**. This is a statutory peace-officer-qualification/suppression ruling, **not a holding that Kunzelman committed a Brady violation in every case**. The Court denied supervisory control to the other eight petitioners for failure to serve Judge Larson and because he had made no ruling, **not** on their merits.[1]
* **Other signed district-court orders/docket entries not independently obtained.** NBC Montana linked Scribd copies of Vannatta's October 2022 mandamus orders, but the retrievable Scribd pages exposed only AI-generated descriptions, not the signed pages; the descriptions and press accounts are leads, **not inspected order text**. The May 2024 public defender's Supreme Court petition describes the sheriff writ, its court-appointed supervisor and exhibits but is a party filing, not itself the order. Subsequent district-court outcomes below are attributed to reporting, pending direct docket verification.[2][14][15]
* **Later endpoint is not January 2026.** A December 2025 order requiring disclosure of certain Funke allegations was vacated in relevant part in April 2026; after a May 13 evidentiary hearing, Vannatta orally ruled the allegations **not Brady material**, according to two contemporaneous reports. Neither a signed May 2026 written order nor an appeal disposition was retrieved. Do not repeat January's headline as the current holding.[11][12][13]

## Kunzelman: what the holdings actually establish

The August 2024 Supreme Court order finds the required mental-health evaluations had been performed by a physician assistant, not a qualifying licensed physician or mental-health professional; the State did not dispute noncompliance. Under § 7-32-303(5), the deputies forfeited statutory peace-officer authority at the times in question. The court said private-person arrest powers did not authorize the investigations/searches and ordered reversal of Vannatta's **three** suppression reversals. It did **not** decide all potentially affected prosecutions or adjudicate the 2013 test-answer allegation. Its denial as to the eight others was procedural, without a merits resolution.[1]

The earlier Halligan and Marks descriptions in the Daily Montanan are distinct: Halligan reportedly found an untimely disclosure; Marks reportedly found **no** Brady violation in his particular case while insisting on broader, timely disclosure and warning of sanctions. At least nine dismissals and a potential universe exceeding 100 were reported in 2022 but need case-level docket corroboration. Likewise alleged defective stops and blood-test requests were described from litigation exhibits, not verified signed orders here.[3]

## DOJ / DCI / POST: keep the claims separate

* Jasper **hoped** the Montana Attorney General or U.S. Attorney would investigate, according to contemporaneous interviews. This is a request/aspiration, **not evidence either agency opened an investigation into Kunzelman or the mandamus matter**.[3][4][5]
* The Montana Supreme Court says POST and the sheriff had treated the deputies as certified, but held that did not overcome the statutory evaluation defect. POST certification status is **not** proof of an independent DOJ Division of Criminal Investigation (DCI) misconduct inquiry. The 2024 petition also cites a county notice identifying deputies without compliant evaluations.[1][2]
* Distinct POST matters in reporting: a 2022 lawsuit allegedly appended a POST communication on Visintin's eligibility and a letter concerning Nobles; a 2024 column describes POST's **Allard** employment investigation. Neither supports a claim that POST formally disciplined **Kunzelman** or that DCI investigated him. The apparent reference to a DOJ major-crimes agent in 2026 election coverage concerns candidate Ben Banks, not the existence of a DCI investigation into these disclosure cases.[3][7][10]
* **Open question:** no DOJ/DCI investigative opening/closing document, case number, POST disciplinary decision for Kunzelman, or agency response to a records request was located in this public-web pass. Do not describe a “separate DOJ DCI/POST investigation” as established; an investigation of an unrelated Mineral County incident is not this case.

## Follow-up primary-record targets and publication cautions

1. Obtain the **signed** October 2022 sheriff and county-attorney mandamus orders; settlement, supervisor Long's report, compliance and dismissal/fee orders; verify case captions, docket numbers, exact filing dates and what was finally discharged. Scribd's visible AI summaries are not substitutes for pages.[2][14][15]
2. Obtain signed Halligan and Marks criminal orders and dockets; disaggregate delayed disclosure from an actual Brady violation, dismissal, or suppression. Check whether “nine dismissed” means orders of dismissal and identify the grounds before publishing a total.[3]
3. Obtain Funke-v-commissioners November 2024 Allard ruling; December 10, 2025 Funke ruling; April 2026 vacatur; **May 2026 written ruling/minute entry and any appeal**. The December 2025 disclosure headline was superseded in part. Seek POST Allard documents separately and check any later Allard civil suit disposition.[7][9][12]
4. The Montana courts describe a [district-court public portal](https://courts.mt.gov/Courts/portals) and [Mineral County clerk's official docket office](https://co.mineral.mt.us/departments/district-court). This pass could retrieve the official Supreme Court PDF but not a usable district-court case-file view; the browser service returned HTTP 402 and the public portal landing page did not expose search results through text extraction. A clerk/portal search and records request would be needed to state that no later ruling exists. Avoid implying that absence from web indexing is absence from a docket.
5. Preserve the subjects' denials and procedural reversals. Do not equate allegations, sealed investigative material, settlement, or POST administrative review with proven perjury, criminal guilt, or a universal “Brady list” designation. Do not publish personal identifiers from underlying criminal case exhibits.
