# Tipp malpractice and Lowney ODC grievance: civil intake case analysis

### Executive snapshot

This page consolidates the existing ODC 25-147 / Lowney case-authorities analysis with the legal-malpractice referral brief titled **Legal Malpractice Referral and Intake Brief - Elvis Nuno v. Bryan Tipp**.

It is designed for civil malpractice counsel, civil-rights counsel, legal clinics, journalists, and oversight reviewers who need a compact intake map of:

1. **The Tipp malpractice track.** Alleged criminal-defense malpractice by Bryan C. Tipp arising from the same Missoula criminal proceedings, including failure to investigate favorable evidence, failure to warn about or preserve civil claims, failure to challenge GPS monitoring after medical documentation of harm, and negotiation or acceptance of a Deferred Prosecution Agreement (DPA) condition restricting protected speech.
2. **The Lowney prosecutorial-misconduct track.** The April 28, 2026 grievance against Deputy County Attorney Brian Lowney and the Missoula County Attorney's Office, alleging Brady/Giglio violations, reliance on conflicted or tainted investigative work, prosecution of protected speech, coercive charging/bail practices, and DPA speech restrictions.

The two tracks are distinct. The Tipp track is a civil-malpractice intake track tied to ODC 25-147, which the current case file treats as denied on review and closed; however, no notice of the review denial was received, so the justification used by ODC is unknown. The May 2026 supplemental filings by Elvis Nuno, Tyrone Nuno, and Eleanor M. Nuno have now been submitted as new supplemental material, functionally reopening the Tipp matter for reconsideration while also opening a separate or expanded investigative track arising from Eleanor Nuno's independent grievance. The Lowney track remains a prosecutorial-misconduct grievance that the local GitBook record treats as pending or unresolved unless later ODC documentation proves otherwise.

### Merged malpractice-intake theories

#### 1. Missed civil-rights and tort preservation

The intake brief's strongest civil-malpractice theory is not simply "Tipp did not file a civil lawsuit." The stronger theory is that criminal defense counsel allegedly gave advice or signals that discouraged civil action, failed to warn about limitation periods, failed to refer civil counsel, and allowed claims to age while the criminal matter remained unresolved.

For intake purposes, counsel should separate the alleged lost claims into buckets:

| Claim bucket | Intake status |
| --- | --- |
| §1983 false arrest / unlawful detention / seizure claims | Needs claim-by-claim accrual analysis under federal law. Wallace defeats a blanket "wait until the criminal case ends" rule for false-arrest-type claims. |
| §1983 malicious-prosecution / fabricated-evidence / First Amendment retaliation claims | Needs favorable-termination, probable-cause, Heck/McDonough/Thompson-style accrual review. Do not assume all accrued on the same date. |
| Defamation | Use a two-year Montana libel/slander period, not three years. Publication dates, republication, and continuing-publication rules require counsel review. |
| False imprisonment | § 27-2-204(3) also lists false imprisonment as a two-year claim. |
| General tort / tortious interference / IIED | Often analyzed under general tort limitations, but the precise theory and injury date matter. |
| Legal malpractice against Tipp | Treat as attorney/professional negligence and calculate under § 27-2-206 from actual or reasonably diligent discovery of the alleged act, error, or omission, subject to the ten-year repose cap. |

The source brief alleges damages in the $8.5-10.5 million range, including lost civil claims, career destruction, contract interference, psychiatric harm, and constitutional damages. Those figures should be treated as claimed intake values requiring expert damages proof, business records, medical records, contract records, and causation analysis.

#### 2. Failure to investigate favorable evidence

The P.S. email and the Stratford letter materially sharpen the existing page's malpractice framing.

* The P.S. email is not just a character exhibit. It is also alleged impeachment evidence because the complainant forwarded favorable information to law enforcement within minutes, supporting a YWCA-law-enforcement information-sharing theory.
* The Stratford letter is not just medical background. It allegedly gave Tipp direct notice that GPS monitoring and ongoing proceedings were causing psychiatric deterioration, creating a concrete basis for a motion to modify release conditions.
* The Brueckner conflict is especially revealing because the alleged YWCA board position was not hidden behind deep discovery or forensic work; according to the request-for-review materials, it was the kind of conflict a minimal Google search would have surfaced in minutes. If counsel failed to perform even that baseline investigation, ODC's willingness to describe the resulting omission as "strategy" becomes the problem, not the explanation.
* The Tyrone and Eleanor witness grievances add a failure-to-interview theory: both parents allege they were available and material witnesses, yet were not interviewed about Officer Smith's contacts/removal, family-pressure tactics, and fabricated or out-of-context statements attributed to Eleanor.

Civil counsel should request the discovery item 2018-37193, the April 10, 2019 Stratford letter, the March 2018 Ethan Smith complaint, all charging affidavits, all bail/release-condition orders, and the DPA.

#### 3. DPA speech-waiver and defense-counsel duty

The DPA issue bridges both tracks. On the prosecution side, the Lowney grievance alleges the State used dismissal leverage to require removal or suppression of speech already treated as protected. On the defense side, the malpractice theory is that Tipp should have challenged the term, obtained informed consent if the term was unavoidable, or preserved the issue for later review.

Rumery prevents overstatement: criminal-resolution waivers are not automatically invalid. The intake question is whether this specific term was voluntary, informed, free of prosecutorial overreach, and supported by a legitimate public-protection purpose rather than institutional reputation management.

Wells adds a useful criminal-condition lens: when a criminal condition burdens constitutional rights, it must be reasonably related to legitimate sentencing or supervision goals and no broader than reasonably necessary. That framework is not a perfect DPA match, but it helps identify the questions civil counsel should ask about tailoring, duration, public-safety rationale, and alternatives.

A related national policy bears on the evidence-handling allegations against both Tipp and Lowney, not just the DPA speech term: ABA Resolution 113E (adopted August 2013, not 2018) opposes plea/sentencing waivers of post-conviction claims involving ineffective assistance, prosecutorial misconduct, or destruction of evidence. See the inventory below for how that third prong maps onto the specific suppression and non-disclosure allegations in this case file. See also [Institutional Willful Blindness: Pattern Analysis of Complaint-Suppression — Destruction-of-Evidence Doctrine](../institutional-willful-blindness-pattern-analysis.md#destruction-of-evidence-doctrine-a-second-independent-theory) for a distinct, independent legal theory: that the DPA's Google-review-removal condition itself — separate from the underlying speech-suppression harm — implicates MCA § 45-7-207 (tampering with or fabricating physical evidence), MRPC 3.4(a) (a lawyer's duty not to destroy or counsel destruction of a document with potential evidentiary value), and Montana's civil spoliation-of-evidence tort, because the review functioned as a documentary record with evidentiary value independent of its protected-speech status.

### Lowney / Brady-Giglio track

The Lowney grievance is strongest where it can prove three things:

1. The information allegedly withheld was favorable, either exculpatory or impeachment material.
2. The information was known to the prosecution team or to law enforcement actors functioning on the State's behalf.
3. The nondisclosure was material to probable cause, bail, DPA leverage, or the defense's ability to challenge the case.

The specific Brady/Giglio proof targets are:

* Officer Ethan Smith's alleged removal for appearance of impropriety and unbecoming conduct.
* Detective Brueckner's alleged YWCA board role while investigating YWCA-centered allegations.
* The P.S. email forwarding chain.
* Eleanor Nuno's denial that she made statements attributed to her about severe mental illness or dangerousness.
* Any hearing transcript or order reflecting that the YWCA complaint was protected speech.

Temple is useful but limiting. It confirms modern Brady framing, but it also warns that constructive knowledge has boundaries and that materiality remains essential. This page therefore treats investigator-conflict evidence as a strong but proof-dependent theory.

### Bottom line

The merged intake analysis supports three public-facing conclusions:

1. **Tipp malpractice track:** The strongest theory is not merely failure to file civil claims; it is alleged deadline misinformation or silence, failure to refer, failure to investigate favorable evidence, and failure to challenge conditions and DPA terms that allegedly harmed the client.
2. **Lowney prosecutorial track:** The strongest theory remains Brady/Giglio plus protected-speech prosecution, but it depends on proof that the challenged information was favorable, prosecution-team knowledge can be established, and materiality can be tied to probable cause, bail, DPA leverage, or dismissal posture.
3. **ODC posture:** ODC 25-147 is recorded here as review-denied and closed, with the denial rationale unknown because no notice was received. The May 2026 supplemental submissions now function as reconsideration material for the Tipp matter and as new or expanded investigative material for Eleanor Nuno's independent grievance.
4. **Verification priority:** The page should not repeat the intake brief's inconsistent ODC posture or incorrect defamation limitation period. Civil counsel should use the corrected timeline and limitations framework above as the working intake checklist.

### Evidence Suppression and the ABA Resolution 113E Evidence-Destruction Prong

ABA Resolution 113E (adopted August 2013) is national policy opposing plea or sentencing agreements that require a defendant to waive post-conviction claims involving, among other things, **destruction of evidence**. See [When a Disciplinary Complaint Closes: Transparency, Evidence, and Public Understanding — Comparative Professional-Ethics Authority](../transparency-and-public-understanding-attorney-discipline.md#comparative-professional-ethics-authority-on-waiver-conditioned-dispositions) for the full national and state-authority survey of that policy. Resolution 113E does not itself adjudicate whether evidence was suppressed or destroyed in any given case; it addresses whether a defendant may be made to waive a *claim* arising from such conduct as the price of a favorable disposition. What follows is a source-attributed inventory of every instance, already alleged in the submitted ODC grievances against both Bryan Tipp (defense counsel) and Brian Lowney (prosecutor), that the complainants characterize as suppression of, or failure to act on, material evidence — the category of conduct Resolution 113E's third prong addresses. **Each item below is an allegation drawn from a specific submitted grievance, not an independent factual finding by this site, ODC, or any court.**

#### Reading the inventory against Resolution 113E

None of the items above describes a waiver of a claim in exchange for a plea or dismissal benefit — that is a separate, narrower question addressed in the DPA speech-waiver discussion above and in the transparency report's comparative-authority section. What this inventory documents is the alleged underlying conduct — suppression, non-disclosure, or failure to act on evidence — that Resolution 113E's third prong (post-conviction claims involving "destruction of evidence") is designed to make sure a defendant is never forced to sign away the right to raise. The practical link between the two: if the DPA's speech-removal and silence conditions functioned as this site's other reports allege, then Mr. Nuno was made to give up his public voice at the same time these evidence-handling allegations were left institutionally unexamined by either his own counsel or the prosecution — the precise pairing (a waiver term plus an unexamined evidentiary record) that Resolution 113E's policy rationale treats as a problem, independent of whether the specific waiver here falls within the resolution's literal claim categories.

**This inventory does not establish that any suppression was intentional, that any specific piece of evidence would have changed the outcome, or that ODC's "strategy" characterization of Tipp's conduct was incorrect as a matter of law.** It compiles what the submitted grievances themselves allege, with each item traceable to its source paragraph, so that a reader — including ODC itself — can evaluate each allegation individually rather than as an undifferentiated "strategy" dispute.
