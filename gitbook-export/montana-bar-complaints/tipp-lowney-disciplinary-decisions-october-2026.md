## Two files, two different stages

The Montana Supreme Court has affirmed the Commission on Practice's dismissal of Elvis R. Nuno's grievance against defense attorney **Bryan C. Tipp**, ODC File No. **25-147**. In a separate matter involving prosecutor **Brian C. Lowney**, ODC File No. **26-145**, the Commission affirmed an earlier ODC dismissal in a letter dated October 23, 2026. The Lowney letter describes an opportunity to request discretionary Supreme Court review; it is not a Supreme Court decision. [Tipp order, p. 1; Lowney letter, pp. 1–2; editorial evidence inventory below.]

## Tipp: the Court's timing and request-framing rationale

The [Tipp grievance index](odc-25-147-bryan-tipp-montana-bar-complaint-index.md) describes Nuno's allegations about his former defense lawyer. The Court's two-page order says ODC determined that the grievance did not allege a rule violation warranting discipline and notified Nuno of dismissal by letter dated **November 3, 2025**. It says the Commission affirmed and notified him by letter dated **February 9, 2026**, which advised that he could ask the Supreme Court to review the disposition within 30 days of the date the notice was sent. [Tipp order, p. 1.]

The Court recounts that Nuno requested review on **August 13, 2026**. It characterizes his request as not asking the Court to review the grievance on its merits, but asking for “direction on the proper filing path and clarification of the review posture.” The order calls the request untimely, says Nuno is not entitled to further relief on that request, and orders that the Commission's decision **“is AFFIRMED.”** The copy's first page bears a **FILED 10/22/2026** stamp; the handwritten day in the signature-page date is not relied upon here as an independently certain signed date. [Tipp order, pp. 1–2.]

A [September 23 Commission letter](odc-25-147-september-23-2026-supreme-court-transmittal.md) had documented transmission of Nuno's August request and the ODC file to the Court. Transmission alone did not prove the Court had accepted discretionary merits review; the later order supplies the disposition that the earlier letter could not. The September letter reports a **January 7, 2026** Commission affirmance, whereas the Court order describes a **February 9, 2026 notice letter**. Those are different propositions and may describe decision and notice dates, but the underlying Commission decision/notice must be compared before treating them as reconciled. Neither date should be silently substituted for the other. [September 23 Commission letter as reproduced in the linked article; Tipp order, p. 1.]

## Lowney: Commission affirmance, not a Court ruling

The Commission's two-page letter identifies Lowney as the respondent in ODC File No. **26-145** and says it considered Nuno's appeal from ODC's **June 9, 2026** dismissal. Its review panel considered the original complaint, ODC's file and original report, and Nuno's **July 14, 2026** appeal letter. The panel concluded the complaint's facts did not demonstrate **clear and convincing evidence of ethical violations** by Lowney, the standard the letter states must be met. The Commission therefore affirmed ODC's dismissal and said it had no further authority over the complaint after that affirmance. [Lowney letter, p. 1; compare the earlier [ODC dismissal record](odc-26-145-rejection-brian-lowney-june-9-2026.md).]

That stated panel rationale differs from the earlier ODC letter's limitations rationale. The present letter does not provide an allegation-by-allegation account of the panel's evaluation. It is inaccurate to call this grievance pending before the Commission, but it would also be inaccurate to say the Montana Supreme Court decided it. [Lowney letter, p. 1.]

The October 23 letter quotes Rule 14 and says Nuno could submit a written request to the Commission within **30 days from the date of that letter**, explaining the basis for review and the alleged misconduct with factual support; it says the Commission would forward the file to the Court. Supreme Court review is described as discretionary. The [court-adopted 2021 Rule 14(A)](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=368130), however, measures its 30 days from when notice **was sent** and says to make the written request **to the Supreme Court**. We report the letter's instruction, not endorse it as a substitute for that rule. The actual sending date and any subsequent request, forwarding, or Court action have not been verified; no individualized filing deadline is asserted. See the [procedural explainer](montana-attorney-discipline-review-process.md) and seek prompt qualified advice on any filing question. [Lowney letter, pp. 1–2; 2021 rules, PDF p. 16.]

## What the outcomes mean—and do not mean

These are adverse outcomes for Nuno in **attorney discipline**. MISJustice Alliance has criticized the handling of the grievances, but that criticism is editorial argument, not a finding that either attorney committed misconduct or that the institutions acted with improper intent. Conversely, affirming a disciplinary dismissal does not decide every factual allegation, prove that all alleged conduct did not occur, or adjudicate a separate malpractice or civil-rights case. Civil claims have their own elements, evidence, defenses, and limitation rules. A grievance or request for reconsideration should not be assumed to extend a civil filing period. See the [procedural explainer](montana-attorney-discipline-review-process.md) for the limited rule context.
