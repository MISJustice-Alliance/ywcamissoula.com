# ODC File No. 25-147 – Clarification Request Regarding July 10, 2026 Letter and Supplemental Submission #4

> Publication note: the mailing address supplied in the original email is omitted from this publication at the author's request.

Dear Chief Disciplinary Counsel Bucy,

I write regarding your July 10, 2026 letter concerning ODC File No. 25-147, my grievance against attorney Bryan C. Tipp. That letter states that “the newly provided information and testimony does not contain any new or changed information, or allegations that have not already been addressed in ODC’s November determination,” and warns that “ethical grievances may not be pursued in a serial fashion.”

Respectfully, that characterization does not accurately reflect the contents or scope of my May 2026 supplemental submission (“Supplemental Submission #4”), publicly indexed as MT Bar Complaint ODC No. 25-147: Supplemental Submission #4 (May 2026). As stated in its introduction, that filing expressly “does not re-argue theories addressed in prior filings” and instead “presents two narrow allegations tied to concrete documentary evidence” drawn from discovery in State v. Nuno. I structured it specifically to avoid repetitive, “serial” grievance-pursuit and to isolate two discrete, evidence-specific concerns about Mr. Tipp’s handling of exculpatory and mitigation evidence.

For clarity, Supplemental Submission #4 addresses only the following two matters:

1. **Failure to investigate and use exculpatory character-witness evidence (“P.S. Email”).** I allege that Mr. Tipp failed to investigate, develop, or deploy a third-party character-witness email dated August 14, 2018 (the “P.S. Email,” discovery document 2018-37193). That email directly rebutted the prosecution’s portrayal of me as dangerous to women and provided impeachment material regarding the complainant’s credibility and institutional conflicts, yet it was never meaningfully used in motion practice, negotiations, or case-resolution strategy.

2. **Failure to act on documented psychiatric harm from GPS monitoring (Stratford letter).** I further allege that Mr. Tipp failed to act on a psychiatric evaluation letter dated April 10, 2019, from board-certified forensic psychiatrist William D. Stratford, M.D., F.A.P.A. That letter documented that GPS ankle monitoring imposed as a condition of pretrial release was causing severe psychiatric harm, functional impairment, and risk of regression, but Mr. Tipp did not seek to modify conditions of release for approximately twenty months thereafter, despite this evidence and the ongoing impact on my health and ability to function.

These are not generalized complaints about outcome or scope of representation; they concern specific discovery documents and specific duties of competence and diligence in the use of exculpatory and mitigation evidence. They were not separately developed in my prior filings, and they are not simply a re-statement of the earlier civil-claims-preservation theory that formed much of ODC’s November 2025 discussion. On the face of the record, they appear to be “new or changed” allegations that the November determination did not reach on their merits.

In light of this, I respectfully request:

1. **Allegation-specific explanation.** A written explanation of how ODC determined that each of these two narrow allegations “does not contain any new or changed information” and “has already been addressed in ODC’s November determination.” If ODC considers them subsumed in the prior ruling, I ask that you identify the page and section of that determination which, in your view, resolves (a) Mr. Tipp’s handling of the August 14, 2018 P.S. Email and (b) Mr. Tipp’s response to the April 10, 2019 Stratford letter.

2. **Confirmation of exhibit-level review.** Confirmation whether ODC reviewed the P.S. Email (discovery document 2018-37193), the April 10, 2019 Stratford letter, and the related narrative and chronology attached to Supplemental Submission #4, including the malpractice chronology and dataset materials catalogued in the Bryan Tipp malpractice index.

3. **Clarification of “serial grievance” framing.** Clarification of how ODC is applying the “ethical grievances may not be pursued in a serial fashion” language in circumstances where a complainant submits a narrowly tailored, exhibit-specific filing that explicitly disclaims re-arguing prior theories and instead raises distinct, discovery-based issues not squarely addressed in a prior dismissal.

To avoid further miscommunication, I will preserve your written response as part of the public documentation record concerning this matter. This documentation is hosted in a research and advocacy capacity through MISJustice Alliance and the YWCAofMissoula.com site, so that any future oversight body or court can understand both the substance of my filings and ODC’s responses.

I am not seeking to reopen every issue previously raised in ODC File No. 25-147. My intent here is limited: to ensure that the two discrete, discovery-based allegations in Supplemental Submission #4 either receive targeted consideration, or that ODC provides a clear, written explanation of why it believes they were already adjudicated in November 2025. Because the underlying matter implicates substantial civil-rights interests and broader questions about attorney oversight, I expect that future reviewers will look closely at how disciplinary bodies engage with such evidence-specific allegations.

I therefore ask that your response address these points directly, so that any further advocacy, referral, or review can accurately reflect ODC’s reasoning.

Thank you for your attention to this request.

Sincerely,

Elvis R. Nuno
