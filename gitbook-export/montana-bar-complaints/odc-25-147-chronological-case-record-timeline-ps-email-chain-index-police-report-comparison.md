## 1. Executive Overview

ODC File No. 25-147 is a Montana attorney-discipline matter opened in July 2025 by Elvis Ryland Nuno against Bryan Charles Tipp of Tipp Coburn Lockwood P.C., Missoula, Montana. The complaint concerns Tipp's representation of Nuno in criminal stalking proceedings in Missoula County (approximately 2018–2021). The criminal charges were dismissed with prejudice. The bar complaint was closed by the Montana Office of Disciplinary Counsel (ODC) in November 2025, a review request was filed, and the matter was again closed in correspondence dated July 10 and July 30, 2026.[^1]

Three documentary sub-issues drive the current advocacy and research record: (1) an August 14, 2018 third-party email (the "PS email," discovery document 2018-37193) that the complaint alleges was favorable character evidence unused by defense counsel; (2) allegations that statements attributed to Eleanor ("Ellie") Nuno in charging documents were fabricated and that Tipp failed to develop her as a denial witness; and (3) the trajectory of the Edmonds/Seattle police-report versions that underlie the parallel Washington-state record. Each is organized below.

## 8. Montana Narrative Chronology (ODC 25-147 Focused)

### The Criminal Case Foundation (2018–2021)

The criminal proceedings in State v. Nuno, Missoula County, form the factual foundation for the bar complaint. Stalking charges were filed approximately July 2018. Bryan Tipp was retained on approximately September 12, 2018. A deferred prosecution agreement was signed December 6, 2020, ending GPS monitoring. Dismissal with prejudice was entered approximately December 23, 2021.[^1]

The parties agree on the fact of dismissal with prejudice. They dispute what that dismissal means. Tipp's ODC response characterizes the dismissal as the product of "competent and vigorous defense" and lists several substantive pretrial motions filed by Tipp. The Tyrone Nuno grievance states: "the case came to an end only after my ex-wife and I directly demanded that it be resolved. That is not a description of vigorous advocacy producing an optimal result." These are conflicting accounts of the same outcome; neither constitutes a formal finding.[^2][^1]

### Officer Ethan Smith and the Investigative Transition

Beginning approximately June 2017, Officer Ethan Smith of the Missoula Police Department is alleged by the complainants to have made repeated uninvited contacts with Tyrone Nuno and Ellie Nuno regarding their son. In March 2018, Tyrone Nuno filed a formal written complaint with Sergeant Dustin DelRidge. The complaint characterized Smith's conduct as harassment and "fishing for information." Following conversations with DelRidge, Smith was reportedly removed from all matters involving Elvis Nuno and E'Lise Chard, with the removal attributed to "appearance of impropriety and unbecoming conduct."[^2]

The complainants assert that Tipp was aware of the removal, was present at meetings with the family during this period, and never interviewed Tyrone Nuno regarding the complaint, the substance of the conversations with DelRidge, or the reasons for the transfer. Tipp's response does not address the Smith removal in specific terms; it addresses the Brueckner connection by acknowledging it as potentially useful impeachment material that became moot given the dismissal.[^2][^1]

### The PS Email and YWCA Information Routing

On August 14, 2018, at 9:15 PM MDT, P.S. sent an email to the YWCA of Missoula general inbox praising Elvis Nuno's character and criticizing E'Lise Chard. Within 19 minutes, Chard forwarded the email to Detective Arianna Adams and Kim Harvey with the note: "Not sure if you want this, since it's purportedly not from Elvis, but I figured I'd send it anyway." Kim Harvey re-forwarded the chain to Detective Adams on August 23, 2018.[^5]

The document entered discovery as record 2018-37193 and was available to Tipp upon his retention in September 2018. Supplemental Submission #4 asserts that no record of Tipp contacting P.S., submitting the email at a bail hearing, or filing any motion citing the exhibit has been identified.

The source analysis identifies three defense uses Tipp allegedly failed to develop: (1) character evidence rebutting the prosecution's "danger to women" narrative (relevant to escalating bail amounts of $50,000, $75,000, and $100,000); (2) impeachment evidence showing Chard was actively routing communications about her own conduct into police channels; and (3) a basis for a Brady/Giglio motion challenging the integrity and impartiality of the investigation.

### Fabricated Statements Attributed to Ellie Nuno

Eleanor M. Nuno's May 2026 independent grievance states that the charging documents "relied heavily on fabricated statements that police attributed to me — specifically, that I had communicated to detectives that my son 'suffered from severe mental illness.'" She states: "I never made these statements. Mr. Tipp was in possession of this information, never interviewed me as a witness, and never used my denial of these fabricated statements as a defense."

Tipp's ODC response does not address this specific allegation. The response does not claim Tipp interviewed Eleanor Nuno regarding statements attributed to her in the charging documents. The charging document text itself is not reproduced in the available public documents, so independent verification of the exact language and its source cannot be performed from this record.[^1]

### The DPA and First Amendment Conditions

The December 6, 2020 DPA is referenced in multiple documents. The source analysis materials allege the DPA contained conditions requiring removal of protected speech or online content as a condition of the non-prosecution agreement. Supplemental #3 describes alleged pressure on Nuno to surrender device passwords. Ellie Nuno's grievance states: "Mr. Tipp additionally pressured Elvis to surrender passwords for electronic devices seized under what Elvis describes as an unlawful warrant, and required Elvis to surrender First Amendment rights as a condition of the non-prosecution agreement negotiated with the prosecutor—matters my son describes as never having been adequately explained or fairly counseled to him."

The full text of the DPA is not reproduced in the available public documents. No official finding has been made regarding the conditions of the DPA or their constitutionality.

### ODC Proceedings and Closure Rationale

The ODC complaint was filed July 2025. Tipp's response was filed September 23, 2025. ODC dismissed the matter November 3, 2025. A request for Commission on Practice review was filed November 18, 2025; the Commission affirmed the dismissal. Supplemental Submission #4 was filed May 2026 with two new exhibit-specific allegations. Parent grievances were submitted May 30, 2026. ODC issued a July 10, 2026 letter stating the submissions contained "no new or changed information" not already addressed in the November determination. A clarification request was filed in July 2026 asking ODC to identify where the November determination addressed the PS email and Stratford letter. A final July 30, 2026 closure letter from Chief Disciplinary Counsel Pamela D. Bucy characterized all contested conduct as attorney "strategy."[^4][^2][^1]

The site analysis notes that the July 30 letter does not separately analyze: the alleged Brueckner conflict; Smith's removal for apparent impropriety; fabricated statements attributed to Eleanor Nuno; the Stratford letter; the PS email; alleged unlawful entry and detention; alleged interception of exculpatory character-witness communications; refusal to send a conflict-of-interest advocacy letter; the 18-month delay; the DPA conditions; or the First Amendment speech-removal allegation. These are described as open items by the complainants; ODC's "strategy" conclusion is the final recorded official position.
