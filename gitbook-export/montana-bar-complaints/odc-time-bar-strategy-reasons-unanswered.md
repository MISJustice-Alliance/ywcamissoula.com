## The update

In one grievance, Montana's Office of Disciplinary Counsel (ODC) invoked a time limit rather than deciding the alleged prosecutorial conduct. In another, it applied a broad “strategy” rationale to later, document-specific allegations without showing how the documents fit that rationale. Those are reasons for closure, but they leave the complainant asking what, exactly, was resolved. On November 4, Elvis R. Nuno asked ODC to identify the written facts and reasons addressing a specific allegation in the Lowney grievance. The supplied correspondence ends with his request; it cannot establish whether ODC replied afterward. The central question is not whether the letters contain *any* reason. It is whether their reasons are intelligible as applied to the complaints. [Lowney dismissal and Commission decision](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026). The November 4 email is identified by date in the source note below.

The [earlier report, published November 3](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026), described two distinct outcomes: the Montana Supreme Court affirmed the Commission's dismissal in ODC 25-147, involving defense lawyer Bryan C. Tipp, on an untimely request; a Commission review panel affirmed ODC's dismissal in ODC 26-145, involving Lowney. Neither outcome establishes that every alleged act occurred or that either attorney committed misconduct. The Tipp order did not decide Nuno's underlying allegations on their merits; no Supreme Court decision in the Lowney matter was verified in the reviewed records. [Order and Commission letter reproductions](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/odc-review-evidence-ledger-2026).

## The correspondence, in order

In the Tipp matter, ODC acknowledged the grievance on July 21, 2025. Tipp's lawyer responded September 23, disputing the allegations and describing the challenged choices as strategy; those are the respondent's positions, not findings. ODC's five-page November 3, 2025 determination analyzed the representation under several professional-conduct rules, noted the criminal charges had been dismissed with prejudice, found insufficient evidence on other allegations, dismissed the grievance, and notified Nuno of Commission review. Calling that determination a dismissal *without reasons* would be wrong. [Public dismissal record](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-response-to-bryan-tipp-bar-complaint-answer-octo/mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-odc-ruling-november-2025).

The dispute was not over whether Tipp had been hired to file a civil suit. Nuno's [October 4, 2025 reply](mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-response-to-bryan-tipp-bar-complaint-answer-octo/mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-response-to-bryan-tipp-bar-complaint-answer-october-4th-2025.md) explicitly acknowledged the criminal-defense engagement. He alleged that Tipp discouraged separate civil action, did not convey the specific limitation periods, and declined requested advocacy or meaningful referral while alleged harm continued. ODC's [November determination, pp. 2–4](mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-response-to-bryan-tipp-bar-complaint-answer-octo/mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-odc-ruling-november-2025.md) actually summarized that distinction; it then credited Tipp's account that he warned of potential time limits, encouraged outside counsel, and communicated adequately, while emphasizing the limited engagement and dismissal of the criminal case. The text does not specify which dates or deadlines were conveyed, or explain how the favorable final disposition bears on Nuno's claimed harm during the case. The missing precision is the objection—not an assertion that ODC never addressed civil advice at all.

A [two-page December 23, 2020 letter from Tipp to prosecutor Lowney](../.gitbook/assets/tipp-lowney-letter-december-23-2020.pdf) gives contemporaneous context. Tipp wrote that Lowney had heard complaints about Nuno's speech; Tipp reported comments about Nuno on an organization's website and said they caused the organization and Nuno “great consternation.” Tipp also reported several employment opportunities withdrawn after background checks. In the same letter he pressed for dismissal *with prejudice*, arguing that a dismissal without prejudice and a confidential deferred prosecution agreement would not readily assure third parties that the criminal jeopardy had ended. [Letter, pp. 1–2.] This is evidence that Tipp raised the speech complaints and employment concerns with Lowney and sought a final dismissal. It does not verify the posts' author, falsity, extent of economic loss, or a causal chain to later business collapse. Nor does it contain the agreement's full terms or prove that an attorney had a duty to pursue a criminal referral or a civil case. Nuno says the eventual dismissal did not undo the alleged speech restrictions, continuing harassment, or damage to his livelihood; those propositions require the agreement, the relevant posts, employment records and other evidence for independent assessment. The ODC determination discusses the favorable final disposition but does not separately analyze those asserted interim costs.

Nuno sought Commission review in November 2025. A [later public case account](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026) identifies a January 7, 2026 Commission decision and a February 9 notice letter, but the underlying documents are not in this packet. Nuno's May 2026 [Supplemental Submission #4](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-supplemental-submission-4-may-2026) alleged failures to investigate or use a 2018 witness email and a 2019 medical letter concerning monitoring conditions. The June 27 Alliance email asked whether mailed submissions were associated with the file; it does not establish postal receipt by itself. ODC's [July 10 letter](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/odc-25-147-july-2026-closure-response-analysis) acknowledged material and said it presented nothing new or changed that the November determination had not already addressed. The Alliance's July 20 email requested the passages resolving the two document-specific allegations and confirmation of exhibit review. ODC's [July 30 letter](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/odc-file-no-25-147-july-30-2026-closure-letter-technicality-defense-analysis) called the material additional examples of counsel's “strategy,” stated that Tipp had been able to explain his approach, and left the matter closed. The problem is not that ODC wrote only the word “strategy”: it supplied more text. The problem is that the label does not identify whether Tipp had examined the particular documents, how the asserted omissions were assessed, or what part of his explanation answered them. The letters identify neither the exhibits considered nor the substance of Tipp's explanation, and do not show how ODC moved from those materials to its strategy conclusion. The July 30 letter may answer a forwarded email, but which email it answers remains to be confirmed. The Court's October 22 order later affirmed the Commission's Tipp disposition on procedural grounds, not an exhibit-by-exhibit merits analysis. [Public order images](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/odc-review-evidence-ledger-2026).

In the Lowney matter, Nuno's [April 28, 2026 grievance](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/odc-lowney-grievance-2026/grievance-to-montana-odc-brian-lowney-4.28.2026) alleges conduct over a period from June 2018 to January 2021. ODC's [June 9 dismissal](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/odc-lowney-grievance-2026/odc-26-145-rejection-brian-lowney-june-9-2026) did not decide whether the alleged prosecutor conduct violated an ethics rule. It said the claims were time-barred because “your charges happened in 2019,” more than six years before the grievance, and stated that no facts supporting tolling or an exception were alleged. A valid time bar can dispose of a grievance without merits findings; invoking one is not itself evidence of evasion. Here the document's brevity poses a concrete accountability question: which alleged acts did ODC assign to 2019, and why did the pleaded later period not matter? The notice gave no answer to that question, although it advised of Commission review and gave July 9 as its stated deadline. ODC investigator Sheena Broadwater wrote October 6 that a mid-July review request and the file had been forwarded for the Commission's October setting. The [Commission's October 23 letter](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/odc-review-evidence-ledger-2026) says it considered the complaint, ODC file/report and a July 14 appeal letter, concluded that the facts did not demonstrate clear-and-convincing evidence of ethical violations, and affirmed dismissal. That is an evidentiary conclusion, but the letter does not explain what alleged conduct was evaluated or whether the panel adopted, replaced or bypassed the time bar. The site's review request is dated June 14, not July 14; receipt and timeliness need reconciliation, not an assumed answer.

Broadwater's November 4, 12:23 p.m. email directed Nuno to the October 23 letter's Supreme Court review instructions. At 12:57 Nuno reiterated that he wanted to know what was considered and on what grounds. At 1:33 p.m. he narrowed the request: identify the document and passage stating the facts and reasons for the Lowney dismissal, including treatment of his allegation about leniency conditioned on removing protected speech; if a separate explanation exists, send it, and if not, say so. His assertion about the speech condition is an allegation here, not a judicial finding established by these documents. The 12:23 response preceded both requests and cannot be described as ODC's answer to the 1:33 request. The supplied email exports contain no later response, though they cannot rule one out.

## What the rules require—and what they do not

The Montana Supreme Court's [2021 Rules for Lawyer Disciplinary Enforcement, effective January 1, 2021](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/0dfa798d-443a-4159-b66e-3a404e41e7a3/downloads/RLDE%202021%20(with%20Order).pdf?ver=1791651978503), govern this 2025–26 record absent a case-specific exception. Rule 5B(10) directs Disciplinary Counsel, when dismissing, to “provide a concise written statement of the facts and reasons a matter has been dismissed.” Rule 10C(2), titled “Notice of Disposition,” requires written notice “stating the reasons for the action taken, and advising the grievant of the right to request review of the dismissal.” In the dismissal and follow-up correspondence reviewed here, ODC did not identify *these two explanation provisions* for Nuno or apply their wording to his requests for a factual basis. That is not the same as saying ODC cited no governing rules: the Lowney letter expressly cited Rule 11 for its time bar, and both original dismissals gave review instructions. Nor does the absence of a citation to 5B(10) or 10C(2), by itself, violate a rule requiring reasons. It does explain the focus of Nuno's November 4 request: review directions and a stated threshold ground are not substitutes for showing which facts the threshold covers.

Rule 10C(3) supplies the initial written Review Panel request mechanism; Rule 14 addresses a further, discretionary Supreme Court request following panel affirmance. Rule 10C(1)(b) permits dismissal without every investigative step listed in 10C(1)(a); Rule 20A protects pre-Complaint disciplinary information subject to exceptions. None of these provisions by its terms promises a separate answer to every question, disclosure of the investigative file, or a public hearing.

There is also a rule-reference discrepancy worth checking before any legal conclusion: the June Lowney notice calls Rule 11(D) an exception, but in the court-adopted 2021 text Rule 11D is the rule of repose and Rule 11E lists exceptions. That mismatch alone does not decide whether the time-bar rationale was correct. The strongest reading for ODC is that its November Tipp determination and June Lowney dismissal identified grounds, that its July Tipp correspondence applied the existing strategy analysis to supplemental material, and that the Commission was entitled to affirm Lowney on its record. The text's call for a *concise* explanation supports that reading. The editorial concern is different: a limitations notice tied to “2019” leaves the grievance's stated later period unexplained, and a later evidentiary affirmance leaves the relationship between grounds unstated. The Tipp letters give a category-level explanation without identifying whether the two exhibits were treated as new or considered within that category. This is a question about intelligibility, not a declaration that the rules were violated.

## Current status

Tipp's Commission dismissal has been affirmed by the Supreme Court on the terms of its October 22 order. Lowney's Commission review has ended in affirmance; this packet does not establish a later Supreme Court filing or ruling. The October 23 Lowney letter describes further discretionary review, but its filing instructions and letter-date trigger differ from the court-adopted 2021 Rule 14A's written request *to the Supreme Court* within 30 days of the date notice *was sent*. The actual sending date is unverified. Nothing here calculates a personal deadline, extends one by seeking clarification, or promises an available remedy. The immediately verifiable open question is whether ODC has a later answer to Nuno's November 4 request and whether the full notices clarify how their reasons apply to the allegations.
