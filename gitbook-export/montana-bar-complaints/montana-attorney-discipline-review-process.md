## The stages are not interchangeable

A complaint to the Montana Office of Disciplinary Counsel (ODC) asks an attorney regulator to consider alleged professional misconduct. The Office may dismiss a grievance. Under [Rule 10C(3) of the court-adopted 2021 Montana Rules for Lawyer Disciplinary Enforcement](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/0dfa798d-443a-4159-b66e-3a404e41e7a3/downloads/RLDE%202021%20(with%20Order).pdf?ver=1791651978503), a grievant may ask a Review Panel of the Commission on Practice to review ODC's dismissal. If the panel affirms it, Rule 14(A) says the grievant may, within 30 days of the date notice **was sent**, request review **in writing to the Supreme Court**. Rule 14(B) makes that review discretionary: forwarding a file is not the same as the Court deciding the merits. [2021 rules, pp. 12–16 of the PDF; compare the [Lowney Commission letter](tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026.md), pp. 1–2, as reported in the companion article.] The 2018 version and the ODC website's FAQ use different instructions; neither supersedes the court-adopted 2021 text for a 2026 notice. The Court's [July 2026 amendments](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=577550) are generally effective January 1, 2027, not a basis to replace the 2021 Rule 14 for this notice.

| Step | What a document at that stage can establish | What it cannot establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ODC dismissal | ODC's stated decision and reasons in that file. | A civil judgment, or that every factual allegation is false. |
| Commission affirmance | The review panel upheld the ODC disposition, and any reasons it states. | That the Supreme Court has acted. |
| Commission transmittal to the Court | A request and file were passed along for possible review. | Acceptance of discretionary review, a merits ruling, or reversal. |
| Supreme Court order | The disposition and rationale the order actually states. | Findings about matters the order never decided, including independent civil claims. |

## Why the words in a request matter

The [Tipp order](tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026.md) says Nuno asked the Court on August 13, 2026 for review “upon reconsideration” but characterizes the request's substance as seeking direction about the filing path and clarification of review posture rather than merits review. The Court also found it untimely and affirmed the Commission's dismissal. That is the Court's characterization of that request—not a general holding that every request for reasons can never seek review. A person considering review should distinguish an express request for the remedy the rule provides from a request for explanation, and should consult the operative rule and notice rather than infer a deadline from this example. [Tipp order, p. 1.]

The [Lowney letter](tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026.md) says a written request should state the reasons for appeal, why misconduct is alleged, and the factual basis. It directs submission to the Commission within 30 days **from the date of the letter**. That is what the letter says, but it differs from the operative 2021 Rule 14's trigger (date the notice was **sent**) and addressee (the **Supreme Court**). The date the notice was sent is not verified in the supplied photos, and this article cannot calculate an individualized deadline or resolve the filing-channel discrepancy. A person contemplating review should seek prompt guidance from qualified Montana counsel or the Court's clerk, not rely on this article as filing instructions. [Lowney letter, pp. 1–2; 2021 Rule 14(A), linked above.]

## Discipline and a civil lawsuit answer different questions

A disciplinary proceeding concerns whether professional regulation should act against a lawyer. A malpractice or civil-rights suit would ask a court to decide separate claims under their own substantive law, evidentiary rules, defenses, and limitation periods. The Supreme Court's Tipp disciplinary order and the Commission's Lowney letter should not be represented as civil-liability judgments. Nor should a person assume that sending a grievance, supplemental materials, or a reconsideration request preserves or extends a civil claim. No individualized civil limitation date can responsibly be inferred from these disciplinary documents alone.

## Check the record before claiming a current status

The [September 23 Tipp transmittal](odc-25-147-september-23-2026-supreme-court-transmittal.md) documented that a request had reached the Court; the subsequent October order established what the Court actually did. The October 23 Lowney letter is a Commission decision with an instruction about possible further review, not evidence that the Court received a request. The chronology and limits of both are set out in the [news update](tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026.md).
