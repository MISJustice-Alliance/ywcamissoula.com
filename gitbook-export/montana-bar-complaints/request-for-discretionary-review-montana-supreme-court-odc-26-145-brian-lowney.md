### I. Submission email and review route

The [public sent-email copy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MISJustice-Alliance/ywcamissoula.com/main/gitbook-export/.gitbook/assets/odc-26-145-rule14-request-submission-email-november-19-2026-public-copy-privacy-and-scope-redactions.pdf) shows a message dated November 19, 2026 addressed to Dia Lang at the Commission on Practice. Nuno says he is submitting a Request for Discretionary Review under Rule 14 of the Montana Rules for Lawyer Disciplinary Enforcement ("MRLDE"), with a supporting memorandum and exhibits, and asks the Commission to transmit the request and file to the Court. He asks for confirmation of receipt and any further steps. The record is the sender's email printout; no Commission acknowledgment or Court action is included.

The email lists five attached filenames: a signed request, memorandum, transmittal letter, proposed exhibit index, and an archive labeled Exhibits A–H. Their filenames do not establish their contents or that they were opened by the recipient. The October 23 [Panel notice](../.gitbook/assets/odc-26-145-commission-decision-october-23-2026-page-2.jpeg) directed a written request to the Commission on Practice for forwarding to the Court. The [ODC FAQ](https://montanaodc.org/faqs) likewise describes a request to the Commission, but says **35 days** from the notice-sent date; the court-adopted Rule 14(A) says **30 days**. The discrepancy has not been resolved here.

Rule 14(A) provides that when a Review Panel affirms Disciplinary Counsel's dismissal, the grievant may, within thirty days of the date the notice was sent, request "in writing to the Supreme Court" review of the disposition. Its wording differs from the Panel notice's Commission addressee and letter-date trigger, and from the FAQ's 35-day period. The actual sending date and the legal effect of those differences are not established; this article does not calculate a deadline or decide whether a submission was timely. Rule 14(B) provides that the Supreme Court "may, in its sole discretion, review the Panel's disposition." The described request seeks discretionary review. It is not an appeal as of right, and it does not entitle the grievant to a ruling on the merits.

In the sent email, Nuno requests confidential handling under MRLDE Rule 20, which generally makes disciplinary information and proceedings confidential before a formal Complaint is filed. He asks the Commission to confirm receipt and advise of any further step needed for Court review. The copy contains no reply, assigned Court case number, or Court order. It does not establish that the request was received, forwarded, or acted on by the Court.

### II. Procedural history

The grievance was submitted April 28, 2026. It alleges prosecutorial misconduct by Deputy Missoula County Attorney Brian C. Lowney in the prosecution of State v. Nuno, Cause No. DC-18-587, including the charging decision, the maintenance of the case, the deferred prosecution agreement ("DPA"), and the delay in dismissal after the DPA period ran. The grievance frames the alleged conduct as running from June 2018 through January 2021.

ODC dismissed the grievance by [letter dated June 9, 2026](odc-26-145-rejection-brian-lowney-june-9-2026.md). It invoked limitations under MRLDE Rule 11(A), which by its terms limits filing a formal disciplinary Complaint, not simply lodging a grievance. The letter stated that the "charges happened in 2019," more than six years before the April 2026 grievance, and that no tolling or exception facts were alleged. It did not address the grievance's asserted conduct through January 2021, within six years of April 2026, or reach the merits of any allegation. Whether the later events constitute a continuing violation or separately actionable conduct is disputed, not established by their dates alone.

The [published Commission review request](request-for-commission-on-practice-review-odc-26-145-brian-lowney.md) is dated June 14, 2026; the [October 23 Panel notice](../.gitbook/assets/odc-26-145-commission-decision-october-23-2026-page-1.png) describes an appeal dated July 14. Whether these refer to the same version or separate submissions remains unresolved. The Review Panel affirmed dismissal by notice dated October 23, 2026. The Panel's notice did not mention limitations. It stated a different rationale: that the complaint did not demonstrate clear and convincing evidence of ethical violations. The notice does not address the limitations analysis that was ODC's only stated basis for dismissal. See the [public evidence ledger](odc-review-evidence-ledger-2026.md) for the June/July date discrepancy and source limits.

The [previously published address-redacted exchange](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MISJustice-Alliance/ywcamissoula.com/main/gitbook-export/.gitbook/assets/odc-26-145-november-5-2026-bucy-response-email-address-redacted.pdf) shows the grievant asked ODC on November 4 to identify the written statement of facts and reasons supporting the dismissal, citing MRLDE Rules 5B(10) and 10C(2). On November 5, Chief Disciplinary Counsel declined to identify the particular passage Nuno requested, stating that ODC's prior correspondence supplied its reasoning, and pointed to Supreme Court review. The November 19 sent-email copy to Dia Lang is the later request for Commission transmittal.

### III. Issues presented for review

As the supplied draft frames them, the reported request presents four questions:

1. Whether the dismissal's limitations ruling applied the correct Rule 11 framework — including the discovery rule, the grievance's framing of conduct through January 2021, tolling during pending proceedings under Rule 11(C)(3), and the exception at Rule 11E(2) for alleged misconduct that constitutes a felony — or whether it treated the grievance as a single 2019 event. (The published Commission review request cited the felony exception as Rule 11(D)(2); in the court-adopted Rules the exception appears at Rule 11E(2), and Rule 11(D) is a ten-year rule of repose. The draft says the memorandum corrects that citation, but the memorandum was not supplied for inspection.)

2. Whether the Review Panel could affirm on a clear-and-convincing-evidence ground that ODC never stated, without addressing the limitations ground ODC did state. The draft notes that clear and convincing evidence is the burden of proof for allegations in a formal Complaint under Rule 22(B)–(C), while ODC's preliminary-review and investigation provisions in Rule 10 describe earlier screening of a grievance. The Panel's October 23 notice did use clear-and-convincing language; whether its application was proper is the grievant's legal argument, not a finding here.

3. Whether the June letter's stated time bar and the November 5 response satisfied Rules 5B(10) and 10C(2), given that Bucy referred to ODC's earlier correspondence without identifying the particular passage Nuno requested or explaining how the Panel's stated evidentiary rationale related to ODC's time bar. The June letter did give a limitations reason; the dispute is about its scope and the later explanation, not the absence of any reason.

4. Whether the dated sequences in the grievance (identified in the Memorandum as S-1 through S-4) were evaluated at all: the DPA chronology, the interval between the DPA's expiration and the dismissal, and the dismissal itself.

* **The reported signed dismissal order in DC-18-587 (described as Ex. ### V. What the described request does not establish

The described request asks the Court to review the disposition; it is not an appeal as of right. If the Court declines review, the Review Panel's affirmance stands. If the Court grants review, the scope of further proceedings is for the Court to set. The November 19 sent-email copy shows Nuno addressed a request for transmittal to Dia Lang at the Commission. It does not establish Commission receipt, forwarding, an assigned Court case number, or Court action. The Court docket was not examined here.

The allegations in the grievance remain allegations. ODC's dismissal and the Panel's affirmance were procedural and screening dispositions; neither decided the truth of the underlying claims, and this article does not present them as doing so.
