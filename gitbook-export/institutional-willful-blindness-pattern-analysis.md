# Institutional Willful Blindness: A Pattern Analysis of Complaint-Suppression Across Missoula's Oversight Architecture

> **November 5, 2026 revision of the June 27, 2026 analysis.** The original title is retained as publication history, not a finding of deliberate blindness or suppression. The earlier account incorrectly assigned a scope-of-representation ground to the Lowney grievance, conflated two different attorney matters, and overstated what the available records show about local police review and institutional intent. This proposed replacement corrects those claims rather than leaving a disclaimer atop contradictory prose. The new evidence is Chief Disciplinary Counsel Pamela D. Bucy's November 5 answer to a narrowed Lowney-file clarification request. The original text remains available in the repository's version history; this revision is subject to editorial approval.

> **About this article:** A case-based comparison of how selected police-certification and attorney-discipline complaints by one complainant were received, closed, and reviewed. The underlying misconduct allegations remain allegations, not findings.

## Executive Summary

On August 13, 2025, POST told Nuno that its director had reviewed his complaint information, that most issues were beyond POST's purview, and that allegations older than five years were generally not investigated. It closed the matter on August 27 after Nuno disputed the framing of his allegations as historical. In June 2026 POST disclosed an internal Case Status Committee review route that was not identified in its earlier messages. Its subsequent correspondence reiterated the availability of rules rather than mapping the five-year screen and jurisdictional limit to the later conduct Nuno alleged. That is a documented explanation gap, not proof that POST read only the cover page or that the entire submission was legally within its officer-certification mandate. [POST complaint and chain](mt-doj-public-safety-officer-standards-training-post-complaint-august-2025.md); [May–June correspondence](post-mortem-of-mt-doj-post-complaint-dismissal-august-2025.md).

ODC's two files show different kinds of closure. In Tipp file 25-147, a November 2025 determination addressed competence, scope, strategy, communication, and the defense account; subsequent letters said additional material was already addressed or constituted strategy. The Montana Supreme Court later affirmed the Commission's dismissal on its stated untimeliness and request-framing grounds, not by resolving each underlying allegation. In Lowney file 26-145, ODC's June 9, 2026 notice invoked a six-year limitation and said the charges occurred in 2019; the Commission's October 23 affirmance instead stated that the complaint did not demonstrate clear-and-convincing evidence of ethical violations. Neither letter makes clear how pleaded later conduct or the claimed speech-related condition fits those grounds, or whether the panel adopted or replaced ODC's time bar. [Tipp disposition](montana-bar-complaints/mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-response-to-bryan-tipp-bar-complaint-answer-octo/mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-odc-ruling-november-2025.md); [Lowney dismissal](montana-bar-complaints/odc-26-145-rejection-brian-lowney-june-9-2026.md); [October outcomes and reproduced decisions](montana-bar-complaints/tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026.md).

The common problem demonstrated here is not that every body did nothing. POST and ODC gave grounds; the Commission and Court acted; the Tipp decision includes adverse substantive analysis. It is that a complainant can receive an outcome and review instructions yet still lack a disclosed account connecting the contested allegation to the dispositive ground. A legitimate time, jurisdictional, or evidentiary screen can end a matter; its legitimacy does not make the disputed application self-explanatory. These are related disputes of one complainant, not independent statistical proof of a statewide practice.

## The hypothesis and its limits

The mechanism to test is specific: a complaint identifies conduct; an authorized body supplies a category-level ground for declining or resolving it; the complainant disputes how the category covers particular conduct; later correspondence repeats the earlier ground or points to review without identifying the requested reasoning bridge. Where that occurs, the disclosed record leaves the complainant to infer what determination to challenge. It does **not** follow that the allegation was true, evidence unread, investigation absent, or officials motivated to suppress it. POST's officer-certification authority, ODC's attorney-discipline authority, the Commission's disciplinary review, and the Court's discretionary review cannot be treated as one unified appeal ladder.

The [cross-institution evidence matrix](#cross-institution-evidence-matrix) distinguishes actual dispositions from unverified local examples. The records here do not establish a completed six-stage pipeline ending every allegation without assessment: Tipp received substantive adverse analysis; Lowney reached Commission review; POST identified an internal committee route. No proof of a committee merits decision, independent adjudication of the alleged police conduct, or later Lowney Court proceeding was found in the reviewed corpus. A formal right to complain or seek review exists; what that right permits a person to contest depends on the clarity of the reasons actually given.

## POST: jurisdiction and age, then an internal route

Nuno's August 12, 2025 submission alleged misconduct by identified MPD officers, conflicts, investigative irregularities, and a continuing pattern. He sought an investigation and wider corrective action, some of which exceeded the certification body's remit. On August 13, Katrina Bolger wrote that Director Allred had reviewed the complaint information, most issues were outside POST's purview, and, except in very limited circumstances, POST does not investigate allegations more than five years old under Council policy. The August 27 message said POST would not investigate for the reasons already stated and closed the matter. Thus there were **two stated grounds**, not just a five-year bar. The available letters neither identify which officer-specific acts fell within the body's purview nor explain whether a recent alleged act was outside the screen, outside jurisdiction, or insufficiently supported. A continuing harm from an old act is not automatically new certifiable misconduct; Nuno's continuing-pattern argument required that distinction, not automatic acceptance. [Complaint and original correspondence](mt-doj-public-safety-officer-standards-training-post-complaint-august-2025.md); [archived chain as linked from the source page](https://cr-2025-002-complaint-12_misjusticealliance.arweave.net/).

In May 2026, MISJustice Alliance asked what material was reviewed and how the rules were applied. Bolger said POST's administrative rules and allegation policy were public and that Nuno had been given reasons. On June 2 she identified the Case Status Committee's ability to review the director's determination on a non-anonymous request. On June 3, she again referred to the rules and policies, without an allegation-specific explanation or a referral to a different office to examine complaint handling. That later disclosure is evidence against a claim that no route existed. It also makes the original failure to identify the route a concrete usability issue. The available correspondence does not establish whether Nuno made such a committee request, what deadline or standard would govern it, or that the committee would necessarily uphold the director. [May–June email reproductions](post-mortem-of-mt-doj-post-complaint-dismissal-august-2025.md); [May 27 and June 2–4 correspondence](post-mortem-of-mt-doj-post-complaint-dismissal-august-2025.md).

POST regulates officer standards and certification; it is not a court awarding damages, a general civil-rights investigator, or a prosecutor. The practical referral question is narrower than a demand that POST itself investigate the entire claimed cross-agency pattern: did it identify a usable route for the officer-specific certification concern or direct the other claims to an institution with the relevant mandate? The reviewed letters supply the internal route only months later and no case-specific referral for the remaining claims.

## Attorney discipline: two different dispositions

**Tipp, ODC 25-147.** Nuno alleged failures during criminal representation, including inadequate communication about civil deadlines, a distinct requested criminal-investigation referral letter, and unexamined evidence. ODC's November 3, 2025 dismissal was not a perfunctory scope-only refusal: it discussed rules of competence, diligence, scope, and communication, summarized the defense account of warnings about time limits and outside counsel, and found insufficient grounds for discipline. Tipp's December 23, 2020 letter to prosecutor Lowney documents an effort to secure dismissal of the existing criminal prosecution with prejudice; it does not prove what Tipp said in a separate client email about recommending a criminal-investigation referral. In July 2026, ODC said identified supplemental exhibits supplied no new allegations, then characterized additional examples as strategy. Its public letters do not map those exhibits to the earlier factual findings or disclose the particular explanation to which they refer. This is a criticism of traceability of later responses, not a claim of no reasons in November. The Court's October 2026 order describes an untimely request for review and affirms the Commission; it does not decide civil liability or every disciplinary allegation on its merits. [ODC November determination](montana-bar-complaints/mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-response-to-bryan-tipp-bar-complaint-answer-octo/mt-bar-complaint-odc-no.-25-147-odc-ruling-november-2025.md); [July closure](montana-bar-complaints/odc-25-147-july-2026-closure-response-analysis.md); [October decisions](montana-bar-complaints/tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026.md).

**Lowney, ODC 26-145.** Nuno's April 28, 2026 grievance concerned alleged prosecutorial conduct from 2018 into January 2021. ODC's June 9 notice said “your charges happened in 2019,” invoked Rule 11's six-year limit, and said the grievance alleged no facts supporting tolling or an exception. This is a limitations disposition, not a scope-of-representation finding. Nuno challenged the date characterization and argued later discovery, later conduct, tolling and an exception in the published review request. The Commission's October 23 letter says it considered the complaint, ODC's file/report and a July 14 appeal, and affirmed after stating that the facts did not demonstrate clear-and-convincing evidence of ethical violations. It does not explain whether that evidentiary statement is an independent ground, a description of the record under the time bar, or a decision on each allegation. The public review request is dated June 14 while the Commission refers to a July 14 appeal; the copies have not been reconciled. No later Supreme Court filing or decision in this file was established. [Grievance](montana-bar-complaints/grievance-to-montana-odc-brian-lowney-4.28.2026.md); [ODC notice](montana-bar-complaints/odc-26-145-rejection-brian-lowney-june-9-2026.md); [published review request](montana-bar-complaints/request-for-commission-on-practice-review-odc-26-145-brian-lowney.md); [Commission decision and evidence ledger](montana-bar-complaints/odc-review-evidence-ledger-2026.md).

The [court-adopted 2021 Rules for Lawyer Disciplinary Enforcement](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=368130), with the October 2021 amendment, govern the period discussed; the amendments adopted in July 2026 generally take effect January 1, 2027. Rule 5B(10) calls for a concise written statement of facts and reasons on dismissal; Rule 10C(2) requires written notice of reasons and review rights. Rule 10C(1)(b) permits dismissal without some or all specified investigative steps. Rule 11A frames the six-year limit in terms of filing a formal disciplinary Complaint, rather than a simple prohibition on submitting a grievance; that textual distinction does not itself invalidate ODC's application in this file. Rule 10C(3) and Rule 14 provide review routes. The 2021 Rule 14A's addressee and trigger language differ from the Lowney Commission letter's instructions; the effect on any filing is unknown. Rule 20 limits public access to pre-Complaint disciplinary material, with exceptions; the reasons provisions do not entitle a grievant to all internal notes or require a separate answer to every question. [Court-adopted rules](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=368130); [procedural guide](montana-bar-complaints/montana-attorney-discipline-review-process.md); [decision report](montana-bar-complaints/tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026.md).

## November 2026 update: When the request for reasons meets a refusal of further clarification

The forwarded November 4, 2026 message is headed **1:34 p.m.** Nuno did not demand a complete investigative file, agreement, an advisory opinion, every investigative step, or a separate answer to every legal question. He asked ODC to identify “the document and relevant passage that state the facts and reasons supporting dismissal of ODC File No. 26-145”; send an electronic copy if the explanation was separate; identify existing language addressing the allegation of leniency allegedly conditioned on removing or refraining from protected speech; or explain how the explanation already supplied satisfied the cited rules. He recognized that the request did not extend a review deadline. [Original November 4–5 exchange, public address-redacted derivative](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MISJustice-Alliance/ywcamissoula.com/main/gitbook-export/.gitbook/assets/odc-26-145-november-5-2026-bucy-response-email-address-redacted.pdf).

Chief Disciplinary Counsel Bucy's **November 5, 8:14 a.m.** answer said:

> “Our dismissal correspondence explained our reasoning for dismissal. You will not be receiving any additional information from us regarding our work. Your next available remedy is an appeal to the Supreme Court. You can raise your issues with them as Ms. Broadwater explained. Please refrain from further correspondence with us. Thank you.”

The reply both **asserted that reasons had already been given** and **declined any further information**, directing Nuno to Court review and asking him not to write again. It answered the request; it did not identify the requested passage or supply an additional factual bridge. Its reference to “appeal” is Bucy's term; Rule 14 describes discretionary Supreme Court review after Commission affirmance. Her email is about **Lowney alone**, not a new Tipp determination, and proves nothing about whether ODC conducted internal investigative steps. It does not establish that no other explanatory record exists. [Email exchange](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MISJustice-Alliance/ywcamissoula.com/main/gitbook-export/.gitbook/assets/odc-26-145-november-5-2026-bucy-response-email-address-redacted.pdf); [full ODC comparison](research-reports-legal-advocacy-and-analysis/odc-time-bar-strategy-duty-to-give-reasons.md).

A reviewing person can challenge the stated 2019 timing premise. But if the panel's evidentiary formulation is an additional basis, that person must address it too. The disclosed letters and Bucy's response do not say how those grounds relate or which passage resolves the specific alleged speech-linked condition. The burden is to construct a review submission while inferring the operative reasoning from two differently worded dispositions. That is the documented accountability gap. It is not a judicial holding that the reasons violated Rules 5 or 10, that the allegations were substantiated, or that review was impossible.

## Local oversight and historical federal findings: context is not a disposition

The June article also treated MPD's internal complaint process, the Missoula Police Commission and the historic federal investigation as if each had rejected this complainant's allegations. No Nuno-specific MPD or Commission disposition was verified in the records examined. The [city's complaint policy](https://www.ci.missoula.mt.us/DocumentCenter/View/21236) describes departmental processing and a route to a Commission hearing; the [city's Commission description](https://bm-public-missoula.escribemeetings.com/BoardDetails/BoardInformation/30) says it reviews complaints and can hold hearings for dissatisfied complainants. The original article's categorical claims that commissioners are unpaid and lack subpoena authority are wrong: [Missoula Code chapter 2.30](https://ecode360.com/51617417) provides limited per-session compensation, and [MCA § 7-32-4159](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0070/chapter_0320/part_0410/section_0590/0070-0320-0410-0590.html) authorizes hearing subpoenas. Neither provision by itself establishes what remedy a civilian complainant could secure in this matter. Aggregate complaint figures cannot prove how one particular complaint was investigated or closed.

The [Justice Department's February 2014 findings letter](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/crt/legacy/2014/02/19/missoula_ltr_2-14-14.pdf) examined the County Attorney's Office's response to sexual assault; the [June 2014 MOU](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/crt/legacy/2014/06/10/missoula_settle_6-10-14.pdf) resolved that specific inquiry among its named parties without a County Attorney admission. The broader federal work included a distinct MPD agreement, not a single proceeding adjudicating Nuno's later claims. YWCA of Missoula is **not** a party to that MOU. Those historical records show that external investigation and negotiated reform did occur in Missoula; they do not establish that any later complaint by Nuno was substantiated or foreclosed by that agreement.

## What meaningful accountability would require here

A threshold rule need not be waived to be explained. In a POST closure, identifying which officer-specific acts were outside certification jurisdiction, which were screened for age, and how claimed recent acts were treated would tell a civilian what to put before the Case Status Committee. In an ODC dismissal, identifying the controlling conduct/date and the relationship between the threshold and a panel's later evidentiary ground would let a grievant direct a request for discretionary review. Those are concrete proposed transparency practices, not an assertion that existing law mandates production of every internal document. A local complaint route should identify who has authority to investigate police conduct and what the reviewing commission can actually do; the archive reviewed here does not establish a case-specific MPD/Commission disposition on which to make a finding of local suppression.

The June article's rhetoric of “willful blindness” and “complaint-suppression” described a theory of effects. It cannot stand as a factual conclusion about intent, deliberate avoidance, or a uniform Montana-wide design. The narrower, stronger conclusion is visible in the letters: two different oversight channels closed linked matters with some explanation; subsequent requests exposed unresolved questions about the application of that explanation; and the complainant was directed to review without a case-specific bridge identifying the disputed ground. In Tipp, contrary evidence matters—the initial substantive decision, Tipp's advocacy for dismissal, and a Court disposition on timing. In POST, the disclosed internal review route matters. In Lowney, Commission review and Bucy's actual answer matter. Their existence does not erase the gap; it defines precisely what remains for a reader to assess.
