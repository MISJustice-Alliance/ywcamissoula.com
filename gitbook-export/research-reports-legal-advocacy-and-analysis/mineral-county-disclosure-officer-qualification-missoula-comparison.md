# Mineral County's disclosure disputes and officer-qualification ruling: what can Missoula learn?

**Research cutoff and source access:** October 26, 2026 (UTC). This is a public research guide, not a finding that anyone in the separate Nuno matters committed misconduct. Court orders, agency responses, party filings, reporting and the publisher's analysis have different evidentiary weight. Where we could not inspect a signed order or case docket, we say so.

{% hint style="info" %}
**The short answer:** A Montana Supreme Court order establishes a specific peace-officer qualification defect in three Mineral County DUI investigations and reverses three district-court rulings that had overturned evidence suppression. Separate reporting describes a substantial disclosure dispute, dismissals and a mandamus settlement, but its district-court orders and case-by-case dispositions need direct verification. A distinct Montana DOJ criminal investigation into that disclosure dispute is **not established by the records located**. The clearest Missoula links are reported prior employment and judicial involvement—not a demonstrated connection to the Nuno prosecution.
{% endhint %}

## First, separate the proceedings

In September 2022, defense lawyers Lance Jasper and Jordan Kilby challenged how the **Mineral County Attorney's Office** handled information potentially useful to criminal defendants and how the **Mineral County Sheriff's Office** vetted deputies. These were related but distinct mandamus matters. The *Daily Montanan* reported that at least nine cases had been dismissed over disclosure issues, while Jasper estimated a much larger number might be affected. Nine is a **reported figure**, not an independently audited docket count; Jasper's estimate is not a finding. The same report says Judge Leslie Halligan had criticized late disclosure in one case, while Judge Jason Marks found **no Brady violation in the particular case before him**, even as he warned of sanctions for future failures. The underlying signed Halligan and Marks orders, dismissal entries and complete mandamus dockets were not obtained for this article. Keith Schubert, [*Lawsuit Accuses Mineral County Officials of Misconduct, Asks for Audit of Court Cases*](https://dailymontanan.com/2022/10/09/lawsuit-accuses-mineral-county-officials-of-misconduct-asks-for-audit-of-court-cases/), *Daily Montanan* (Oct. 9, 2022) (reporting on filings and rulings).

Local reporting says Judge Shane Vannatta later approved disclosure-related work under a settlement with attorney Robert Long supervising compliance, and eventually dismissed the **county-attorney** mandamus matter after a dispute about Jasper's additional fees. The reported fee figures are attorney-fee awards, not criminal-defendant damages or overturned convictions. The sheriff-side writ must be followed separately. Signed writs, the settlement, Long's report, fee and dismissal orders have not been checked here. Bruce Moats, [*County Attorney Court Case Settled*](https://vp-mi.com/news/2026/jan/21/rediscovering-home-county-attorney-court-case-settled/), *Valley Press/Mineral Independent* (Jan. 21, 2026).

A later fight involved **Sheriff Ryan Funke's** earlier employment allegations and whether particular material needed to be disclosed if he testified. A December 2025/January 2026 disclosure ruling was reconsidered. Following a May 13, 2026 evidentiary hearing, reporters for the *Daily Inter Lake* and KPAX described Vannatta's oral conclusion that the litigated allegations did **not** qualify as Brady material; much of the evidence was sealed. The signed final written order and any appeal were not obtained. This is not the same as the earlier Kunzelman qualification ruling. It is also not the same as the separate employment/rehiring dispute concerning **Micah Allard**, whose allegations were reportedly found not disclosable in a 2024 ruling. Hannah Shields, [*Court Rules That Misconduct Allegations Against Mineral County Sheriff Are Not Brady Material*](https://dailyinterlake.com/news/2026/may/16/court-rules-that-misconduct-allegations-against-mineral-county-sheriff-are-not-brady-material/), *Daily Inter Lake* (May 16, 2026); Zach Volheim, [*Judge Rules Mineral County Sheriff's Past Conduct Does Not Meet “Brady” Material*](https://www.kpax.com/news/western-montana-news/judge-rules-mineral-county-sheriffs-past-conduct-does-not-meet-brady-material), KPAX (May 2026); Moats, supra. Reporting about an oral ruling is not a substitute for the signed order or sealed record.

## What the Supreme Court actually decided

The accessible primary record is *Patrick v. Montana Fourth Judicial District Court*, No. OP 24-0341 (Mont. Aug. 13, 2024) ([signed order](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=490058)). Three Mineral deputies—Kunzelman, Eric Lindauer and Shawn Visintin—had received mental-health evaluations from a physician assistant before the investigations and arrests at issue. For the investigations at issue, the State did **not** dispute that these evaluations failed the statutory requirement for a licensed physician or qualified mental-health professional. Later compliant evaluations for Kunzelman and Lindauer did not cure their status at the time of the 2022 investigations; whether Visintin ever received one before leaving the office was unclear on the record. The Court made no finding that any deputy was mentally unfit or had never undergone screening. *Id.* at 3–5.

The relevant [Montana peace-officer statute](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/2019/mca/title_0070/chapter_0320/part_0030/section_0030/0070-0320-0030-0030.html), Mont. Code Ann. § 7-32-303(2)(g)(i)–(ii), (5) (2019) (unchanged in the [2023 edition](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/2023/mca/title_0070/chapter_0320/part_0030/section_0030/0070-0320-0030-0030.html) on these points), specified a qualifying evaluation **or** a properly scored standardized instrument and provided that an appointee failing the subsection (2) minimums forfeited the position, authority and arrest powers of a peace officer. The Court held these deputies were not peace officers when they investigated and arrested the three petitioners, and it rejected an additional requirement that the defendants show individualized prejudice from that status defect. *Patrick*, slip op. at 4, 8–9. The Court distinguished an out-of-jurisdiction **qualified** officer in *State v. Updegraff*, 2011 MT 321, 363 Mont. 123, 267 P.3d 28, and refused to use the de facto-officer doctrine to override the statute. *Patrick*, slip op. at 5–9.

The State's contrary arguments about POST/sheriff certifications, reserve deputies, appeal, lack of prejudice and the exclusionary rule appear in the [State's response](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=479757), State's Resp. to Pet. for Writ of Supervisory Control 2–3, 11–19, No. OP 24-0341 (Mont. June 28, 2024). A **litigation brief is not an agency investigation or a judicial holding**. Nor did Montana's limited private-person arrest statute save the investigations: it allows an immediate arrest under stated conditions, followed by notification and transfer of custody, but not the investigation, search or evidence-gathering performed here. *Patrick*, slip op. at 9; Mont. Code Ann. § 46-6-502(1)–(2) ([official text](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0460/chapter_0060/part_0050/section_0020/0460-0060-0050-0020.html)).

**The remedy was precise.** The Court granted supervisory control to Patrick, Ovitt and Green and reversed Vannatta's reversals of justice-court **evidence-suppression** orders in *State v. Patrick*, DC-23-35; *State v. Ovitt*, DC-23-34; and *State v. Green*, DC-23-36. *Patrick*, slip op. at 10. It did **not** itself dismiss their charges, vacate convictions or award damages. Eight other petitioners had justice-court dismissals **without prejudice** and State appeals pending before Judge John Larson in August 2024. Because Larson had not been served with the writ and had made no ruling to supervise, the Court denied those eight petitions without deciding their merits. *Id.* at 1–2, 10. The [Supreme Court docket](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getCaseInformation?caseId=OP%2024-0341) later recorded administrative closure of OP 24-0341, not the final outcome of each criminal prosecution. The State's brief lists different district-court docket numbers for the three cases; this article uses the signed order's numbers pending review of the underlying dockets.

## Was there a Montana DOJ investigation?

**Not established by the records located.** The public-web and official-court-record pass found no DCI opening or closure record, case number, investigative scope, findings or disposition for a distinct investigation of Mineral County prosecutors' handling of these cases. Jasper's reported wish for Attorney General or U.S. Attorney intervention was a request; the Montana Attorney General's OP 24-0341 response was advocacy in litigation. A court-appointed compliance supervisor is not DOJ. POST certification and accounts of **other** discrete officer/personnel proceedings do not establish a DCI case about Kunzelman or an investigation of Mineral prosecutors. [Schubert](https://dailymontanan.com/2022/10/09/lawsuit-accuses-mineral-county-officials-of-misconduct-asks-for-audit-of-court-cases/); [State's response](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=479757); *Patrick*, slip op. at 4–5. This was **not** an agency-file search or a no-records determination; nonpublic inquiries cannot be ruled out.

The Nuno corpus contains **different** agency material. The [Nuno POST complaint](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-state-complaints/mt-doj-public-safety-officer-standards-training-post-complaint-august-2025) states the complainant's allegations; [later POST correspondence](../.gitbook/assets/POST-Final-Correspondance.pdf) concerns that complaint's processing and closure. Those records do not adjudicate every underlying constitutional allegation or establish an investigation into Mineral County. Likewise a [U.S. DOJ Civil Rights Division receipt](https://arweave.net/p8GuVC8SNzjtgFoKw1KDvVJMPgwoDvYbfelQJ1pzGR4) for the Nuno submission reflects intake and prospective review, not verified FBI referral, findings or a Mineral case. The distinction between **Montana DOJ**, **federal DOJ**, **POST**, and a court's supervision matters.

## Which Missoula links are real?

The 2022 *Daily Montanan* article describes a petition exhibit about Kunzelman's earlier **Missoula County Sheriff's Office** employment and reported termination. The underlying personnel record and its route into any prosecutor's file are not inspected here, so the employment and records connection is **reported**, not proof that a Missoula prosecutor concealed anything. The same article identifies Kilby, who formerly worked in the **Missoula County Attorney's Office**, as counsel representing Mineral defendants; her [professional biography](https://www.stephensbrooke.com/jordan-kilby) describes that prior role. These are personnel overlaps with **different** implications, not evidence that her former employer controlled the Mineral lawsuit. Schubert, supra.

Missoula-based judges deciding Mineral matters acted as **courts**, not as the county attorney, county sheriff or **City of Missoula Police Department**. The latter city agency is not shown in the inspected *Patrick* record to have hired these Mineral deputies or conducted the litigation. We found no identified shared actor, transferred Nuno case record, policy, instruction or adjudicated connection joining *Patrick* to the Nuno matter. That is a bounded statement about inspected records, not a claim that all possible archives have been searched. Geographic proximity, a former employer, or the same kind of legal question cannot establish coordinated conduct.

## The comparison worth making—and where it stops

The useful comparison is a **method**: identify the specific information, who held it, when the prosecution and defense learned of it, what proceeding was affected, and what a court actually ordered. On this site, the [P.S. email exhibit hub](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/additional-evidence-and-documentation/ps-email-addendum-august-2018) links a [two-page image-only discovery exhibit](../.gitbook/assets/discovery-ymca-em-from-P.S.-81418.pdf); the hub's account of its recipients and routing was **not independently verified from the image** in this research pass. The [Montana stalking-case analysis](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-legal-cases/2017-2019-misdemeanor-felony-stalking-charges-civil-rights-violations-false-imp) and [prosecutorial-evidence index](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/additional-evidence-and-documentation/missoula-1983-misconduct-mpd-prosecutors-ywca-of-missoula-expanded-history/evidence-related-to-civil-rights-violations-of-mr-nuno-2015-2025/prosecutorial-misconduct-and-brady-issues-evidence-index) organize allegations and possible records; neither is itself a signed judicial finding. A PDF labeled “discovery” may even indicate a defense received it at some stage. Production dates and case dockets are essential before alleging suppression.

At trial, *Brady v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 83, 87–88 (1963) ([opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/373/83)), and *Giglio v. United States*, 405 U.S. 150, 153–55 (1972) ([opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/405/150)), govern material favorable evidence and impeachment withheld from the defense. Montana imposes a related statutory discovery duty: Mont. Code Ann. § 46-15-322(1)(e), (4) (2017) ([official historical text](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/2017/mca/title_0460/chapter_0150/part_0030/section_0220/0460-0150-0030-0220.html)) reaches material tending to negate guilt or reduce punishment upon request and extends to investigation participants. *State v. Severson*, 2024 MT 76, ¶¶ 15–18, 27–31, 416 Mont. 201, 546 P.3d 765 ([opinion text](https://courtlistener.com/opinion/9492202/state-v-k-severson)), distinguishes that statutory duty from the constitutional Brady test and ordered a new trial for cumulative error—not automatic dismissal for one disclosure issue. *City of Bozeman v. Howard*, 2021 MT 230, ¶¶ 12–15 ([opinion text](https://courtlistener.com/opinion/5093568/state-v-j-howard)), illustrates preservation and why an unsupported general request to inspect an officer's personnel file is not enough. The statute cited is the **2017 edition relevant to 2018**, not a retroactive application of today's code.

There are important adverse limits. *United States v. Ruiz*, 536 U.S. 622, 629–33 (2002) ([opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/536/622)), rejected a federal constitutional duty to disclose material **impeachment** information before the plea agreement at issue; it did not decide every claim involving direct innocence evidence or Montana's independent discovery statute. In civil litigation, *Parker v. County of Riverside*, 78 F.4th 1109, 1115–18 (9th Cir. 2023) ([official opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/08/15/22-55614.pdf)), rejected a § 1983 Brady theory without an affected judicial proceeding. [Montana Rule of Professional Conduct 3.8(d)](https://courts.mt.gov/external/rules/mtr_prof_cond.pdf) imposes an ethical disclosure duty; its Preamble ¶¶ 20–21 cautions against treating a rule violation alone as a civil cause of action. None of these rules establishes a violation in Nuno's cases on an index entry alone.

Nor can *Patrick* be transported into an unrelated MPD case simply because both raise fairness concerns: it turns on proven **appointment qualifications** under § 7-32-303(2)(g), (5), not a general finding that any disputed report invalidates every arrest. Suppression of illegally obtained evidence in *Patrick* is **not the same event** as suppression or withholding of favorable information by a prosecutor. Before any analogy becomes a claim, researchers need the signed charging and dismissal papers, case-specific discovery requests and production logs, complete underlying exhibits, officer qualification records where genuinely relevant, and the subsequent docket and appeal history.

## How to check and extend this research

Start with the [signed *Patrick* order](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=490058), the [State's response (a party brief)](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=479757), the [Supreme Court docket](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getCaseInformation?caseId=OP%2024-0341), and the site's [sources and record index](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/overview/sources-and-record-index). The [full Nuno article map](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/overview/nuno-case-system-overview-and-full-article-index) helps locate related but **separate** records. Obtain the two signed mandamus writs and underlying case-level dismissals before publishing a distinct-case total; seek Long's appointment/report, settlement and fee orders, the written Funke/Allard rulings and later docket treatment; and request the original Missoula personnel/transmission and Nuno discovery records through appropriate lawful processes. Access to personnel, criminal-justice and sealed materials may be restricted; preserve necessary privacy redactions.

**Limits as of the cutoff:** The signed Supreme Court order and official historical statutes were inspected. Several district-court outcomes rely on identified reporting rather than their signed orders. The image-only P.S. exhibit, later underlying criminal dockets, any DCI agency file, and comprehensive citator treatment were not independently verified. Midpage was unavailable; no Midpage analysis or certified subsequent-treatment check is represented here. These gaps limit conclusions, not the value of asking targeted, answerable questions.
