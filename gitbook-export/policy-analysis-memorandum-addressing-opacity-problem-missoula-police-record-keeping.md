# Policy Analysis Memorandum: Addressing the 'Opacity Problem' in Missoula's Police Record-Keeping

**To:** Oversight Stakeholders and Policy Analysts  
**From:** Senior Policy Counsel, Constitutional Transparency and Law Enforcement Oversight  
**Date:** May 2026  
**Subject:** Examination of Record Suppression and Structural Barriers to Accountability in the Missoula Police Department (MPD)

---

## 1. Strategic Overview of the Transparency Crisis

The integrity of law enforcement oversight in Missoula is currently compromised by an entrenched record-keeping posture that effectively nullifies the "right to know" — a foundational pillar of democratic governance. Public access to police misconduct records is the primary mechanism through which the citizenry audits the state's exercise of coercive power and ensures constitutional compliance. Currently, the Missoula Police Department (MPD) operates under a systemic "Opacity Problem," defined by the categorical classification of civilian complaint files as confidential personnel records under Administrative Rules of Montana (ARM) 2.21.6612. This classification creates an impenetrable shield, protecting the department from external audit and independent verification of disciplinary outcomes. This memorandum demonstrates that this opacity is not merely an administrative preference but a structural barrier that manifests in profound statistical anomalies.

## 2. Quantitative Analysis: The Statistical Manifestation of Record Suppression

Statistical disparities within law enforcement data serve as empirical indicators of structural failures in reporting and internal accountability. When departmental outcomes deviate radically from established national benchmarks, it suggests a systemic suppression of legitimate grievances.

### MPD Accountability Metrics (2016–2022)

| Metric | MPD Value | National Benchmark (DOJ/BJS) |
|--------|-----------|------------------------------|
| Overall Sustain Rate | 16% | 8% (Conservative Baseline)* |
| Use of Force Sustain Rate | 0% | 8% |
| Discrimination Sustain Rate | 0% | 8% |
| Accountability Section Score | 27 / 100 | 50 (National Median) |

*Note: The 8% figure is a conservative baseline derived from large departments; research indicates that similar-sized departments often achieve significantly higher sustain rates, making the MPD's zero-percent rate in critical categories more concerning.

### Deconstructing the Use-of-Force Disparity

An analysis of MPD annual reports compared to civilian filings reveals a staggering "Use-of-Force Disparity." In the 2021–2022 period alone, the department documented 505 use-of-force (UoF) incidents (211 in 2021; 294 in 2022). However, over the entire seven-year aggregate from 2016 to 2022, only four civilian use-of-force complaints were officially filed. This represents a ratio of 126 force incidents per single complaint filed.

Based on Bureau of Justice Statistics (BJS) metrics (8 complaints per 100 officers annually), a department of MPD's size (approx. 114 officers) should expect a lower-bound baseline of 440–490 UoF complaints over a seven-year period. The actual filing count of 4 indicates a reporting rate 99.1% below the lower-bound expectation. This massive gap does not reflect exceptional conduct but rather identifies insurmountable "structural barriers to complaint filing." This data suppression is finalized by the City's refusal to provide even redacted records to investigative bodies like NBC Montana, asserting that "no parts" of these records are public information.

## 3. The Primacy of Constitutional Mandates over Administrative Exemptions

The current crisis is rooted in the friction between local administrative convenience and the mandates of the Montana Constitution. The City of Missoula's position of blanket suppression relies on the "personnel records exemption" to maintain total secrecy.

As Senior Policy Counsel, I evaluate this claim as legally untenable. The Montana Public Records Act (MCA § 2-6-1003) provides a broad right to examine public information. Crucially, MCA § 2-6-1003(4) explicitly prohibits a public agency from refusing to disclose information solely because it may relate to litigation — a frequent justification used by the City. Furthermore, the Montana Supreme Court has established that the public's "right to know" overrides individual privacy interests when the records concern the conduct of public officials in their official capacities. The City's assertion that "no parts" of a misconduct file are public is a direct challenge to this constitutional hierarchy, effectively attempting to use an administrative rule (ARM) to sunset a constitutional mandate.

## 4. Institutional Architecture: The Mechanics of Accountability Failure

Missoula's "Opacity Problem" is the logical output of a "self-investigative" institutional architecture. Civilian complaints are routed exclusively through the MPD's Office of Professional Standards (OPS). This internal unit functions as the sole gatekeeper, investigating its own colleagues and reporting findings to the Chief of Police without independent intake.

The secondary oversight layer, the Missoula Police Commission, is characterized by a profound accountability asymmetry. Composed of mayoral appointees, the Commission lacks subpoena power and enforcement authority. Commission Chair Dan Doyle has conceded this structural impotence, stating: "The police commission does not tell the police department what to do." Consequently, the Commission acts as little more than a "rubber stamp" for OPS findings, handling what Doyle described as "usually not the serious matters." This framework ensures that civilian oversight remains a formalistic shell rather than a functional check on departmental power.

## 5. Compounding Suppression: State-Level Barriers (Montana POST)

When local mechanisms fail, the Montana Public Safety Officer Standards and Training (POST) Council represents the final state backstop. However, POST's own "Architecture of Early Closure" compounds local opacity.

The case of Elvis Nuno (August 2025) serves as a diagnostic study of this failure. Mr. Nuno submitted a multi-document complaint alleging systemic corruption and civil rights violations. This complex submission was dismissed in approximately 15 hours. Notably, while the dismissal was attributed to Director Timothy Allred, the triage was performed by a paralegal (Katrina Bolger). This "Surface-Level Triage" demonstrates that the state backstop is a procedural formality rather than a substantive review.

POST's efficacy is further degraded by two structural rules:

1. **The Gatekeeper Requirement**: Complainants must first exhaust local remedies with the accused agency. This allows the MPD to filter the very record POST eventually reviews.
2. **The Temporal Rule**: POST's categorical "5-year rule" excludes allegations older than 60 months. This is a structural design failure that immunizes long-term patterns of misconduct, which often only become visible over decades. By the time a pattern is undeniable, the earliest and most critical evidence is suppressed by the calendar, rewarding agencies that successfully block records in the short term.

## 6. Conclusion and Strategic Recommendations for Oversight Recovery

The "Opacity Problem" in Missoula is not an administrative accident; it is a structural design optimized to minimize civilian-origin investigations and shield the department from audit. Internal governance has proven incapable of self-correction. To bridge these data gaps and recover the oversight function, we recommend immediate targeting of the following records:

### Critical FOIA Targets for Oversight Recovery

1. **Per-allegation Disposition Totals (2020–2022)**: Explicit breakdowns of "Sustained," "Exonerated," and "Unfounded" findings to verify if the documented zero-sustain trend for use-of-force and discrimination has persisted into the current reporting cycle.
2. **Police Commission Hearing Records**: Comprehensive agendas, minutes, and written findings transmitted to the Mayor to audit the independence of the Commission's review of OPS findings.
3. **Complaint Lag-Time Data**: Analysis of the duration between filing and final disposition to determine if protracted timelines are used as a tool to suppress or exhaust complainants.
4. **Use-of-Force Demographic Data**: Statistical breakdowns of force actions by subject race and age to evaluate the 9.7x racial disparity in enforcement documented in the Police Scorecard.

The scale of this accountability failure necessitates that the Montana Law and Justice Interim Committee exercise its jurisdiction to initiate a formal legislative audit of the MPD's record-keeping and the City's compliance with the Montana Constitution. Without federal civil rights enforcement or a legislative mandate to penetrate these records, Missoula's police department will remain effectively beyond the reach of the law it is sworn to uphold.
