# Start Here — Partner Summary: Representation Evaluation (Individual & Collective Matters)

**From:** Elvis Nuno, individually and as public coordinator of the MISJustice Alliance

**Re:** Two related matters for representation evaluation — (1) individual legal-malpractice and civil-rights claims; (2) a possible collective civil-rights action concerning alleged recurring conduct in Missoula, Montana

**Research archive:** [ywcaofmissoula.com](../README.md), an independent MISJustice Alliance site with source records and advocacy analysis. This public summary is a request for counsel to evaluate disputed claims, not a pleading or a finding of liability. Some supporting records and other people’s accounts are not public; no attorney-client relationship is created by reading this page.

**Dated:** November 22, 2026. **Source:** Elvis Nuno’s November 22 partner summary, revised for public sourcing and third-party privacy.

## The ask

I, Elvis Nuno, am seeking counsel on two tracks, which may be taken together or separately:

1. **Individual track** — a legal-malpractice action against my former criminal-defense attorney, Bryan C. Tipp, together with my individual civil-rights claims. I report discovering the full extent of the alleged malpractice on July 5, 2025; on that discovery date, the three-year limitations period for the malpractice claim runs to July 5, 2028. Counsel should assess each act or omission, its discovery date, and the ten-year repose limit separately.
2. **Collective track** — a potential collective civil-rights action, structured as a class action only if evidence supports certification, concerning alleged conduct by the City of Missoula / Missoula Police Department, the Missoula County Prosecutor's Office, and the YWCA of Missoula. Counsel should test the proposed First, Fourth, Fifth, and Fourteenth Amendment theories, and the thesis that complaint channels exist in theory but are effectively blocked in practice, against the documented complaint routes and outcomes in specific cases. With diligent investigation, a RICO theory may also be available; I present it as a question for counsel's assessment, not a conclusion.

## Track 2 — Collective: pattern and practice

My case is the best-documented entry point into a wider pattern — but by no means the most severe. Others have reported to me and to the MISJustice Alliance experiences they describe as prosecution, retaliation, and re-victimization involving local institutions. Some report severe housing and economic harm. These accounts require consent, independent intake, records review, and comparison by specific actor and alleged mechanism before I can assert a common practice or classwide injury. I dispute the adequacy of the responses I received in several complaint channels; I cannot speak for every other person or treat an adverse disposition as proof of the underlying claim.

The public record supplies leads for investigation, not a finding of a single unlawful pattern:

- **Complaint data.** The archive's MPD Scorecard (2016–2022, with a methods appendix) reports no sustained findings among four use-of-force and six discrimination complaints in the reviewed 2016–2022 data, and 505 use-of-force incidents in 2021–2022. These are different units and periods, not proof that incidents were suppressed or that a particular complaint was meritorious. The [scorecard](../mpd-civilian-complaint-scorecard-analysis.md) also reports a racial-disparity estimate whose underlying data and methods should be examined.
- **DOJ history.** DOJ investigated several Missoula institutions in 2012. Its [February 14, 2014 findings letter to the Missoula County Attorney](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/crt/legacy/2014/02/19/missoula_ltr_2-14-14.pdf) found substantial evidence of gender discrimination in that office’s response to sexual assault: among 85 adult-woman reports referred by police in the period reviewed, prosecutors charged 14 and declined 42. A separate [June 2014 MOU](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/crt/legacy/2014/06/10/missoula_settle_6-10-14.pdf) required reforms. Those historical findings do not establish wrongdoing in my case or by the proposed collective defendants.
- **Complaint channels.** The archive documents distinct routes and outcomes: police internal-affairs and Police Commission limits; [ODC files 25-147 and 26-145](../montana-bar-complaints/tipp-lowney-disciplinary-decisions-october-2026.md), including adverse dispositions; the [Montana DOJ POST dismissal analysis](../post-mortem-of-mt-doj-post-complaint-dismissal-august-2025.md); and health-department and WSBA records in related Washington matters. A closure must be read on its own terms; it is not a collective civil-rights ruling.
- **Later harm.** I report losing St. Regis school work in 2024 and leaving my business later that year; I relocated from Missoula in 2026. The reasons, dates, and causal links need records-based testing. Counsel should assess whether any later event was independently actionable; later consequences alone do not restart an older claim’s clock.

**The pool.** Some people who contacted the Alliance are willing to be identified publicly; others have asked for privacy. This page does not identify them. I am the Alliance's public coordinator and sole publicly identifiable member (elected to that role because I already experience retaliation); contributors are anonymous. I have gathered accounts for an initial confidential inquiry, but numerosity, commonality, and consent-cleared documentation have not been established publicly. I can discuss an accounting with cleared counsel under appropriate confidentiality terms.
