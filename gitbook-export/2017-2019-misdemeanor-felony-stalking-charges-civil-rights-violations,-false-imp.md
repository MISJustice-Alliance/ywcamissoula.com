# 2017–2019 stalking charges (MT): civil-rights violations

## Introduction

This comprehensive legal analysis examines the systematic civil rights violations, false imprisonment, and prosecutorial misconduct of misdemeanor and felony stalking charges that resulted from Elvis Nuno's formal complaint to the YWCA of Missoula in 2017.

The case ultimately resulted in **dismissal with prejudice** in 2018. This page argues that the dismissal and the underlying record support civil-rights and misconduct theories; it does not treat that result as a judicial finding on civil liability or damages.

## X. Damages evidence status

The amounts previously summarized on this page were advocacy estimates rather than a source-supported damages calculation. A recoverable amount depends on liability, causation, admissibility, mitigation, apportionment, and claim-specific limits. The record should be organized as follows before any total is stated:

| Monitoring, bail, and related out-of-pocket expense | The page alleges 18 months of electronic monitoring; receipts, bond records, and payment records are not cited | Actual payments during the monitoring and prosecution periods | Not yet quantifiable | Partly documented allegation; amount not yet quantifiable |
| Emotional distress, reputational harm, or punitive damages | No verdict, expert valuation, or admissible valuation evidence is cited here | Factfinder assessment after proof of liability, causation, and the governing legal standard | No fixed amount stated | Contingent / disputed |

These categories must be tested for overlap before aggregation. In particular, a lost-career model must not also count the same contract revenue as a separate loss, and fees, costs, non-economic damages, and punitive damages should remain separate.
