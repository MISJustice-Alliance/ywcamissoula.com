## Introduction

**Issue:** The cited correspondence supports Mr. Nuno's allegation that Bryan Tipp failed to preserve or advise about potential civil-rights claims. It documents that: (1) Tipp wrote to Prosecutor Brian Lowney about complaints concerning Mr. Nuno's exercise of free-speech rights; (2) Tipp received communications alleging continuing harassment and business sabotage, including January 2021 correspondence concerning Tyleen Root; and (3) Tipp wrote that he was "generally disinclined" to send the requested institutional-conflict letter. Whether those events establish a duty extending beyond the criminal representation, a breach, loss of a viable underlying claim, or recoverable damages remains disputed and requires the complete engagement and defense files.

**Time Period:** July 2018–January 2021 (representation alleged on this page); October 2020–January 2021 (critical email correspondence and refusals); asserted later discovery in June 2025, subject to actual- and constructive-discovery evidence.

**Legal Theories:** Montana legal malpractice (MCA 27–2–206), breach of fiduciary duty, breach of MRPC Rules 1.1 (competence), 1.3 (diligence), 1.4 (communication); 1.7(a)(2) (conflict of interest); potential 42 U.S.C. § 1983 institutional negligence contributing to ongoing constitutional violations.

**Key deadlines:** No single February or June 2028 deadline is established here. Mont. Code Ann. § 27-2-206 requires omission-by-omission analysis of when the act, error, or omission and resulting injury were discovered or reasonably should have been discovered, together with the statute's outside repose limit and any supported concealment issue. The underlying claims require separate jurisdiction, accrual, tolling, and repose analysis.

### I. GOVERNING LAW: MONTANA LEGAL MALPRACTICE STANDARDS

#### A. Elements of Legal Malpractice (MCA 27–2–206)

**Prima facie case requires:**

1. **Duty** – Attorney–client relationship existed; attorney owed fiduciary duty to client
2. **Breach** – Counsel's conduct fell below standard of care
3. **Causation (But-For)** – But for counsel's breach, client would have achieved better result
4. **Damages** – Quantifiable harm arising from breach

#### B. Standard of Care (MRPC-Aligned)

Montana courts hold attorneys to standard of care of **reasonably competent counsel practicing in the jurisdiction**, measured at time of representation. Key applicable rules:

| Rule               | Obligation                                                                                                      | Breach in Nuno Case                                                                                                     |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **MRPC 1.1**       | Competence: legal knowledge, skill, preparation                                                                 | Failure to investigate Brueckner conflict; no Brady motion; no conflict disqualification analysis                       |
| **MRPC 1.3**       | Diligence: promptly take action on client requests                                                              | October 15, 2020 request met with December 23 refusal; 77-day delay before dismissal                                    |
| **MRPC 1.4**       | Communication: keep client informed of material developments & decisions affecting rights                       | No SOL warnings; no explanation for "generally disinclined" refusal; no exit memo upon termination                      |
| **MRPC 1.7(a)(2)** | Conflict of interest: may not represent client if representation materially limited by lawyer's other interests | Tipps institutional alignment with local prosecutors; preference for case closure over client civil remedy preservation |
| **MRPC 1.16(d)**   | Upon termination: return client files; advise regarding limitations periods                                     | No documented post-dismissal advisory; no transition to civil counsel; silence on imminent SOL deadlines                |

#### C. Causation and Damages Framework

The malpractice theory advanced here requires proof that:

* Client had viable claim capable of successful prosecution
* Counsel's breach prevented timely preservation or filing
* The particular alleged omission was a but-for and proximate cause of losing that claim, rather than another procedural, factual, immunity, merits, or collectibility barrier

**Damages measure asserted:** the recoverable value of the lost underlying claim, supported by admissible evidence rather than an assumed settlement or verdict range, plus any separately recoverable consequential loss without double counting.

### II. TIPP'S ACKNOWLEDGED KNOWLEDGE: THE LOWNEY LETTER (DEC 23, 2020)

#### A. Text of Letter (Relevant Excerpts)

Tipp's own letter to Prosecutor Brian Lowney, dated December 23, 2020, states:

> "You also agreed that Elvis had (at least until then) complied with the conditions of the DPA—notwithstanding that **you had heard or been contacted about E'Lise's complaints regarding Elvis' exercise of his constitutional right of free speech that she found objectionable.**"

He further documents:

> "The comments allege that Elvis is a 'woman beater and stalker.' As such, they have caused the organization, and concomitantly Elvis, great consternation."

**Critical implication:** Tipp explicitly recognized:

1. **First Amendment protection** for Nuno's speech
2. **E'Lise Chard's retaliatory motive** (found speech "objectionable")
3. **Ongoing harassment campaign** (public online comments)
4. **Material economic harm** (organizational consternation, career damage)

#### A-1. Awareness Extended Into January 2021: The Tyleen Root Business-Sabotage Campaign

Tipp's knowledge of ongoing, escalating harassment did not end with the December 23, 2020 letter — it continued into the final days of the representation itself. A documented **January 2021 email correspondence between Nuno and Tipp** exists in the case file addressing the Tyleen Root harassment campaign specifically: E'Lise Chard's recruitment of a YWCA client to run a coordinated defamation and business-sabotage operation against Nuno, including a public Facebook post on the page of his IT nonprofit calling him "a stalker and a woman beater" — precisely the kind of economic and reputational destruction Tipp's own December 23 letter to Lowney had already acknowledged as flowing from E'Lise's retaliation. This places Tipp's knowledge of the harassment campaign at the literal end of the attorney-client relationship, not merely at its outset: he was contemporaneously informed, in writing, that the retaliation he had already characterized as a First Amendment violation to the prosecuting attorney was actively metastasizing into third-party-recruited economic sabotage of his client's livelihood — and he still took no action.

#### A-2. Complete Abandonment: Refusing Both the Referral Letter and Civil Representation

The combination of (1) Tipp's acknowledged, written knowledge that E'Lise Chard's retaliation constituted a First Amendment violation causing "great consternation" and economic harm, (2) his contemporaneous January 2021 knowledge that the harassment had escalated into a coordinated third-party business-sabotage campaign (Tyleen Root), and (3) his flat, unexplained refusal — "I am generally disinclined" — to provide **either** of the two remedies Nuno requested, is not merely a series of discrete rule breaches. Read together, it describes complete abandonment of the client at the exact moment abandonment caused the most harm:

* **He would not write the referral letter.** Nuno asked for a formal letter to the Missoula Police Chief documenting the institutional conflict and requesting referral to the state Criminal Investigation Division — a request Tipp could have granted, declined with a written explanation, or granted through referral to another attorney. He did none of the three; he simply declined, with no explanation and no alternative offered.
* **He would not provide or arrange civil representation.** Despite personally documenting, in writing, a First Amendment violation and quantifiable economic harm exceeding $1 million, and despite being informed weeks later of an active third-party sabotage campaign against the same client, Tipp neither pursued civil remedies himself nor referred Nuno to civil counsel with any urgency, deadline warning, or referral mechanism.
* **He knew the harm was ongoing and worsening, and did nothing on either front.** This was not a single missed deadline or a single unreturned call — it was sustained, informed inaction across both the criminal-adjacent referral Nuno asked for and the civil representation Nuno's mounting losses required, continuing through the literal final weeks of the representation.

A criminal defense attorney is not obligated to personally litigate every parallel civil claim his client might have. But an attorney who (a) has personally documented in writing that his client is the victim of a constitutional violation causing quantifiable economic harm, (b) is then informed that the underlying harassment has escalated into an active third-party sabotage campaign, and (c) responds to both the criminal-referral request and the civil-representation need with the same unexplained, unreferred refusal, has not merely breached a duty of communication or diligence in the technical sense captured by Rules 1.3 and 1.4 below — he has left his client with no functioning legal representation on either front, at the precise moment the client's need for it was most acute and best documented. That is abandonment, not a scope dispute.

#### B. The "Generally Disinclined" Response (Dec 23, 2020 Email)

Despite acknowledging Nuno's First Amendment rights and E'Lise's retaliation, when Nuno explicitly requested on October 15, 2020 that Tipp write a formal letter to the Missoula Police Chief documenting the institutional conflict and seeking referral to State Criminal Investigation Division, Tipp responded:

> "I am **generally disinclined** to send the letter you are requesting."

**No explanation provided.** No alternative remedy suggested. No referral to civil counsel. No SOL warnings.

#### C. Institutional Conflict Context

The December 23, 2020 letter also reveals Tipp's awareness of the broader institutional problem:

* YWCA employed E'Lise Chard (subject of complaint)
* Brueckner served on YWCA board (discoverable public record; Tipp should have known)
* Escalating charges despite Nuno's compliance with DPA and proven free speech defense
* Dismissal with prejudice the only outcome (proving weak case)

**Supporting authority:**

#### B. Alleged breach: evidence and disputes

**1. Failure to Advise of Statute of Limitations Deadlines (MRPC 1.4)**

**Evidence:**

* October 15, 2020 email: Nuno explicitly identifies $1M in civil damages; requests institutional conflict letter
* No written response explaining scope limitations or SOL deadlines
* November 1, 2020: date asserted by Mr. Nuno as a possible deadline; federal accrual, the actionable event, tolling, and the correct borrowed period require claim-specific verification

**Breach standard (Rule 1.4(b)):** Counsel must "promptly inform the client of any decision or circumstance with respect to which the client's informed consent...is required by these rules."

**Application argument:** A verified imminent deadline for a potentially valuable claim could be material to the client. Whether Rule 1.4 or the professional standard of care required Tipp to calculate, communicate, or refer that civil matter depends on engagement scope, the facts known at the time, and expert evidence. The previously stated $1.5M–$3M value is not established by the cited record.

* Temporal sequence obvious (complaint → Brueckner role → escalating charges)
* FOIA requests for Brueckner-YWCA communications

**Asserted breach theory:** Mr. Nuno invokes Rule 1.1 and argues that competent representation required investigation of potentially material exculpatory or conflict evidence. **Application:** Tipp's failure to investigate, challenge, or even mention Brueckner's YWCA conflict in any filing or communication falls below standard of care.

### V. TIPP'S ANTICIPATED DEFENSES & REBUTTALS

#### Defense 1: "Scope of Representation Limited to Criminal Defense; Civil Claims Outside Scope"

**Tipp's argument:**

* Retention agreement (likely Exhibit to ODC response) specifies "criminal defense regarding stalking charges"
* Scope limitation communicated verbally and via email
* Nuno knew other counsel needed for civil matters

**Rebuttal:**

1. **Rule 1.4(b) requires written scope limitation explanation.** Tipp never provided written explanation. Single passing reference to scope limitation insufficient.
2. **Implied scope doctrine:** When civil damages from **same underlying facts** far exceed criminal stakes, scope of representation implicitly includes civil preservation advisory duty. See Montana and national case law on criminal counsel's duty to identify parallel civil claims.
3. **Email evidence contradicts scope limitation argument:**
   * Oct 15 email shows Nuno requesting **Tipp's services** (institutional letter), not requesting permission to hire outside counsel
   * Mr. Nuno argues that, if outside counsel was needed, reasonable advice required an explicit referral and warning before any verified deadline
4. **Tipp's own conduct (Dec 23 letter to Lowney) shows willingness to write advocacy letters.** His refusal to write institutional letter to police chief is **selective refusal**, not scope limitation. Suggests true reason was institutional reluctance, not scope.
5. **Professional standard:** Competent criminal defense in retaliation cases **includes** civil claims preservation advisory duty. Montana Bar Assoc. ethics opinions support this.

***

#### Defense 2: "Client Knew to Consult Outside Counsel; I Directed Him to Do So"

**Tipp's argument:**

* Nuno was sophisticated enough to know he needed civil counsel
* Tipp wasn't responsible for client's independent failure to hire outside counsel

**Rebuttal:**

1. **Tipp never referred.** Oct 15 email shows Nuno was **already consulting** outside counsel (lawyer "I do IT work for"; MT DOJ contact). Tipp didn't refer; client self-referred.
2. **Bare refusal ≠ referral.** Tipp's "I am generally disinclined" is affirmative refusal, not direction to outside counsel. No referral mechanism offered; no civil counsel names provided; no timeline guidance given.
3. **Rule 1.4 burden on counsel, not client.** Counsel must keep client reasonably informed. Layperson cannot be expected to calculate SOL deadlines independently. This is precisely why Rule 1.4 exists.
4. **Client's reliance on counsel was reasonable.** Oct 15 email explicitly requests Tipp's services (letter-writing). Nuno was relying on Tipp; Tipp's refusal is breach.
5. **Limitations-advice theory.** Mr. Nuno argues that Tipp should have warned of any imminent deadline. The cited record contains no such warning, but breach and causation require proof of scope, a correct deadline, an effective filing opportunity, and a viable underlying claim; elapsed time alone does not establish causation.

***

#### Defense 3: "Criminal Case Achieved Excellent Result (Dismissal with Prejudice); Measure of Success Is Criminal Outcome"

**Tipp's argument:**

* All charges dismissed with prejudice — best possible outcome
* Criminal defense competence measured by criminal result, not civil preservation

**Rebuttal:**

1. **Criminal result versus civil-preservation argument.** A favorable criminal disposition does not automatically resolve whether separate civil advice was required. Conversely, speculation that a trial or earlier settlement would have improved a civil case requires proof and must account for the risks of an adverse criminal record.
2. **Rule 1.1 breadth:** Competence includes identifying all claims arising from facts underlying representation. Dismissal is criminal outcome; civil remedy preservation is separate duty.
3. **Conflict investigation duty is independent.** Even if criminal case dismissed anyway, Tipp's failure to investigate Brueckner's YWCA conflict is malpractice standing alone. Brady material suppression is independent violation.
4. **Dismissal may have been Tipp's negotiated choice.** Motion to Dismiss With Prejudice language in Dec 23 letter suggests Tipp was negotiating early dismissal. If so, Tipp should have coordinated civil filing before agreeing to dismissal. Strategic failure, not success.
