## 1. Executive summary

Connie Brueckner is well documented in the public record as a Missoula Police Department detective who worked sexual-assault and related investigations during and after the 2012–2014 Missoula DOJ review. Local reporting and public court filings name her in the Jordan Johnson trial, the 2018 Mark McNeeley investigation, and later Montana appellate briefs involving sexual-offense or related cases. See, for example, NBC Montana’s coverage of Brueckner’s Johnson interview testimony, KPAX’s report on the McNeeley case, and public Montana Supreme Court filings in State v. Smith, State v. Hamernick, State v. Roberts, and State v. Purcell. [NBC Montana](https://www.nbcmontana.com/news/local/jury-sees-video-of-detective-interview-with-johnson), [KPAX](https://www.kpax.com/news/2018/08/16/missoula-man-charged-with-rape), [State v. Smith brief](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=355623), [State v. Hamernick brief](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=422425), [State v. Roberts brief](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=449571), [State v. Purcell brief](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=553616)

**Updated finding:** Connie Brueckner is listed as a member of the YWCA Missoula Board of Directors in the organization’s 2023 GSAFAC audited financial statements (filing 2023-06-GSAFAC-0000027064). The board roster names her alongside Shayla Beaumont, Erika Colstad, Rachel Dierken, and others. The same filing shows schedules for both 2022 and 2023, suggesting the roster reflects service during that period. What remains unverified: the exact start and end dates of her board service, any specific board role (e.g., “board member at large”), and whether she served during the 2012–2014 DOJ investigation period. Other years’ filings in the local archive did not list board members in the extracted text, so the service window cannot be fully established from the files reviewed here. GSAFAC filing 2023-06-GSAFAC-0000027064 (local archive file not bundled in this repo)

The DOJ’s official Missoula findings letter and settlement documents documented systemic problems in how sexual-assault cases were investigated and prosecuted, but my full-text search of those official DOJ documents did not find Brueckner’s name. The official documents focus on systemic failures: more than 350 police sexual-assault reports reviewed, poor communication, weak evidence development, and gender bias concerns. [DOJ findings letter](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/crt/legacy/2014/02/19/missoula_ltr_2-14-14.pdf), [DOJ settlement/MOU](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/crt/legacy/2014/06/10/missoula_settle_6-10-14.pdf)

Bottom line: Brueckner’s status as an MPD detective and investigator is verified; YWCA board membership is verified for at least 2022–2023 via a primary-source government filing; direct DOJ naming is not; claims about earlier board service (including 2012–2014) remain unverified.

**Reliability note:** This report weights official DOJ documents, court filings, and official organizational pages above local news. Advocacy pages and self-published allegations are treated as allegations only.

## 3. Brueckner’s documented roles and affiliations

### 3.1 Missoula Police Department detective

Brueckner is repeatedly identified as a Missoula Police Department detective in local reporting and public court records. In NBC Montana’s trial coverage, she testified about the Johnson interview she conducted about six weeks after the alleged rape. The article quotes her explaining the delay: “It came in predictably late -- cases like this often come in late. It was about six weeks after the incident...” [NBC Montana](https://www.nbcmontana.com/news/local/jury-sees-video-of-detective-interview-with-johnson)

The Montana Kaimin reported that defense counsel asked Brueckner whether she had developed a relationship with the complaining witness beyond a working relationship; Brueckner denied that allegation. That is a defense challenge raised in trial, not a judicial finding. [Montana Kaimin](https://www.montanakaimin.com/news/paoli-questions-detective-s-relationship-with-johnson-accuser/article_60c75c48-7df4-11e2-a266-0019bb30f31a.html)

The 2018 KPAX report quotes court documents stating that Brueckner was assigned to review the McNeeley case, spoke to the complainant’s mother, set up a First Step interview, reviewed text messages, and questioned McNeeley after he acknowledged intercourse but claimed he believed the girl was 16. [KPAX](https://www.kpax.com/news/2018/08/16/missoula-man-charged-with-rape)

## 4. DOJ findings and Missoula scandal context

The DOJ Civil Rights Division’s Missoula findings letter concluded that the county attorney’s office and the local response to sexual-assault complaints showed evidence of gender bias and systemic failure. The letter states that the investigation reviewed “more than 350 reports of sexual assault received by the Missoula Police between January 2008 and May 2012” and that the office “fails to engage in the most basic communication about its cases of sexual assault with other law enforcement and other partners.” [DOJ findings letter](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/crt/legacy/2014/02/19/missoula_ltr_2-14-14.pdf)

The 2013 MOU between the Missoula County Attorney’s Office and MPD required structured referrals, written declination explanations, victim meetings, and improved coordination. It also explicitly names the Missoula YWCA as one of the community advocates with whom the county attorney’s office would coordinate. That shows YWCA was a community partner in the broader reform ecosystem, but it does not prove Brueckner held any board role. [DOJ settlement/MOU](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/crt/legacy/2014/06/10/missoula_settle_6-10-14.pdf)

### Did the DOJ documents name Brueckner?

I searched the official findings letter, the compliance/settlement materials, and the MOU for “Brueckner” and found no occurrence in the accessible text reviewed. On the material reviewed here, DOJ did not single her out by name.

**Reliability note:** These are official federal civil-rights documents. They are the strongest source for what DOJ found and what it did not specifically attribute to Brueckner.

## 5. Named mentions in lawsuits, investigations, and reports

NBC Montana reported that the jury saw video of a detective interview with Johnson in which Brueckner asked about football and about the night of the alleged assault. The story quotes Johnson telling Brueckner, “She never said stop or no. If she had I would have stopped.” [NBC Montana](https://www.nbcmontana.com/news/local/jury-sees-video-of-detective-interview-with-johnson)

The Montana Kaimin’s trial coverage shows the defense pressing Brueckner on an alleged relationship with the accuser. Brueckner denied any relationship outside a working one. Again, that is a contested trial question, not a finding of misconduct. [Montana Kaimin](https://www.montanakaimin.com/news/paoli-questions-detective-s-relationship-with-johnson-accuser/article_60c75c48-7df4-11e2-a266-0019bb30f31a.html)

### 5.2 2021 State v. Smith (Montana Supreme Court)

A public appellate brief in State v. Smith (Doc. 355623) describes Brueckner as the investigating officer. The snippet states that she “watched E.G.’s forensic interview and interviewed E.G.’s friends and family.” That is a court filing, not a judicial holding about her conduct. [State v. Smith brief](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=355623)

### 5.3 2023 State v. Hamernick and State v. Roberts

Public appellate briefs in Hamernick and Roberts again name Brueckner in the investigative role. The Hamernick brief describes “MPD Detective Connie Brueckner” as having interviewed the complainant; the Roberts brief refers to the complainant’s interaction with Detective Connie Brueckner during the investigation. [Hamernick brief](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=422425), [Roberts brief](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=449571)

### 5.4 2025 State v. Purcell postconviction filing

The Purcell postconviction brief includes an appendix transcript of B.B.’s interview with Brueckner and situates her in the investigative record for that Missoula case. [Purcell brief](https://juddocumentservice.mt.gov/getDocByCTrackId?DocId=553616)

**Reliability note:** Court filings are reliable for what was alleged or argued in the proceeding, but not every statement in a brief is a judicial finding. I treat these as documentary evidence of Brueckner’s role in investigations, not as findings of wrongdoing.

## 6. YWCA governance and conflict-of-interest analysis

The conflict-of-interest theory in the starting file is straightforward: if Brueckner simultaneously served as an MPD detective and as a YWCA board member while the police investigated complaints involving YWCA staff or clients, that would raise governance and appearance-of-impropriety concerns.

What is verified:
- YWCA Missoula is a community advocate partner named in the DOJ MOU. [DOJ MOU](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/crt/legacy/2014/06/10/missoula_settle_6-10-14.pdf)
- Brueckner is listed on the YWCA Missoula Board of Directors in the 2023 GSAFAC audited financial statements (filing 2023-06-GSAFAC-0000027064), indicating service during at least the 2022–2023 period. GSAFAC filing 2023-06-GSAFAC-0000027064 (local archive file not bundled in this repo)
- YWCA Missoula’s current public board page does not list Brueckner. [YWCA Missoula staff & board](https://www.ywcamissoula.org/about-us/our-staff-board)

What is not verified:
- Whether Brueckner served on the board during the 2012–2014 DOJ investigation period or during the 2018 investigations discussed in this report.
- Any specific investigative matter in which her board role created an actual conflict, as opposed to an appearance concern.

**Conclusion:** The dual-role predicate is partially verified: Brueckner’s MPD detective status is confirmed, and her YWCA board membership is confirmed for at least 2022–2023. Whether that combination created an actual conflict in any specific case depends on whether her board service overlapped with investigations touching YWCA staff or clients. That overlap is not established in the sources reviewed here.

**Reliability note:** This section is a falsifiable governance analysis. It does not accuse Brueckner of wrongdoing; it tests the factual predicate for a conflict theory.
