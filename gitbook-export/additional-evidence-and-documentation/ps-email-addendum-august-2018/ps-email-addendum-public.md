# Public Addendum: August 14, 2018 P.S. Email and Forwarding Chain

## Overview

This public addendum examines a discovery record showing that a third-party email sent to the general YWCA of Missoula address on August 14, 2018, criticizing employee E'Lise Chard and supporting Elvis Nuno, was forwarded from Chard's YWCA account to Arianna Adams and Kim Harvey approximately nineteen minutes later. The exhibit substantiates the displayed sequence and interval. It does not establish who first opened the shared mailbox, whether any director knew of or reviewed the message, what policy or delegated authority governed the forwarding, or why it was done.

This version is revised for public evidentiary use and anonymizes the third-party sender as **P.S.** to reduce unnecessary disclosure of personal identifying information while preserving the substance of the exhibit. It is intended as a factual and analytical summary for public review, not as legal advice or a final adjudication of any person's liability.

## What the Record Shows

The [discovery exhibit](../../.gitbook/assets/discovery-ymca-em-from-P.S.-81418.pdf) contains several internally consistent layers of the same chain:

- The underlying message is displayed as sent to `ywca@ywcaofmissoula.org` on August 14, 2018, at **9:15:28 p.m. MDT**, with the subject "Attn: Regarding conduct of employee E'Lise Chard."
- A forward from E'Lise Chard's YWCA account is displayed at **9:34 p.m.** to `adamsa@ci.missoula.mt.us` and Kim Harvey at `missoulacounty.us`. Chard wrote: "Here is an email to the YWCA, about me, on Elvis' behalf. Not sure if you want this, since it's purportedly not from Elvis, but I figured I'd send it anyway."
- On August 23, 2018, at **2:35 p.m.**, Harvey forwarded the preserved chain to Arianna Adams. Harvey's signature identifies her as a Crime Victim Advocate with Missoula City-County Relationship Violence Services.
- The two-page copy is marked "ORIGINAL Records Copy" and numbered `2018-37193`.

These repeated layers substantiate the sequence and an interval of approximately nineteen minutes. Because the original timestamp includes seconds and the 9:34 p.m. forward does not, the displayed interval is between 18 minutes 32 seconds and 19 minutes 31 seconds, assuming both displays use the same clock and time zone. The record therefore supports **approximately nineteen minutes**, not an exact 19:00 measurement. The later Harvey forward independently preserves the same embedded timestamps and routing, providing further internal corroboration.

The underlying message from P.S. described Elvis Nuno as kind, respectful, generous, and non-abusive, stated that E'Lise Chard had been causing harassment and trouble to Elvis and his family, and asked the YWCA to address what was "really going on." In substance, the email functioned both as a character reference and as a complaint about employee conduct sent to the organization's general inbox.

## Why This Matters Publicly

The public significance of this exhibit is straightforward: it documents criticism of a YWCA employee being routed from that employee's organizational account to a municipal recipient and a county victim advocate within approximately nineteen minutes. That does not by itself prove institutional wrongdoing. It does raise a legitimate public-interest question about whether complaints concerning staff conduct were screened neutrally, logged appropriately, and reviewed by independent leadership.

This issue carries additional weight when read alongside earlier documented complaints to YWCA leadership alleging misconduct by E'Lise Chard and Rebecca Pettit and requesting internal action. If multiple complaints reached YWCA from different sources, yet the visible record reflects escalation into police and prosecution systems rather than documented internal investigation, that pattern supports public scrutiny of YWCA's governance, grievance handling, and boundaries with law enforcement.

The October 7 record identifies the sender as **MISJustice Alliance, `misjusticealliance@proton.me`**. It lists these recipients: `cweese@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `bmargolis@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `pmurphy@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `jmonis@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `ehatten@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `jmaturen@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `mdenny@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `mschulte@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `lmattis@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `croberts@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `mrichards@ywcaofmissoula.org`, `ahaugo@ywcaofmissoula.org`, and `bcarr@ywcaofmissoula.org`. The message's stated purpose was to obtain an independent board-level review, a verifiable account of the routing and decision-making, and any corrective action warranted by the records.

The October 7 message is another documented good-faith attempt to obtain a verifiable institutional account. It asks focused questions, identifies the records capable of answering them, permits redaction of sensitive information, invites contrary evidence, and expressly states that timing alone does not establish motive. Read with the earlier notices, it shows repeated efforts to obtain substantive answers about operational standards, internal decision-making, conflicts, and correspondence handling. The available project record documents no substantive YWCA answer to the earlier requests. It does not document an affirmative YWCA refusal, and the newest request was not yet due when reviewed.

## Questions the record resolves and leaves open

The exhibit raises several fact questions that remain unresolved on the current public record.

- **Resolved by the exhibit:** the displayed original message was sent to YWCA's general inbox at 9:15:28 p.m. MDT; a Chard-account forward to Adams and Harvey is displayed at 9:34 p.m.; and Harvey re-forwarded the preserved chain to Adams on August 23.
- **Not resolved:** how Chard obtained the message; whether she personally accessed the general mailbox or received the message from another person or routing rule; whether anyone directed or authorized her; whether any Bcc or envelope recipients existed; and the contemporaneous rationale.
- **Not resolved:** whether the Board Chair or any director received, knew of, or reviewed the message before or after the 9:34 p.m. forward. The 2018 message was addressed to the general YWCA inbox and saluted "To whom it may concern," not visibly to the board.
- **Not resolved:** whether the message was logged or reviewed as a personnel complaint, what YWCA policies governed shared-mailbox access and external disclosure, or whether the handling complied with those policies.
- **Not established by the exhibit alone:** that Adams was acting for a prosecutor, that Harvey's victim-advocate role made the initial transmission a prosecutorial referral, or that the message reached a prosecutor. Organizational and case-file records are required to establish that characterization.

The term **intercepted** should remain an attributed allegation, not a factual or legal conclusion. Montana law prohibited purposeful interception of an electronic communication in 2018 and continues to do so, but the discovery copy does not establish the technical or authorization facts required to decide whether an interception occurred. *See* Mont. Code Ann. § 45-8-213(2), (4) (2017); *id.* § 45-8-213(3), (6)(b) (2025). The verified formulation is that the message was received at the general YWCA address and then forwarded from Chard's YWCA account approximately nineteen minutes later.

## Governance framework

Montana's Nonprofit Corporation Act places corporate powers under board authority and the corporation's affairs under board direction. Mont. Code Ann. § 35-2-414(2) (2025). It also provides that an officer's authority and duties derive from the bylaws, board resolutions, or authorized direction. *Id.* § 35-2-440. Directors and officers with discretionary authority are subject to good-faith, prudent-care, and corporate-best-interest standards. *Id.* §§ 35-2-416(1), -441(1).

Those provisions frame the governance inquiry; they do not answer it. The Act does not prescribe how a Montana nonprofit must route unsolicited email addressed to a general inbox, and the authorities reviewed do not require board approval before staff contact law enforcement. Whether Chard had authority to access or forward the message therefore depends on YWCA's own governing instruments, delegations, job duties, mailbox controls, and complaint or threat-assessment policies. Timing alone does not establish a breach of duty or an improper motive.

## Records needed to resolve the uncertainties

1. Native `.eml` or `.msg` copies of the original and each forward, including full RFC headers and envelope-recipient data.
2. Mail-server receipt, routing, access/audit, forwarding-rule, delegation, and mailbox-permission logs for `ywca@ywcaofmissoula.org`.
3. Records identifying who monitored the general inbox in August 2018 and all internal messages created between 9:15:28 and 9:34 p.m.
4. The complete file associated with `2018-37193`, including intake metadata, chain of custody, attachments, and any law-enforcement or prosecutor referral record.
5. YWCA bylaws, board resolutions, committee charters, job descriptions, and policies then governing grievance intake, shared-mailbox access, confidentiality, external disclosure, conflicts, board notification, records retention, and threat reporting.
6. Board-distribution emails, acknowledgments, agendas, minutes, and portal audit logs showing whether and when any director received or reviewed the message.
7. Delivery logs and the complete correspondence thread for the October 7, 2026 inquiry, together with any acknowledgment, substantive response, or express refusal.

These questions are appropriate subjects for public accountability because they concern process integrity, confidentiality boundaries, and whether a nonprofit advocacy organization functioned independently from police and prosecutors in a matter involving one of its own employees.

## Relationship to Broader Pattern

Existing chronology materials describe a broader pattern in which Elvis Nuno's formal complaints to YWCA were followed by no documented internal response, continued employment of the accused staff, and escalating police and prosecutorial activity involving YWCA-linked actors. Those materials also describe structural conflicts, including Detective Connie Brueckner's role on the YWCA board while police activity related to the same dispute intensified.

Within that larger pattern, the P.S. email is important because it supplies a concrete, time-stamped forwarding chain rather than a general theory. The chain substantiates rapid external routing by the employee criticized in the message. It does not establish that the board was bypassed, but it makes the absence of records showing neutral intake, independent review, delegated authority, or board notice a specific and answerable governance issue.

## What This Addendum Does Not Claim

This public addendum does not claim that the exhibit alone proves a complete conspiracy, establishes criminal liability, or resolves all factual disputes about the Missoula matter. It also does not assume, without further records, that every YWCA leader or every prosecutor personally reviewed the email or made a specific decision based on it.

What it does support is a narrower and more defensible conclusion: a complaint and character reference sent to YWCA's general inbox was forwarded approximately nineteen minutes later from the criticized employee's YWCA account to two external recipients, one identified in the exhibit as a crime-victim advocate. The cumulative outreach record makes YWCA's lack of a documented substantive answer to the earlier governance questions significant. It does not, without the requested records, establish interception, prior board non-awareness, policy violation, prosecutorial receipt, improper motive, or an affirmative refusal to respond.
