## Executive narrative

The source documents allege that Danielle Christine Chard, also known as Danielle Christine Bemis, participated in a multi-year pattern of false or materially misleading allegations against Elvis Ryland Nuno in Washington and Montana. The alleged pattern begins with a November 22, 2015 emergency report in Edmonds, Washington, allegedly describing Mr. Nuno as armed and making murder-suicide threats. The Declaration states that officer documentation instead recorded that Mr. Nuno "did not threaten to use a firearm against her," and that no weapon or threat supported the emergency characterization. See Declaration §§ IV, VIII; Prosecutor Cover Letter § II.

The documents then identify December 2015 Seattle Police reports, including GO2015-421345 and GO2015-429912, that allegedly gave materially inconsistent accounts of the relationship history, contact history, Alaska-related facts, and threat narrative. The Declaration states that the first report minimized the relationship and asserted no contact since May 2015, while a later report acknowledged a longer relationship and approximately sixteen years of on-and-off contact. See Declaration §§ V-VIII; Executive Summary, "Materially Contradictory December 2015 Reports."

The documents treat the "Alaska narrative" as the most objectively verifiable alleged falsehood. They state that Ms. Chard allegedly told Seattle Police that both parties lived in Alaska and that she had previously sought an Alaska protective order that was denied. The Declaration states that Mr. Nuno lived in Washington since approximately 2005, never lived in Alaska, and instead filed an Anchorage small-claims action against Ms. Chard and her Alaska partner after she allegedly solicited and accepted financial support. See Declaration §§ VI-VII; Prosecutor Cover Letter § I.

The referral also alleges that an unsupported email-violation report, identified as Seattle Police Report GO2016-148053 and Edmonds Police Report #2016-00009660, led to arrest, $75,000 bail, family payment of $7,500, and nearly two weeks of detention, despite no produced email. See Declaration § VIII; Prosecutor Cover Letter § V.

The Montana phase allegedly involved coordination between Ms. Chard/Bemis and her estranged sister, E'Lise Chard, then associated with YWCA Missoula, to advance mirrored claims through a Montana protection-order petition and related criminal process. The Declaration states that E'Lise later admitted under oath that Mr. Nuno had sent only a small number of emails and no actual threats. It further alleges that Ms. Chard/Bemis monitored custody status, received GPS-monitoring information, and helped transmit a false extradition narrative. See Declaration §§ X-XII; Prosecutor Cover Letter §§ VI-VII.

The Consolidated Referral expands this Montana phase by describing an August 2018 "lost in jail" incident. It alleges that after Mr. Nuno posted bail and was placed on GPS monitoring in Missoula County, E'Lise Chard transmitted custody-status information to Danielle Chard/Bemis in Washington; Danielle Chard/Bemis then allegedly contacted an Edmonds Police Department officer and falsely represented that Washington intended to extradite Mr. Nuno or that an outstanding Washington warrant justified custody. The source document alleges that Montana law enforcement then entered Mr. Nuno's home and arrested him without a valid warrant, governor's extradition demand, prosecutor authorization, or formal interstate process. See Consolidated Referral §§ IV, X-XI.

Finally, the documents allege witness tampering through custody threats in or about 2019. The Declaration states that an Edmonds police officer contacted Mr. Nuno's romantic partner, a widowed single mother, and threatened that she would lose custody and "never see her son again" unless she cooperated and testified consistently with the narrative against Mr. Nuno. The record presently alleges that Ms. Chard/Bemis encouraged, facilitated, or benefited from that tactic, but the direct evidence of her role is not described in the source packet. See Declaration §§ XIII-XIV; Prosecutor Cover Letter § VIII.

#### Extradition-process issue

The updated source packet argues that the alleged August 2018 arrest bypassed normal interstate extradition procedures. Investigators should verify whether any of the following existed at the time of arrest:

* a Washington warrant or charging document authorizing custody;
* a prosecutor-approved extradition request;
* a governor's demand or asylum-state warrant;
* a Montana court order authorizing detention;
* an exigent circumstance justifying entry; or
* any formal interstate communication through ordinary extradition or warrant channels.

Absence of those records would materially strengthen the Fourth Amendment, false-imprisonment, and civil-rights screening issues. Presence of those records would materially narrow or refute this part of the referral.
