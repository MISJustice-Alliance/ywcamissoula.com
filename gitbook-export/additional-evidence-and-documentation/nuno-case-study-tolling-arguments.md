## Executive assessment

The source materials describe serious present barriers: Mr. Nuno reports that an unresolved Washington warrant prevents safe travel; a Montana traffic matter and custody concerns obstruct licensing; an allegedly inaccurate criminal-history description has impaired employment; and continuing contacts or threats have caused persistent safety concerns. Those circumstances are important evidence of present harm. But present harm and timely legal claims are not the same thing.

The strongest legal distinction is between a **new, independently actionable act** and the **continuing consequence of an older act**. A new background report, a new refusal based on a presently applied policy, or a newly documented act of harassment may have its own accrual date. By contrast, continuing career damage, fear, travel restriction, or reputational effects ordinarily do not restart the clock for an old search, arrest, seizure, charging decision, or publication. The Ninth Circuit treats a later event as newly accruing only when it is independently wrongful and establishes a complete claim without relying on the earlier event as the present violation. [Madero Pouncil v. James Tilton, 704 F.3d 568 (9th Cir. 2012)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/madero-pouncil-v-phil-clark-812364?utm_source=mcp&lines=303-309) (treating a later denial as a stand-alone application to a new request). A later adverse effect of a past act does not, by itself, create a new violation. [Madero Pouncil v. James Tilton, 704 F.3d 568 (9th Cir. 2012)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/madero-pouncil-v-phil-clark-812364?utm_source=mcp&lines=274).

The proposed tolling theories are therefore mixed. Montana legal-malpractice claims are governed by the discovery-based framework of Mont. Code Ann. § 27-2-206 — a three-year period measured from discovery of each alleged act, error, or omission, subject to a ten-year repose period measured from that same act. That framework is the strongest timeliness theory in the record, but it must be applied separately to each alleged act or omission, and no tolling theory overcomes the repose period. Disciplinary proceedings, continuing harm, and continued representation are not automatic civil tolling. Some prosecution-based fabricated-evidence claims may accrue at favorable termination. But old search-and-seizure claims generally accrued when the search or seizure occurred, and later discovery of a conflict or additional evidence does not automatically reset federal accrual. Fraudulent-concealment or equitable-tolling arguments require proof of concealment and diligence, not only proof that an important fact was learned late.

## What Section 1983 accrual permits—and does not permit

### Montana supplies the limitations period; federal law supplies accrual

For a 42 U.S.C. § 1983 claim filed in Montana, the Ninth Circuit applies Montana's three-year personal-injury limitations period. Federal law determines when the claim accrues. [Duane Belanus v. Phil Clark, 796 F.3d 1021 (9th Cir. 2015)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/duane-belanus-v-phil-clark-2824561?utm_source=mcp&lines=37-42). The general federal rule is that accrual occurs when the plaintiff knows or has reason to know of the injury forming the basis of the action. [Duane Belanus v. Phil Clark, 796 F.3d 1021 (9th Cir. 2015)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/duane-belanus-v-phil-clark-2824561?utm_source=mcp&lines=43-45).

That rule creates a substantial obstacle for the drafts' broadest theory. A search-and-seizure claim generally accrues when the challenged act occurs—even if the plaintiff did not yet know the search lacked a warrant. [Duane Belanus v. Phil Clark, 796 F.3d 1021 (9th Cir. 2015)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/duane-belanus-v-phil-clark-2824561?utm_source=mcp&lines=79). Thus, discovering an alleged institutional conflict in 2025 may strengthen the merits, credibility, motive, or impeachment record without necessarily changing the 2018 accrual date. Tolling would need a separate, fact-supported basis.

### Prosecution-based claims may follow a different clock

Claim characterization matters. The Supreme Court has held that a § 1983 fabricated-evidence claim challenging a criminal prosecution does not accrue until the prosecution terminates favorably. [McDonough v. Smith, 588 U.S. 109 (U.S. 2019)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/mcdonough-v-smith-9226046?utm_source=mcp&lines=34). The rule reflects the analogy to malicious prosecution and avoids parallel civil and criminal proceedings. [McDonough v. Smith, 588 U.S. 109 (U.S. 2019)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/mcdonough-v-smith-9226046?utm_source=mcp&lines=59-63). That principle may matter for a properly pleaded claim tied to the Montana prosecution, but it does not convert every search, arrest, or reputational injury into a favorable-termination claim.

The precise dismissal dates and terms are therefore essential. “Dismissed with prejudice” should be stated only from certified orders, and counsel must determine whether each termination satisfies the elements of the specific claim asserted.

### Outstanding pleas may create a separate Heck problem

Where a damages claim would necessarily imply that an existing conviction or sentence is invalid, *Heck v. Humphrey* may bar the § 1983 claim unless the conviction has been invalidated. [Ronald Martell v. Brian Cole, 115 F.4th 1233 (9th Cir. 2024)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/ronald-martell-v-brian-cole-10590666?utm_source=mcp&lines=10-10). The inquiry is exact, not categorical: when the conviction rests on a guilty plea, a court examines the record to determine which acts formed the plea's basis. [Ronald Martell v. Brian Cole, 115 F.4th 1233 (9th Cir. 2024)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/ronald-martell-v-brian-cole-10590666?utm_source=mcp&lines=86-87). Any civil theory connected to the 2016 or 2019 pleas therefore requires the judgments, plea agreements, colloquies, and charging documents before counsel can assess timeliness or cognizability.

## Tolling theories applied cautiously

### Discovery rule

Montana generally does not postpone a limitations period merely because a person did not know all facts or did not understand the available legal theory. Where the discovery rule applies, notice that would prompt a reasonable person to investigate starts the clock unless the plaintiff exercises reasonable diligence. [Christian v. Atlantic Richfield Co., 358 P.3d 131 (Mont. 2015)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/christian-v-atlantic-richfield-co-2832772?utm_source=mcp&lines=510-511); [Christian v. Atlantic Richfield Co., 358 P.3d 131 (Mont. 2015)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/christian-v-atlantic-richfield-co-2832772?utm_source=mcp&lines=545-548).

The reported 2025 discovery of the Brueckner/YWCA relationship is therefore relevant but not self-executing. The public availability of nonprofit board filings can cut both ways: it may support the existence and timing of the relationship, but a defendant may argue that reasonable investigation could have found it earlier. The record would need to show what was concealed, who had a duty to disclose it, what Mr. Nuno and counsel knew, what inquiries they made, and why those inquiries did not reasonably reveal the fact.

### Fraudulent concealment and equitable tolling

Montana equitable tolling is narrow. It may apply in rare circumstances where the defendant's conduct concealed the existence of the claim or where the plaintiff, despite diligence, was actually prevented from filing on time. [Duane Belanus v. Phil Clark, 796 F.3d 1021 (9th Cir. 2015)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/duane-belanus-v-phil-clark-2824561?utm_source=mcp&lines=66). Nondisclosure of a board relationship may support further investigation, but the source drafts do not yet establish the affirmative concealment, reliance, prevention, and diligence facts needed to predict tolling.

The drafts also invoke *Brady* disclosure duties as if a possible disclosure violation necessarily proves fraudulent concealment for a later civil limitations dispute. That equation is too broad. Criminal discovery duties, constitutional materiality, civil accrual, and state tolling are separate legal questions. A public report should describe the alleged nondisclosure and preserve the argument, not announce that *Brady* automatically tolled civil claims.

### Continuing violation and fresh accrual

The continuing-violation label is the weakest way to revive completed 2015–2019 acts. The better analysis asks whether a later event is independently actionable. A new act starts a new clock only when it can establish a present violation on its own; later consequences of a time-barred act do not. [Madero Pouncil v. James Tilton, 704 F.3d 568 (9th Cir. 2012)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/madero-pouncil-v-phil-clark-812364?utm_source=mcp&lines=244-246).

Applied here:

* A new consumer report containing materially inaccurate criminal-history information may be a new event, attributable to the entity that prepared, furnished, or used that report.
* A new, documented threat or act of harassment may be a new event if it independently satisfies a recognized claim.
* A current application of an unlawful policy to a new request may be a new event.
* Ongoing fear, lost opportunities, inability to travel, or continued reputational damage may prove damages, but they do not automatically revive claims arising from an old search, arrest, prosecution, plea, report, or disclosure.

This distinction makes event-by-event logging more useful than a single “continuing conspiracy” label.

### Disciplinary proceedings do not preserve the civil limitations period

The civil malpractice deadline must be calculated independently of the disciplinary proceedings. The Montana Office of Disciplinary Counsel describes attorney discipline as a separate regulatory process with its own discovery-based filing rule, and it cannot represent the complainant or intervene in pending litigation. [Montana ODC FAQs](https://montanaodc.org/faqs). Nothing in that structure suspends § 27-2-206, and this page does not contend that filing a grievance tolls the civil statute.

The disciplinary record may still matter — as evidence, not as tolling. Site materials identify an ODC disposition letter dated November 3, 2025 in the original Tipp grievance, followed by a November 18, 2025 request for Commission on Practice review, and a later [July 30, 2026 closure-letter analysis](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/montana-bar-complaints/odc-file-no-25-147-july-30-2026-closure-letter-technicality-defense-analysis). Those events may document discovery, diligence, admissions, inconsistent explanations, or damages. They should not be represented as tolling the civil limitations period unless controlling authority specifically supports that proposition. The separate [Lowney grievance](https://www.ywcaofmissoula.com/odc-lowney-grievance-2026/odc-lowney-grievance-index) was reportedly dismissed on June 9, 2026 under the disciplinary limitations rule, with a Commission review request prepared afterward; that administrative disposition should not be conflated with accrual or tolling of any civil claim.

## Claim-specific observations

### Montana legal malpractice

Montana's attorney-malpractice statute supplies the strongest timeliness framework in the record, and it should be quoted and applied precisely. Under [Mont. Code Ann. § 27-2-206](https://leg.mt.gov/bills/mca/title_0270/chapter_0020/part_0020/section_0060/0270-0020-0020-0060.html), an action against an attorney "based upon the person's alleged professional negligent act or for error or omission in the person's practice must be commenced within 3 years after the plaintiff discovers or through the use of reasonable diligence should have discovered the act, error, or omission, whichever occurs last, but in no case may the action be commenced after 10 years from the date of the act, error, or omission."

Two consequences follow. First, the threshold timeliness argument for the malpractice theory is **statutory delayed discovery under § 27-2-206** — not "equitable tolling" in the abstract. Equitable tolling and fraudulent concealment should be pleaded, if at all, only as alternative theories supported by particularized concealment, reliance, prevention, and diligence facts. Second, the ten-year period is a true statute of repose: it is measured from the date of each particular act, error, or omission, and no discovery rule, continuing harm, continued representation, or pending disciplinary proceeding extends it. Section 27-2-206 does not measure repose from the end of the attorney-client relationship.

The present record supports preserving — but not yet conclusively resolving — a delayed-discovery argument. Mr. Nuno reports receiving the complete client file in 2025 and learning during the same general period of information allegedly bearing on conflicts, undisclosed institutional relationships, omitted civil claims, and prior limitations advice. The operative question is not simply when Mr. Nuno concluded that malpractice occurred. It is when he possessed, or reasonably should have obtained, facts sufficient to identify each alleged professional error.

Continued representation should be treated as evidence relevant to reasonable reliance and discovery, not as automatic tolling. Montana authority concerning reasonable reliance on counsel may affect when a client should have discovered an error, but that inquiry is fact-sensitive and is not equivalent to a categorical rule suspending limitations until representation ends. Montana should not be described as having adopted Washington's continuous-representation rule; Washington recognizes a continuous-representation doctrine limited to continued representation in the same matter, and Montana's analysis runs through reasonable reliance and diligence instead. [Ehrman v. Kaufman, Vidal, Hileman & Ramlow, PC, 246 P.3d 1048 (Mont. 2010)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/ehrman-v-kaufman-vidal-hileman-889202?utm_source=mcp&lines=47-49); cf. [RCW 4.16.080](https://apps.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=4.16.080) (Washington's three-year period and discovery framework).

The source drafts should also not publish a specific filing deadline based only on Mr. Nuno's statement that he discovered the problem in June 2025. Each alleged omission must be dated separately. Counsel must establish duty, breach, causation, actual damages, and — where the claimed loss is an unfiled civil action — the merits and value of that underlying action. [Snyder v. Love, 153 P.3d 571 (Mont. 2006)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/snyder-v-love-887455?utm_source=mcp&lines=38-42).

#### Elements, scope of the engagement, and expert proof

Montana treats legal malpractice as professional negligence. The claim requires four elements:

1. **Duty:** the attorney-client relationship and the actual scope of the engagement, established by the retainer, billing records, pleadings, correspondence, and the actual course of representation.
2. **Breach:** what a reasonably careful Montana attorney would have done under the same circumstances, ordinarily established through qualified expert testimony.
3. **Causation:** proof that the alleged breach — not an earlier independent event — caused the loss, impairment, or reduction in value of an identifiable underlying claim or defense.
4. **Damages:** the result that probably would have been achieved absent the breach, adjusted for collectability, litigation risk, comparative responsibility, and intervening causes.

The scope record must establish whether counsel undertook responsibility for criminal defense only; investigation of police or prosecutorial misconduct; preservation of potential civil claims; advice about § 1983 or Montana tort deadlines; return and organization of the client file; post-dismissal advice; or communications with Washington counsel or courts. A lawyer may limit representation only when the limitation is reasonable and the client gives informed consent. The malpractice theory is considerably stronger if the record shows affirmative advice that civil claims were legally unsupported, premature, retaliatory, or harmful to the criminal defense — that differs from a clearly disclosed decision not to accept a civil matter. The November 2025 review request reportedly relies on counsel's own June 30, 2025 explanation that he regarded potential claims as legally weak or impractical; that communication must be authenticated and read in context before it is characterized as an admission.

The Montana Rules of Professional Conduct do not independently establish civil negligence. They may help define the engagement, identify conflicts, explain communication obligations, or inform an expert's opinion, but the claim should be tied to an expert-defined professional duty, not pleaded as a collection of disciplinary-rule violations. An expert should address, at minimum: whether competent criminal-defense counsel should have identified foreseeable civil limitation issues; whether counsel should have advised the client to seek separate civil counsel; whether counsel possessed information sufficient to investigate the alleged YWCA/law-enforcement overlap; whether the allegedly undisclosed relationship was material; whether advising against civil action was reasonable strategy while the criminal case remained pending; whether any omitted claim was legally viable and timely when counsel allegedly failed to act; and whether the alleged breach probably caused a recoverable loss.

#### Claim-by-claim lost-opportunity analysis (causation)

The phrase "missed § 1983 deadlines" should give way to a claim-by-claim lost-opportunity analysis. The fact that an attorney did not file a federal action does not itself establish malpractice. *Bellamah* bars reducing the theory to one missed filing deadline if earlier advice, investigative failures, evidentiary omissions, or record-development decisions affected the underlying claim; *MacLaurin* requires excluding injuries caused by preexisting events.

For each underlying claim, the record must eventually establish: the proper defendant; the constitutional or statutory right involved; the accrual date under federal law; the borrowed Montana limitations period; applicable immunity; municipal-policy or custom evidence if a municipality is targeted; favorable-termination or *Heck* implications; available tolling; probable success and recoverable damages; and whether a collectible judgment was realistically obtainable. That is the "suit within a suit": the malpractice action succeeds only if the underlying action probably would have succeeded and had measurable value.

### Background reports and employment harm

This is potentially the clearest present-tense issue because each actual report, dispute, reinvestigation, and adverse employment decision can be dated. The first task is factual: obtain the report and identify the consumer reporting agency, information furnisher, employer, source record, and exact disposition described.

The Fair Credit Reporting Act may provide a more direct framework than trying to revive old defamation or civil-rights claims. A consumer pursuing an FCRA claim against a furnisher must first show that the reported information was inaccurate. [Marshall Gross v. Citimortgage, Inc., 33 F.4th 1246 (9th Cir. 2022)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/marshall-gross-v-citimortgage-inc-6341048?utm_source=mcp&lines=92). After the statutory dispute process triggers a furnisher's duties, its investigation must be reasonable and non-cursory. [Marshall Gross v. Citimortgage, Inc., 33 F.4th 1246 (9th Cir. 2022)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/marshall-gross-v-citimortgage-inc-6341048?utm_source=mcp&lines=62-63). This page does not conclude that an FCRA violation occurred; the actual reports and dispute history are indispensable.

## Immediate drafting priorities arising from the relocation decision

1. **Add a distinct relocation-damages subsection to the draft federal civil-rights complaint.** Counsel should evaluate a subsection describing the alleged compelled international relocation and distinguish it from the 2017 interstate relocation already identified in the record. “Constructive exile” may serve as a descriptive heading or advocacy characterization, but it should not be pleaded as an independent damages count unless counsel identifies a recognized cause of action and supporting authority.
2. **Prepare a sworn relocation declaration.** The declaration should document that the decision to relocate is final, explain the circumstances Mr. Nuno contends compelled it, confirm his intent to continue pursuing his claims, and provide counsel with a reliable contact method. Personal addresses, telephone numbers, travel details, and other sensitive contact information should remain in counsel's private file rather than on this public site.
3. **Confirm remote-participation options.** Counsel should determine whether remote appearances, video testimony, and appearances through local counsel are available for the Montana traffic matter, the contemplated malpractice action concerning Bryan Tipp, and any federal civil-rights filing. Residence abroad does not by itself resolve those questions, which depend on citizenship, domicile, parties, claims, and the governing procedural rules.

## Balanced conclusions

The source materials support documenting a present, consequential set of barriers. They do not yet support announcing that broad categories of old civil-rights claims have been revived. The most defensible conclusions are these:

1. **Old searches, arrests, and seizures face serious limitations obstacles.** Under controlling Ninth Circuit law, those claims generally accrued when the acts occurred. Later discovery of additional evidence does not automatically restart accrual.
2. **Prosecution-based claims require exact characterization and disposition records.** A fabricated-evidence claim may accrue at favorable termination, while outstanding pleas may trigger a *Heck* inquiry.
3. **The alleged Brueckner/YWCA relationship is potentially important evidence, but not automatic tolling.** Concealment, materiality, notice, and diligence must be proved.
4. **The malpractice theory's strongest timeliness framework is statutory, not equitable.** Section 27-2-206 supplies a three-year discovery-based period for each alleged act, error, or omission, capped by a ten-year repose period measured from that same act. No safe filing deadline can be published until each omission is separately dated and authenticated.
5. **The 2026 Montana decisions cut both ways and must be argued around, not past.** *Bellamah* supports analyzing pre-filing and pre-appeal negligence omission-by-omission; *MacLaurin* and *Engellant* make causation and the suit-within-a-suit proof the center of the case; *Konesky* makes an engagement-scoped expert opinion effectively indispensable; *Smith v. Green* (nonprecedential, noncitable) signals the defense risk that a court will fix discovery at what the client knew by the end of the underlying proceedings.
6. **Disciplinary proceedings, continued representation, and continuing harm are not automatic civil tolling.** The ODC record may evidence diligence, discovery, or admissions, but the civil clock under § 27-2-206 runs independently.
7. **Recent background reports may present independently timely issues.** The report, vendor, dispute, and adverse-action records should be obtained before selecting an FCRA, state-law, or other theory.
8. **Current consequences remain important.** Even where they do not revive an old claim, they may document damages, explain present barriers, support administrative correction efforts, or form the facts of a separate recent claim.
