# St Regis School and Derek Larson request for comment on access restrictions and the 2024 IT contract

*MISJustice Alliance | Published October 11, 2026 | Response pending*

> **Record status:** This article summarizes Elvis Nuno's firsthand account and the questions presented to Mr. Larson. It is not a judicial or administrative finding. The school has not yet supplied its records or explanation, and the present record does not establish that Mr. Larson originated the reported assault characterization or acted unlawfully.

## The issue presented

MISJustice Alliance has asked Mr. Larson to explain whether St. Regis School restricted Mr. Nuno's access or changed its IT service arrangement because a traffic-related bench-warrant arrest was described as an arrest for assault.

Mr. Nuno states that his truck struck a deer near St. Regis in late 2023 and became disabled. According to his account, he was off the highway waiting for towing and family assistance when law enforcement stopped, questioned him, discovered an outstanding warrant arising from an earlier traffic matter, and arrested him.

He alleges that the incident was later discussed within the school community as though he had been arrested on an assault charge. Mr. Nuno describes the warrant as traffic-related and states that no assault charge arose from that roadside encounter. Primary court and school records have not yet been published on this page to independently establish those points.

That factual distinction is critical. A school may have legitimate reasons to review the background and access of a contractor entrusted with facilities, keys, codes, equipment, and information systems. But an allegation of assault communicates a risk of violence that a traffic-related warrant does not. If the violent-crime description was false, accepting or repeating it without checking the underlying record could produce consequences grossly disproportionate to the actual matter.

## Actions reported by Mr. Nuno

Mr. Nuno states that Mr. Larson requested a background check after learning of the arrest. He further alleges that, during a later school visit, Mr. Larson and an onsite contact referred to information that Mr. Nuno had been arrested for assault.

According to Mr. Nuno:

* his keys and access codes were revoked without advance notice;
* he was no longer permitted to enter independently;
* he could return onsite only when accompanied by a business partner; and
* the school work went to another IT contractor when the arrangement was reviewed in 2024.

Mr. Nuno describes the contract as an important source of revenue and professional credibility. He alleges that the loss damaged his business, standing, and career and that his business partner later used the loss as leverage to force him out.

The request for comment does not allege that Mr. Larson intended or participated in the later business separation. It asks whether he knew of that consequence, whether anyone asked the school to act in connection with Mr. Nuno's business role, and whether inaccurate arrest information influenced any school decision.

## Why verification matters

Schools must be able to respond to credible safety concerns. Their responsibility to protect students, employees, facilities, and computer systems is real. Sound decision-making also favors accurate information, an identifiable policy or contractual basis for restrictions, appropriate notice when required, and a clear distinction between an allegation and an adjudicated fact. Whether any particular legal duty applied here depends on the governing contract, district policy, protected interests, and other facts that have not yet been established.

The present record does not establish:

* who first described the arrest as involving assault;
* whether the description came from law enforcement, a contractor, an employee, or community discussion;
* whether the school reviewed court records or a qualified screening report;
* what any background check showed;
* which policy or contract term governed the access restriction;
* whether Mr. Nuno was allowed to explain or correct the information; or
* whether the 2024 provider change resulted from the arrest, price, performance, procurement requirements, or another reason.

An unverified criminal label can become functionally punitive before any court evaluates it. Restricted access can undermine a service relationship; the damaged relationship can contribute to nonrenewal; nonrenewal can produce substantial economic loss; and each consequence can make the original label appear more credible merely because others acted on it.

## Questions sent to Mr. Larson

The October 11 request asks Mr. Larson to address:

1. **Information received.** When and how did he learn of the arrest, and what did he understand the underlying matter to involve?
2. **The reported assault statement.** Did anyone characterize the arrest as involving assault, violence, drugs, or another matter beyond a traffic-related warrant? What was the source and approximate date?
3. **Verification.** Did the school consult court records, law enforcement, a screening provider, or another source before acting?
4. **The background check.** Was one requested or completed, what prompted it, what information was considered, and which policy or contract provision governed it?
5. **Notice and correction.** Was Mr. Nuno given notice and an opportunity to explain or correct the information?
6. **Access restrictions.** Were his keys, access codes, or authorization revoked or limited? Who decided, why, and was review or reinstatement available?
7. **Communications with the service provider.** What discussions occurred with Max Moss, Centric IS, Mr. Nuno, or other representatives? Did the school request that Mr. Nuno be removed or replaced?
8. **The 2024 contract decision.** Did the change involve expiration, nonrenewal, termination, competitive procurement, or another process? Who approved it and what criteria were used?
9. **Connection to the arrest.** Did information about the arrest or background play any role in the contracting decision?
10. **Records and corrections.** What policies, notices, correspondence, contracts, evaluations, or board records establish the sequence and reasons for the decisions?

## Legal context under Montana and federal law

The authorities below identify potentially relevant principles. They do not establish liability on the present record, resolve governmental immunity, determine whether a privilege applies, or replace review by qualified counsel.

Montana's slander statute requires a false and unprivileged publication and specifically reaches a false statement charging a person with a crime or directly tending to injure the person in an office, profession, trade, or business. [Mont. Code Ann. § 27-1-803 (2025)](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0270/chapter_0010/part_0080/section_0030/0270-0010-0080-0030.html). In *[McConkey v. Flathead Electric Cooperative](https://erd.dli.mt.gov/_docs/labor-standards/McConkey-v-Flathead-Electric-Cooperative.pdf)*, 2005 MT 334, ¶¶ 44–49, 330 Mont. 48, 125 P.3d 1121, the Montana Supreme Court explained that the court must determine whether the challenged language is capable of a defamatory meaning and that mere unpleasantness is insufficient.

Montana has long treated false accusations of criminal conduct and statements directly injuring a person's trade or business as especially serious. *[McCusker v. Roberts](https://hallapproved.com/mt/cases/supreme/1969/1164497/)*, 152 Mont. 513, 523–24, 452 P.2d 408, 414 (1969). *[Blue Ridge Homes, Inc. v. Thein](https://law.justia.com/cases/montana/supreme-court/2008/53626f2e-366c-42e4-97d9-bacf06090837.html)*, 2008 MT 264, ¶ 37, 345 Mont. 125, 191 P.3d 374, reiterates that defamation per se does not require proof of special damages. Those decisions do not dispense with proof that a particular defendant made or was responsible for the false publication.

Context and privilege are equally important. *[Small v. McRae](https://law.justia.com/cases/montana/supreme-court/1982/b6e6ee6f-6730-4145-93d8-b67352a17fb1.html)*, 200 Mont. 497, 524, 651 P.2d 982, 998 (1982), applied Montana's official-duty privilege to an internal communication explaining a public-college personnel decision. *[Sacco v. High Country Independent Press, Inc.](https://law.justia.com/cases/montana/supreme-court/1995/eafab6e0-f333-4c42-9370-110c4caabf74.html)*, 271 Mont. 209, 234–36, 896 P.2d 411, 426–27 (1995), cautions that not every pre-arrest accusation or citizen report automatically becomes an official proceeding or privileged report. Determining privilege requires the actual communication, speaker, recipient, purpose, and governing duty.

Federal law protects speech while also recognizing state remedies for provably false factual assertions. *[Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/1/)*, 497 U.S. 1, 18–21 (1990), rejects a blanket constitutional privilege for statements labeled as opinion when they reasonably imply provably false facts. *[Gertz v. Robert Welch, Inc.](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/418/323/)*, 418 U.S. 323, 347–50 (1974), requires fault before a state may impose liability for defamatory falsehoods injuring a private individual and limits presumed or punitive damages in matters of public concern unless the constitutional standard is met. For public officials and public figures, *[New York Times Co. v. Sullivan](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/376/254/)*, 376 U.S. 254, 279–80 (1964), requires knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for truth.

The claimed contract and career consequences do not themselves prove unlawful interference. Montana requires evidence of intentional and willful conduct calculated to cause business harm, undertaken for an unlawful purpose without right or justifiable cause, and resulting in actual loss. *[Grenfell v. Anderson](https://openjurist.org/2002/mt/225/grenfell-v-anderson-886036)*, 2002 MT 225, ¶¶ 64, 75, 311 Mont. 385, 56 P.3d 326. *Grenfell* rejected the claim where the relevant sublease had already ended and the challenged action rested on a good-faith belief that it was justified. *Id.* ¶¶ 66, 75. Evidence is therefore needed to separate a legitimate school-safety or contracting decision from any alleged intention to damage Mr. Nuno's business role.

## The broader public-interest concern

The public-interest issue is not whether a school may investigate a contractor. It is whether a severe professional restriction was based on an accurate record and a documented process.

If Mr. Nuno's account is corroborated, a traffic-warrant arrest was recast as a suspected assault and allowed to cause consequences associated with violent conduct. The resulting access restrictions, contract loss, business instability, and reputational damage would represent an extreme escalation from the underlying event.

If the school instead acted on verified information, followed a valid policy, afforded appropriate process, and selected another contractor for reasons unrelated to the arrest, its records should clarify that. Publication of the questions is intended to obtain that clarification while preserving the distinction between allegation and proof.
