# Aug 2018: warrantless arrest and false imprisonment

## Supplemental Analysis: Improper Arrest Without Probable Cause

The August 2018 incident presents a textbook case of **false imprisonment**, **Fourth Amendment violations**, and **malicious prosecution**.

## I. Federal Constitutional Violations Under 42 U.S.C. Section 1983

### A. Fourth Amendment Violations

1. **No Valid Warrant Existed**: Bryan Tipp's investigation confirmed no new warrant had been issued
2. **No Prosecutorial Authority**: Multiple prosecutors were unaware of any basis for arrest
3. **Informal Coordination**: Arrest resulted from off-record phone call between Washington and Montana law enforcement

### B. Malicious Prosecution Under Fourth Amendment

* **Lack of Probable Cause**: No evidence supported arrest beyond unsubstantiated claim
* **Favorable Termination**: Arrest resolved in Mr. Nuno's favor
* **Damages**: Deprivation of liberty for several days

## II. Montana State Law Violations

### A. False Imprisonment (MCA Section 27-2-204)

1. **Unlawful Restraint**: Detained without legal authority
2. **Substantial Interference with Liberty**: Multi-day detention
3. **Intentional Conduct**: Officers acted deliberately

## III. The GPS-Travel Scheduling Burden That Made This Incident Possible

The warrantless arrest did not occur in isolation — it occurred against a backdrop of concurrent criminal proceedings pending simultaneously in Washington State courts (Seattle Municipal, Lynnwood Municipal, Edmonds Municipal and Edmonds Superior) while Mr. Nuno remained under a Missoula County GPS-monitoring condition restricting him to Missoula County. That overlap imposed a distinct, ongoing burden layered on top of the underlying prosecutions themselves:

* **An added Missoula County court appearance for every Washington trip.** Because leaving Missoula County required prior court authorization, each Washington procedural hearing Mr. Nuno was required to attend generated a corresponding hearing requirement in Missoula County court simply to obtain permission to travel — meaning a single Washington court date did not cost him one hearing, but two: the Washington hearing itself, and the Missoula County hearing needed to authorize leaving the county to attend it.
* **GPS removal and reattachment logistics.** Each authorized trip required separately scheduling with pretrial services to have the GPS monitor physically removed before departure and reattached upon return, with the exact date and time of departure and return communicated to the court with precision in advance — turning ordinary travel for a legal proceeding into a recurring administrative and compliance exercise layered on top of the litigation itself.
* **Compounding effect.** With cases pending concurrently in two states, this cycle repeated for every Washington procedural hearing during the release period, multiplying the sheer number of hearings, court filings, and compliance touchpoints Mr. Nuno had to manage to remain in good standing with both jurisdictions — a structural burden separate from, and additional to, the substantive difficulty of defending multiple prosecutions at once.
* **This is the operational context in which the August 2018 incident occurred.** The warrantless arrest and days-long detention described above arose during exactly this kind of coordinated, GPS-monitored, cross-jurisdictional travel arrangement — Washington appearance dates coordinated between counsel in both states, with GPS-monitoring status and travel logistics known to, and allegedly monitored by, third parties on the Washington side of the case. The complexity and precision the GPS-travel arrangement demanded is also what made the fabricated-extradition scheme executable: Mr. Nuno's custody and monitoring status was information that had to be tracked and coordinated as a matter of routine, which meant it was also information that could be tracked and misused by others outside the court system.

## IV. Counsel's Silence After Securing Release

Bryan Tipp obtained Mr. Nuno's release by proving to the satisfaction of Montana authorities that no warrant, no extradition request, and no new charges existed — meaning Mr. Tipp necessarily learned, at the moment of release, that his client had just been unlawfully entered upon, arrested without any legal basis, and held for several days on nothing more than an unofficial and unverified back-channel phone call between an out-of-state officer and a Montana officer. Despite possessing this knowledge directly and contemporaneously, the record reflects no indication that Mr. Tipp raised the incident with the court, raised it with prosecutors, pursued any civil rights claim arising from it, or otherwise treated it as anything other than a closed, unremarkable matter once his client was physically freed. An unlawful home entry and multi-day false imprisonment of his own client — established by his own investigation — was not mentioned again.

## V. Legal Malpractice Implications

Bryan Tipp failed to pursue civil rights claims despite having immediate knowledge of constitutional violations, substantial damages, and clear liability. This failure was not an oversight born of ignorance: Mr. Tipp was the very person who established, through his own investigation, that the arrest and detention had no legal basis whatsoever. Securing Mr. Nuno's physical release and then declining to pursue, or even flag for the court or prosecutors, one of the clearest and best-evidenced civil rights violations in the entire case record is itself a distinct instance of the pattern this site documents elsewhere: substantive misconduct against Mr. Nuno that his own retained counsel had direct, contemporaneous knowledge of and chose not to surface.
