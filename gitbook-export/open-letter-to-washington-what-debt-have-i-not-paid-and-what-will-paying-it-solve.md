# Open Letter to Washington

## What Debt Have I Not Paid, and What Will Paying It Solve?

*Elvis Nuno — MISJustice Alliance | September 28, 2026*

{% include ".gitbook/includes/analysis-page-disclaimer-seo-geo-standard.md" %}

***

To Washington courts, court administrators, public defenders, records custodians, elected officials, legal-service providers, and the public:

I am writing to ask for clear written answers to questions that should not require a person to risk detention in order to ask them.

For years, I traveled back and forth in connection with Washington legal matters at substantial financial, emotional, and psychological cost.

I attended proceedings, attempted to comply with demands, sought legal assistance, dealt with the consequences of allegations, and tried to bring matters to a conclusion.

At a certain point, I entered a plea under circumstances that I experienced as coercive. I did not experience that outcome as justice, vindication, or a full examination of the facts. I understood it as a tragic resolution—but a resolution nonetheless. I believed it ended the ordeal.

The available project record includes a [declaration](/overview/sworn-declaration-of-elvis-nuno-detailed-summary) concerning an Edmonds Municipal Court case in which I alleged that, at a March 23, 2016 confirmation hearing, I was presented with a choice between pleading guilty to Assault 4 DV and being taken into Snohomish County Jail. The declaration also alleges inadequate investigation and failure to review related Seattle materials. Those are serious claims that should be evaluated against the actual hearing record, plea materials, court docket, and [case file](/washington-cases/edmonds-case-2015-2017).

Years later, I personally returned to Edmonds court to raise concerns regarding what I contend was an unauthorized disclosure of protected health information connected to a psychological evaluation.

In that effort to seek clarification and relief, I learned that unresolved warrants may exist in connection with my cases, including what I was told was a felony warrant.

I have also been told that fees may be involved.

But I have not received a clear written accounting of what I allegedly owe, why I allegedly owe it, how the alleged obligation arose, or what payment would actually resolve.

That is why I am asking Washington to answer two basic questions.

## First Question: What Debt Have I Not Paid?

I am asking for a clear, written, case-specific accounting of every alleged financial obligation connected to me.

Not a vague reference to "fees."

Not an instruction to "take care of it."

Not a requirement that I appear in person at the risk of arrest before I can learn what the alleged obligation is.

I am asking for answers in plain language:

- What debt or financial obligation is alleged to exist?
- Which court, agency, clerk, collection entity, or other institution claims it is owed?
- What case number is associated with it?
- What order, judgment, sentence, condition, or event created it?
- Is it a fine, court cost, collection balance, surcharge, restitution amount, bail-related amount, missed-appearance consequence, probation-related obligation, or another category?
- What was the original amount?
- What fees, interest, penalties, assessments, or collection costs were added?
- What payments have been credited?
- What is the present balance?
- Is the alleged obligation connected to an active warrant, a hold, a suspension, a collections referral, or another legal restriction?
- What procedure exists to challenge an inaccurate balance, request a hearing, seek remission, request a payment plan, or demonstrate inability to pay?

Most importantly:

> **What will paying the alleged debt actually solve?**

Will payment recall a warrant?

Will it close a case?

Will it satisfy a judgment?

Will it remove an arrest risk?

Will it terminate a hold?

Will it restore a legal status?

Or will it merely pay one administrative balance while leaving the underlying legal problem unresolved?

These are not complicated questions. They are the minimum information necessary for a person to understand how to comply with the law.

No court should be able to impose a financial obligation, maintain a warrant, or restrict liberty while refusing to provide a written explanation of the obligation and the legal consequence of payment.

## Second Question: How Can I Seek Review Safely?

The problem is not merely that I need information.

The problem is that I have reason to believe appearing in person to seek that information could result in my immediate detention.

A person should not have to choose between ignorance and incarceration.

If someone has unresolved warrants, disputed court records, or an unclear financial obligation, the system should provide a safe and defined procedure for obtaining review before requiring an in-person appearance that may lead to custody.

Washington courts should provide, at minimum:

- Written confirmation of every active warrant.
- The issuing court, case number, date, warrant type, stated basis, and bond or bail condition.
- A clear procedure to request warrant recall, quash, clarification, modification, or hearing.
- A scheduled warrant-review process.
- Remote hearing options where legally available.
- Counsel-assisted appearance procedures.
- A process to request a temporary stay or safe-conduct arrangement while obtaining records and preparing a motion.
- Clear instructions for out-of-state persons who need to understand their legal status before traveling.
- A procedure to present documented caregiving obligations before custody or forced travel creates preventable harm.

Due process should not mean:

> "Appear first. Risk custody. Ask questions later."

A process that works only for people who can safely walk into court without fear of detention is not equally accessible.

## The Missing Evaluation

The Washington matter also involves a psychological evaluation and what I contend was an unauthorized disclosure of protected health information.

When I sought relief regarding that concern, my understanding was that the judge could not locate the evaluation in the available case file.

I have also sought assistance through the Edmonds public-defender office. To date, I understand that those efforts have not located the evaluation or provided a clear written explanation of its status.

I do not know whether the evaluation:

- Was never filed.
- Was filed under another case number.
- Was placed in a sealed or confidential portion of a record.
- Was kept by a provider, agency, attorney, law-enforcement office, or another custodian.
- Was transferred, misindexed, or stored outside the ordinary court file.
- Was destroyed or disposed of under a retention policy.
- Was otherwise removed from the accessible record.

I am not asserting, without evidence, that a specific person intentionally removed, concealed, altered, or destroyed the document.

I am asking for a documented answer.

If an evaluation was transmitted, disclosed, summarized, used, or relied upon in a legal matter affecting a person's liberty, reputation, mental-health record, legal status, or ability to defend themselves, then the relevant institutions should be able to account for it.

I request a written, case-specific answer to these questions:

- Was the evaluation received by the court?
- On what date?
- Who submitted it?
- What case number, docket entry, exhibit number, or sealed-file designation applies?
- Was it filed publicly, confidentially, or under seal?
- Who had possession, custody, or access to it?
- Was it disclosed to law enforcement, prosecution, defense counsel, probation, a provider, or another court?
- What retention schedule governs it?
- Does an exhibit log, docket history, clerk log, e-filing history, case-management audit log, correspondence record, or custody record identify its location or disposition?
- What repositories have been searched?
- Who conducted the search, on what date, and with what result?
- Can a certified replacement be obtained from the original evaluator or another lawful custodian?

"We cannot find it" is not an adequate final answer when the missing document may have affected a person's liberty or ability to defend themselves.

## The Cost of Uncertainty

The uncertainty surrounding unresolved Washington matters has continued long after I believed those matters were behind me.

The costs are not merely legal.

They include the inability to plan, the fear of detention, the expense of travel, the need to repeatedly locate old records, the difficulty of obtaining clear answers, the risk to professional stability, and the effect of unresolved legal status on family responsibilities.

The risk is especially serious because I have vital caregiving responsibilities for my elderly father.

If I am required to travel without notice, appear in person without a safe procedure, or submit to uncertain custody, I may be forced to leave him without necessary support.

That is not a scheduling inconvenience.

It may create real danger.

I also have household responsibilities, including care for my cat. But the central issue is that no court process should require a person to gamble with the safety of a dependent elderly parent merely to learn what debt is allegedly owed, what a warrant means, or whether an old matter has actually been resolved.

Washington courts should have clear procedures for people with documented caregiving responsibilities to request remote access, scheduled review, reasonable accommodation, time to arrange substitute care, and lawful alternatives to surprise detention.

## What I Am Asking Washington To Do

I am not asking for special treatment.

I am asking Washington institutions to provide the minimum clarity and procedural access necessary for a person to understand and address matters that may affect their liberty.

I request:

1. **A written debt accounting.**
   Identify each alleged obligation, its case number, source of authority, original amount, added charges, payment history, present balance, and the exact consequence of payment.

2. **Written warrant transparency.**
   Identify every active warrant, issuing court, case number, warrant type, stated basis, bond or bail condition, and procedure for recall, quash, hearing, or review.

3. **A safe process for court access.**
   Provide remote, scheduled, counsel-assisted, or otherwise safe access to records and hearings before requiring an appearance that may lead to detention.

4. **A full documented search for the psychological evaluation.**
   Identify whether it was received, filed, sealed, retained, transferred, disclosed, destroyed, or held by another custodian, and disclose the documented results of the search.

5. **A records-custody explanation.**
   Identify the chain of custody, access history, filing status, and retention policy for the evaluation and any related protected-health-information records.

6. **A caregiving accommodation procedure.**
   Provide a way to present documented dependence and caregiving responsibilities before travel, detention, or in-person appearance creates preventable harm at home.

7. **A clear path to finality.**
   Identify what actions, if any, are necessary to resolve every remaining Washington matter fully and permanently.

## Closing

I am asking Washington to answer questions that should have simple, written answers.

> **What debt have I not paid?**

Identify it. Explain it. Show the case number, order, amount, legal basis, warrant connection, and what payment would resolve.

> **How can I safely seek review?**

Provide a procedure that allows a person to obtain records, challenge inaccuracies, understand warrant status, and be heard before risking detention.

> **Where is the evaluation?**

Locate it, produce it, or provide a documented accounting of its custody, use, filing status, retention, and disposition.

A court system cannot call itself accessible if a person must risk incarceration merely to learn why a warrant exists or what a court claims they owe.

Due process must mean more than "appear, surrender, and hope someone explains later."
