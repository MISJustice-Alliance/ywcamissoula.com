## Overview

The declaration is a 35-page, notarized account of what Mr. Nuno describes as a multi-year, multi-jurisdictional pattern of false accusations, perjury, criminal defamation, retaliatory prosecution, and institutional corruption involving E'Lise Chard and Danielle Chard, with alleged assistance from YWCA Missoula employees, Missoula Police Department officers (Officer Ethan Smith, Detective Connie Brueckner), and law enforcement and prosecutorial actors in Washington State and Montana between 2015 and 2025. It requests that state and federal authorities open or expand criminal investigations into perjury, criminal defamation, witness intimidation, suppression of exculpatory evidence, conflicts of interest, and conspiracy to violate civil rights (18 U.S.C. § 241).

Every criminal charge against Mr. Nuno in both states was ultimately dismissed — in Montana and Washington, with prejudice.

## I. Declarant identification and background

- **Name:** Elvis Ryland Nuno; resident of Montana; previously resided in Redmond, Washington (Dec 2006–Dec 2019) and Missoula, Montana (Jan 2017–Aug 2020).
- **Career:** Senior telecommunications engineer with 20+ years in telecom systems, ISP network architecture, and IT consulting; earned $150,000–$250,000 annually in contracts with major telecom and technology firms before the events described.
- **Criminal history — complete disclosure (as stated by declarant):**
  - **2007:** One minor misdemeanor "disturbing the peace" in his early twenties, unrelated to the Chard allegations.
  - **March 2016:** DV Assault 3rd Degree plea in Edmonds Municipal Court — asserted to be **coerced** via prosecutorial threats, ineffective assistance of counsel (attorney Patricia Fulton), the psychological impact of a Nov 22, 2015 SWAT-style raid, and financial pressure.
  - **September 2019:** Guilty pleas to no-contact order violations (Seattle, Lynnwood, Edmonds Municipal Courts) and misdemeanor stalking (Snohomish County Superior Court) — asserted to be **coerced** through law enforcement threats against his then-partner that she would "lose custody of her child and never see her son again" unless she cooperated.
  - **Dismissed with prejudice (complete vindication):** Seattle 2015–2016 harassment/cyberstalking (dismissed Aug 2016, no evidence found); Montana 2018–2019 felony and misdemeanor stalking (dismissed after the State repeatedly failed to produce evidence; court recognized his communications as protected First Amendment speech).
  - **Plea withdrawal attempts:** Sept 2016 motion to withdraw the 2016 Edmonds plea (denied); April 2017 appeal to Snohomish County Superior Court (denied on the post-sentencing withdrawal standard).

- Known of E'Lise Chard since ~2002 through isolated social interactions while dating her sister Danielle; first in-person meeting included her stating: *"I hope you know I'm obligated to hate anyone my sister dates."*
- Over 16+ years, only a handful of brief in-person interactions — none involving threats, violence, or harassment by Mr. Nuno. Communications were sporadic and primarily digital; no shared residence, no sustained relationship.

## III. The June 2018 YWCA complaint (protected First Amendment speech)

- **June 3, 2018:** Filed a formal written complaint with YWCA Missoula (President Deirdre Flaherty, Executive Director Cindy Weese) regarding YWCA employees E'Lise Chard and Rebecca Pettit, alleging: a pattern of false police reports (2016 WA through 2018 MT); vindictive intent stemming from termination of Danielle Chard's relationship after she became "frighteningly abusive and violent"; June 2017 contact with MPD Officer Ethan Smith under the guise of official YWCA business; knowingly false statements and fabricated evidence; mischaracterization of his IT career as a "cyber crime spree"; and abuse of YWCA positions and resources.
- Complaint detailed the consequences, including Officer Smith's ~year-long pattern of off-the-record harassment of Mr. Nuno and his parents, contributing to his mother's emergency psychiatric intervention. Enclosed a **$400 donation** in good faith.
- **YWCA never responded** — no investigation, no contact, no action. Instead, the letter and donation were treated as suspected evidence of criminal actions and provided to E'Lise Chard, who filed a retaliatory protection order petition **nine days later (June 12, 2018)**.
- Montana courts subsequently found the complaint constituted **protected First Amendment speech** (Judge Sam Warren, presiding judge at the protection order hearing).

## III-A. The 2025 MISJustice Alliance formal notices to YWCA

- **October 12, 2025:** MISJustice Alliance (on behalf of Mr. Nuno and other victims) sent a formal legal notice requesting response on systemic issues, possible criminal conduct, and conflicts of interest — including Detective Connie Brueckner's simultaneous role as law enforcement officer and YWCA board member; retaliation patterns; misuse of confidential client information (including documented HIPAA violations); funding practices prioritizing crisis metrics; and the Board's conflict-management and complaint-handling protocols.
- **December 5, 2025:** A comprehensive second notice to YWCA leadership, copied to senior YWCA staff and key Missoula city/county officials (Mayor, Police Chief, County Attorney), documenting: how E'Lise Chard imported and amplified Danielle's discredited Washington accusations; recruitment of subordinate Rebecca Pettit and liaison Officer Ethan Smith; repeated baseless arrests and harassment; recruitment of YWCA client Tyleen Root for online harassment and defamation; MPD's refusal to intervene (alleged joint state-private conspiracy); and YWCA's institutional indifference.
- The December notice detailed federal causes of action: § 1983 (First, Fourth, Fourteenth Amendments), § 1985 conspiracy, state tort defamation/IIED, and civil/criminal RICO (18 U.S.C. § 1962).
- **As of the declaration (60+ days after the first notice; 1+ month after the second): YWCA provided no response whatsoever** — no acknowledgment, investigation, or corrective action. The declaration argues this establishes consciousness of institutional guilt, deliberate indifference supporting *Monell* liability, and the futility of internal remedies.

## IV. E'Lise Chard's June 2018 petition, perjury, and constitutional violations

- **June 12, 2018** (nine days after the YWCA complaint): E'Lise Chard filed a petition for a temporary order of protection in Missoula County alleging a pattern of threatening, stalking, and harassing conduct, including supposed contact with her children — sworn to under penalty of perjury.
- **The perjury contradiction:** In the written petition she claimed Mr. Nuno had "repeatedly" contacted and harassed her. At the subsequent hearing, under oath and in open court, the judge directly asked whether he had made any contact beyond the three emails sent in 2017 — she **twice answered "no."** The declaration argues both statements cannot be true; at least one version is knowingly false, constituting perjury under MCA § 45-7-202.
- Mr. Nuno did not contest the order, telling the court he was fine with it and had no desire for contact with E'Lise Chard.

## V. The Montana stalking prosecution: eighteen months of constitutional violations

- Criminal stalking charges followed the protection order hearing, based on E'Lise's false allegations and the investigation by Officer Ethan Smith and Detective Connie Brueckner.

**Search warrant based on fabricated evidence and institutional conflict:**
- A search warrant on his Missoula residence was obtained by **Detective Connie Brueckner**, based on an affidavit incorporating investigative reports and statements from Officer Ethan Smith.
- **Officer Smith's fabricated evidence (as alleged):** fabricated phone conversations with Mr. Nuno's parents (explicitly denied by them); false claims his mother "alluded to" mental health issues (denied); mischaracterization of professional IT skills as criminal intent; reliance on E'Lise's uncorroborated allegations.
- Smith was **removed from the case by MPD for "obvious appearance of impropriety"** — his close personal relationship with E'Lise Chard — the sole instance of institutional accountability in the case.
- **Brueckner's undisclosed conflict:** Detective Brueckner simultaneously served on the **YWCA Missoula Board of Directors** — the same organization where E'Lise worked and against which Mr. Nuno had filed the June 2018 complaint. Her conflict was never disclosed to the issuing judge, defense counsel, prosecutors, or Mr. Nuno.
- **Franks v. Delaware analysis (438 U.S. 154, 1978):** The declaration argues the warrant was void — (a) the affidavit contained deliberately false statements or statements made with reckless disregard for the truth (Smith's fabrications), and (b) those false statements were necessary to establish probable cause. Concealment of Brueckner's conflict also violated *Brady v. Maryland* and constituted fraud upon the court.
- **Economic destruction:** Seizure of all work equipment (laptops with proprietary client work, phones with business contacts and credentials, storage devices, tablets) made work impossible; employment was terminated; equipment was retained for the full 18-month investigation; when returned, work product was obsolete and client relationships severed. Alleged losses: employment/income exceeding $150,000 annually; economic damages exceeding **$2.1 million** over 2018–2025; permanent exit from the telecommunications engineering career.
- **Legal violations cited:** Fourth Amendment; *Franks*; *Brady*; Montana professional-conduct rules; 18 U.S.C. § 242; Montana perjury/false-statement statutes.

## VII. Detective Connie Brueckner: weaponizing institutional conflicts

- **Dual roles:** YWCA Board member (with fiduciary duty to the organization, insider knowledge of the June 3, 2018 complaint, awareness of the suppressed Aug 14, 2018 character-reference letter from Witness PS) **and** Missoula PD detective (authority to seek warrants, present evidence, coordinate with prosecutors).
- **Alleged weaponization:** leveraging YWCA's reputation to lend credibility to E'Lise's allegations; suppressing the Witness PS exculpatory letter; protecting YWCA from accountability by prosecuting the complainant; using dual institutional weight to obtain the fraudulent warrant; and shutting down internal YWCA accountability by framing the complainant as the criminal.
- **Recusal required but withheld:** Under law-enforcement ethics and Montana POST standards, Brueckner should have recused from any investigation involving YWCA employees or complaints against YWCA. Instead she concealed the board membership from all parties — a matter of public record (IRS Form 990 filings on ProPublica).
- **Conduct characterized as:** fraud upon the court; *Brady* violation; deprivation of rights under color of law (18 U.S.C. § 242); conspiracy against rights (§ 241); POST professional misconduct; abuse of authority.
- **Discovery only in 2025:** Mr. Nuno learned of the YWCA conflict in 2025 while researching the case — over six years of concealment — supporting **equitable tolling** of the statute of limitations for federal civil rights claims.

## XIV. The 2019 witness intimidation and the impossible catch-22

- In 2019, multiple Washington cases were filed (no-contact violations in Seattle, Lynnwood, Edmonds; misdemeanor stalking in Snohomish County) based largely on Danielle Chard's complaints.
- **The threat:** An Edmonds PD officer allegedly threatened his romantic partner (a widowed single mother) that she would "lose custody of her child and never see her son again" unless she testified against or cooperated in prosecuting him.
- **The catch-22:** (A) expose the threat and risk custody retaliation against Partner A and her child; (B) accept guilty pleas to protect them — sacrificing constitutional rights; no realistic third option existed. He accepted pleas in September 2019.
- The declaration alleges the threat meets the elements of witness intimidation under **18 U.S.C. § 1512** and violated his Fifth and Fourteenth Amendment rights. He states he is prepared to identify Partner A to investigators with appropriate protections.

## XVII. SLAPP principles: weaponization of legal process

- The campaign is characterized as a **criminal SLAPP prosecution** punishing protected First Amendment speech:
  - **Timing:** June 3, 2018 complaint → June 12, 2018 retaliatory petition → immediate stalking charges after Judge Warren's protected-speech finding.
  - **Forum shopping / charge stacking:** simultaneous charges across seven courts in two states (Missoula Municipal, Missoula Superior, Seattle Municipal, Lynnwood Municipal, Edmonds Municipal, Edmonds Superior ×2), forcing five attorneys and an insurmountable financial burden.
  - **Dismissal and re-filing to escalate bail:** $10,000 → $50,000 bail escalation with added felonies; third attempt to raise bail to $100,000 (unsuccessful).
  - **Resource drain:** loss of $150,000 annual IT contract (June 2018); legal fees exceeding **$387,000**; equipment seizure; GPS monitoring restricting travel; loss of $60,000 annual St. Regis School District contract (2024); total career destruction of $2.1 million (2018–2025).
  - **Complete lack of merit:** all charges dismissed with prejudice — demonstrating no probable cause ever existed.
  - **Chilling effect:** forced relocation; effective silencing of further complaints; family agoraphobia and PTSD; deterrence of future complainants.
- **Montana's 2025 anti-SLAPP law:** HB 292 (May 1, 2025) adopts the Uniform Public Expression Protection Act (UPEPA); while not retroactive, it establishes that Montana formally recognizes SLAPP prosecutions as violations of fundamental rights, supporting federal civil rights claims.
- **Federal theories:** § 1983 First Amendment retaliation (*Hartman v. Moore*); malicious prosecution (universal dismissals prove lack of probable cause); abuse of process (dismissal/re-filing to increase bail); § 1985 conspiracy.

## XVIII. Warrantless arrest and unlawful interstate coordination (August 2018)

- **Context:** On GPS monitoring restricted to Missoula County (with a 1,500-yard exclusion zone around the YWCA office), under court-ordered release, with coordinated Washington appearance dates arranged by attorneys Bryan Tipp (MT) and Tim Milios (WA).
- **The fabricated extradition claim:** E'Lise, monitoring his custody status through MPD contacts and YWCA staff, told Danielle he was out on bail; Danielle (who stated she would "never be safe until [he] was behind bars") contacted an Edmonds officer with an established relationship; that officer made an **unofficial back-channel call** to a Montana officer falsely claiming an outstanding extradition warrant.
- **Result:** Montana officers entered his home without a warrant and arrested him. No warrant, no extradition request, no new charges existed; his "failure to appear" was due to GPS restrictions already communicated to all courts.
- **"Lost in jail":** He was held for several days with no documentation of why he was in custody; attorneys had to prove no prosecutor in either state had called for arrest and no warrants existed to secure release.
- **Constitutional violations cited:** Fourth Amendment (warrantless home entry/arrest, arrest on unverified unofficial communication); Fourteenth Amendment due process (detention without charges); interstate-compact/extradition-circumvention violations.
- **Alleged conspiracy table** (per the declaration): E'Lise (monitoring + communication), Danielle (false urgency, goal of keeping him "behind bars"), Edmonds officer (manufactured extradition claim), Montana officer (warrantless arrest), Missoula County Jail (held without charges).

## XX. RICO predicates: organized criminal enterprise

- The declaration alleges the coordinated campaign constitutes a **pattern of racketeering activity** under 18 U.S.C. §§ 1961–1968.
- **Alleged enterprise** (§ 1961(4)): private citizens (E'Lise Chard, Danielle Chard, Rebecca Pettit, Steve Leutcheman, Tyleen Root); YWCA Missoula (institution and employees); law enforcement (Officer Smith, Detective Brueckner, Edmonds officers); agencies (MPD, Edmonds PD, Seattle PD); prosecutors (Missoula County Attorney's Office, King County Prosecutor's Office) — with common purpose (suppression of protected speech), structure, 10+ year longevity, and interstate scope.
- **Chronological predicate acts (≥20 alleged):**
  - Nov 22, 2015 — mail/wire fraud (§§ 1341, 1343): false murder-suicide report triggering SWAT raid
  - Mar 2016 — Hobbs Act extortion (§ 1951): coerced guilty plea
  - Dec 2015–Aug 2016 — deprivation of rights (§ 242): advancement of baseless charges
  - Jun 2017 — conspiracy against rights (§ 241): recruitment of Officer Smith
  - Jun 2017–May 2018 — obstruction of justice (§ 1503): off-record harassment
  - Jun 12, 2018 — mail/wire fraud: protection order petition with false statements
  - Jun 2018 — perjury (§ 1621): contradictory sworn testimony before Judge Warren
  - Jun–Sep 2018 — conspiracy (§ 241): YWCA-police coordination, undisclosed conflict
  - Jul 2018 — deprivation of rights (§ 242): home raid, seizure of all electronics
  - Aug 14, 2018 — obstruction (§ 1503): suppression of Witness PS letter
  - Aug 2018 — mail/wire fraud: interstate communication of custody status
