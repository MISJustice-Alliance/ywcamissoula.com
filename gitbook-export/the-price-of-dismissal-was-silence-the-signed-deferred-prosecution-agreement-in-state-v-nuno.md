# The Price of Dismissal Was Silence: The Signed Deferred Prosecution Agreement in State v. Nuno

**MISJustice Alliance — case record article, DC-18-587 (Missoula County, Montana)**

Until this week, everything this archive has published about the deferred prosecution agreement that ended State v. Nuno, No. DC-18-587, rested on paraphrase: grievances describing its conditions, correspondence referring to it, and a dismissal order that referenced it without reproducing it. The archive's own tolling analysis stated the limit plainly — the signed agreement had not been located, and the record therefore supported "a preservation and investigation issue, not a published finding."

On November 23, 2026, the Missoula County Attorney's Office provided the executed agreement in response to a November 17 request. The signed instrument is now in the record, and it is linked in full below. What it documents is the subject of this article: a criminal case resolved through deferred prosecution conditioned on removal of specified existing public posts. Whether those posts were protected, whether copies survive, and whether the condition was lawful are questions this article examines.

## I. What the agreement required

The “Waiver and Agreement to Defer Prosecution” associated with DC-18-587 was executed on **December 11, 2019**. It is signed by Elvis R. Nuno, by his defense counsel Bryan C. Tipp, and by Deputy County Attorney Brian C. Lowney, the prosecuting attorney. Raymond P. Tipp notarized Nuno’s signature on December 11; the attorneys’ signatures were not separately notarized. It provides a one-year deferral of prosecution, with a return to the County Attorney’s Office with counsel for a compliance review on December 11, 2020.

Its conditions include:

- No contact with E'Lise Chard;
- Abiding by the order of protection in OP-2018-120;
- Compliance with conditions in “Edmunds Municipal Court (WA) Case 5Z1231987” (the agreement spells the city “Edmunds”) and Missoula TK-2017-11037, and with another deferred prosecution agreement including Missoula TK-2018-8237; and
- Removal within two days of signature of public Internet posts Nuno created or facilitated that were directed to E’Lise Chard or her employer.

Condition 5 (page 3) reads:

> The Defendant shall remove any public Internet posting created or facilitated by the Defendant directed to E’Lise or her employer within two (2) days of his signature on this agreement. Those postings include, but are not limited to, the Defendant’s Google review of the YWCA, Defendant’s Yelp review of the YWCA, and any posting on the YWCA’s Facebook page regarding E’Lise.

That last condition is the one this article examines. It was not incidental to the agreement. It was a stated term of the bargain under which the State agreed to defer prosecution; a later order dismissed DC-18-587 with prejudice.

## II. The removal condition and the reported protected-speech ruling

The agreement stipulates that Nuno emailed the YWCA on August 1, 2018 and posted that email to its Facebook page on August 24, characterizing those acts as violations of the protection order. The filed Lowney grievance describes Judge Warren as having ruled a YWCA complaint protected speech; the hearing record is needed to establish that ruling’s precise language and whether it covered the later posts. The agreement required removal of existing public posts but does not state a general twelve-month ban on Nuno’s speech. The one-year period was the deferral of prosecution.

The sequence matters: a complaint and subsequent criticism entered the protection-order and criminal-case record. The deferred-prosecution agreement then required removal of posts directed to Chard or her employer. Whether each post fell within the earlier reported protected-speech ruling requires the hearing record and the posts themselves.

## III. A constitutional right as the price of dismissal

The grievances filed over this prosecution — against Tipp (ODC 25-147) and against Lowney (ODC 26-145) — frame the condition in the terms the doctrine supplies:

- **Unconstitutional conditions.** [*Perry v. Sindermann*, 408 U.S. 593, 597–98 (1972)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/408/593), as invoked in the Tipp review request, holds that government may not deny a benefit because of protected speech, even when the recipient has no independent entitlement to that benefit. *Perry* involved public employment, not a negotiated criminal disposition. Dismissal of a prosecution is among the most valuable benefits the State controls; conditioning it on the removal of protected speech warrants constitutional scrutiny, not a presumption that this term has been adjudicated unlawful.
- **Scrutiny of government-demanded waivers.** The Commission on Practice review request in 26-145 invokes three different authorities: [*Town of Newton v. Rumery*, 480 U.S. 386 (1987)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/480/386) rejects a categorical ban on voluntary agreements releasing § 1983 claims in exchange for dismissal while considering voluntariness, prosecutorial overreach, and the public interest; [*MacDonald v. Musick*, 425 F.2d 373 (9th Cir. 1970)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/425/373/175172), reversed a conviction after a prosecutor withdrew a proposed dismissal when the defendant refused a probable-cause stipulation aimed at impeding a civil action; and [*Davies v. Grossmont Union High School District*, 930 F.2d 1390 (9th Cir. 1991)](https://app.midpage.ai/document/thomas-davies-v-grossmont-union-559562), declined to enforce a civil settlement term restricting candidacy for a school board without an adequate public interest or close nexus. None decided the validity of a DPA requirement to remove online posts.
- **Professional standards.** [ABA Resolution 113E (August 2013)](https://www.americanbar.org/advocacy/governmental_legislative_work/publications/governmental_affairs_periodicals/washingtonletter/2014/october/ineffectivecounsel) opposes plea or sentencing agreements waiving post-conviction claims concerning ineffective assistance of counsel, prosecutorial misconduct, or destruction of evidence, except for specifically identified past conduct. It is nonbinding policy and does not by its terms address this public-post removal condition; the comparison is by analogy. The Lowney grievance additionally frames the condition against a prosecutor's Rule 3.8 duty to seek justice rather than merely convictions.

No court decision identified here adjudicates the constitutionality of the removal condition. The DPA shows the term; it does not establish who proposed it, whether it was negotiated, what alternatives were considered, or whether a court separately reviewed it.

## IV. Removal of public posts, required as a condition

The removal condition addressed existing public speech, not merely future conduct. Removal of a public-facing copy is distinct from destruction of the underlying content or every recoverable copy.

The agreement names a Google review, a Yelp review, and a YWCA Facebook post regarding E’Lise as examples; it does not reproduce those posts or direct destruction of the June 3 letter, grievance, or supporting materials. The archive’s earlier evidence-preservation analysis reports that Nuno performed the removal to obtain or preserve the disposition. The signed term confirms the removal obligation, but platform exports, screenshots, archived copies, and negotiation records remain necessary to establish what was removed, what survives, and who proposed the term.

The potential evidentiary consequence is structural: requiring a defendant to take down existing public posts can make later review depend on secondary copies, even when the content remains recoverable. This agreement establishes the removal requirement, not unlawful destruction by an individual. The DPA itself was not destroyed; it survives and is published here.

## V. The deferral period in practice

The deferral ran for one year. The Lowney grievance alleges that, during this period, individuals connected to E’Lise Chard and the YWCA engaged in a campaign of defamation and business harassment against Nuno and Pacific NW Rural Broadband Alliance — including public posts calling him a "stalker and a woman beater," allegations about customer privacy, and messages to prospective customers carrying booking photographs and disputed narratives. The grievance alleges Lowney knew of the reported campaign and did not intervene. The pending Rule 14 request presents this as allegation S-2; neither the grievance nor the DPA establishes that the campaign occurred or that Lowney had such knowledge. The DPA’s removal term should not be described as a general year-long speech ban.

A removal condition can operate asymmetrically if others continue posting while the subject must take down his posts. The grievance’s account warrants investigation, but the agreement alone does not verify the alleged third-party conduct.

## VI. Where it stands

Tipp's letter of December 23, 2020 to Lowney — which itself referred to complaints about Nuno's "exercise of his constitutional right of free speech" — recorded Lowney's acknowledgment that Nuno had complied with the agreement. Repository accounts report a January 5, 2021 order signed by Judge Halligan dismissing DC-18-587 with prejudice; the signed order and its precise scope were not independently inspected for this article.

The Tipp and Lowney disciplinary proceedings reached adverse dispositions on their own stated grounds, without a judicial merits ruling on the DPA removal condition. The DPA conditions are among the allegations designated S-1 in the later Rule 14 review request. Nuno reports supplying the executed agreement to the Commission on Practice on November 24, 2026 as a supplement to a pending request; this article has not independently verified receipt or the Commission’s action. The agreement establishes the execution date previously described in that request as "[DATE UNVERIFIED]."
