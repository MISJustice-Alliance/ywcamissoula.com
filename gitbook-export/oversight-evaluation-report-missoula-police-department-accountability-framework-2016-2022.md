## 1. Institutional Context and Strategic Evaluation Framework

### Audit Scope and Objective

This evaluation conducts a systemic, data-driven analysis of the Missoula Police Department's (MPD) internal and external oversight mechanisms between 2016 and 2022. In municipal governance, police accountability is not merely a legal requirement but a strategic pillar of public legitimacy. The objective of this report is to identify "accountability asymmetry" — the structural gap between the documented use of police authority and the functional ability of the public to seek redress for grievances.

### Strategic Synthesis

By evaluating the documented patterns of the MPD framework, this report moves beyond the analysis of isolated incidents to scrutinize the "architecture of failure." The scope focuses on how structural design — including self-investigatory internal affairs, restricted municipal commission authority, and state-level triage policies — creates a closed-loop system. This design inherently shields institutional actors and suppresses civilian-origin complaints, resulting in a framework that is optimized for early closure rather than substantive adjudication. These statistical anomalies serve as the primary "red flags" necessitating this audit.

The following section benchmarks MPD performance against national standards to quantify this accountability gap.

## 2. Quantitative Performance Benchmarking: Complaint Trends and Disposition Data

Statistical sustain rates — the frequency with which an agency validates a civilian grievance — serve as the primary quantitative indicator of an oversight system's health and public legitimacy. Disparities in these rates often signal structural barriers that discourage reporting or indicate a bias toward officer exoneration.

Note: The ambiguity in the total sustained count (56 vs. 16) arises from conflicting interpretations of aggregate percentage data versus raw counts provided in public records. Forensic analysis suggests the 16% rate is likely padded by "General Misconduct" findings to mask failure in high-liability categories.

### Comparative Performance Evaluation

* **Overall Sustain Rate vs. National Average**: MPD's 16% sustain rate ostensibly doubles the DOJ national average (8%). However, forensic breakdown reveals a 0% sustain rate for Use-of-Force and Discrimination complaints. The overall rate is effectively buoyed by low-liability "General Misconduct" findings, creating a facade of activity while providing zero accountability for constitutional violations.
* **Accountability Section Score**: MPD received a score of 27/100, placing it in the bottom 27% of comparable U.S. departments. This score highlights a systemic failure in independent oversight and policy transparency.
* **Racial Disparity and Credibility Gaps**: Black residents in Missoula are 9.7x more likely to be arrested for low-level offenses — a figure that is 2.6 times the national median. The concurrent 0% sustain rate for discrimination complaints suggests a closed-loop system that ignores clear population-level data as evidence of disparate treatment.

These anomalies in complaint disposition are most visible when contrasted with the volume of physical force documented by the department.

## 3. The Use-of-Force Paradox: Incident Volume vs. Reporting Barriers

Monitoring Use-of-Force (UoF) incidents is the primary safeguard against constitutional violations. A significant statistical gap between the application of force and the reporting of grievances suggests severe structural barriers to the civilian complaint process.

### Statistical Disparity

In 2021 and 2022, MPD documented 505 UoF incidents. However, the total number of UoF complaints filed over the entire seven-year audit period was 4. This results in a ratio of 126 UoF incidents for every one civilian complaint filed.

### Structural Analysis of the "Gap"

According to Department of Justice (DOJ) benchmarks, a department of MPD's size (~114 officers) should expect 63–70 UoF complaints over seven years. The actual count of 4 represents a 99% deviation from federal expectations. This statistical outlier suggests that the near-total absence of complaints is the product of structural discouragement rather than exceptional professional conduct.

### Structural Barriers to Complaint Filing

1. **Fear of Retaliation**: High-risk populations often avoid formal processes due to the perceived risk of further law enforcement attention following adverse contact.
2. **Discouragement at Intake**: Investigative data suggests "gatekeeping" where potential complainants are informed their grievances do not qualify for investigation before a formal record is created.
3. **Lack of Independent Intake Mechanisms**: Because complaint intake is routed entirely through the MPD's Office of Professional Standards (OPS), the accused agency serves as the primary gatekeeper for all civilian grievances.

These internal barriers are reinforced by the procedural limitations of the local and state oversight architecture.

## 4. Structural Analysis of Oversight Architecture: Local to State

Independent oversight is essential to check internal police investigations. In the Missoula framework, however, both local and state bodies are designed with restricted enforcement capabilities.

### The Missoula Police Commission

The Missoula Police Commission lacks the authority to conduct independent investigations. Chair Dan Doyle testified that the commission "does not tell the police department what to do" and primarily reviews matters that are "not the serious matters." Relying entirely on MPD's OPS for data, the commission functions as a non-binding advisory body rather than an independent auditor.

### Montana POST Council: Architecture of Early Closure

The state-level Montana POST Council utilizes a "Case Status Committee" to triage complaints, often resulting in early closure. Procedural rules require civilian complainants to first engage the accused agency and submit that agency's response. This requirement empowers the department under investigation to frame the record before POST review even begins.

### Case Study: The Nuno Dismissal (August 2025)

The failure of this triage system is evidenced by the Nuno complaint.

* **Submission**: August 11, 2025.
* **Dismissal**: August 13, 2025. The dismissal of a complex, multi-document complaint occurred in approximately 15 hours. The review was conducted by an Advanced Certified Paralegal (Katrina Bolger), whose rapid closure suggests a lack of substantive review and a reliance on narrow temporal rules to exclude patterns of misconduct that manifest over long periods.

This structural shielding is further maintained by significant transparency gaps.

## 5. Transparency Gaps and Institutional Interdependencies

The "Right to Know" is a constitutional mandate in Montana. However, the City of Missoula's position on record access has created an "Opacity Problem" that prevents meaningful public audit.

### The Opacity Problem

The City routinely cites the personnel records exemption (ARM 2.21.6612) to refuse the release of redacted complaint files. This stance contradicts the Montana Constitution and MCA § 2-6-1003, which prioritizes the public interest in government performance over individual privacy in matters of official conduct.

### Institutional Capture: Red Flags

Forensic evidence suggests a theory of institutional capture involving the YWCA Missoula and local law enforcement:

* **Conflict of Interest**: Detective Connie Brueckner served on the YWCA Board while simultaneously investigating matters involving YWCA staff.
* **Confidentiality Breaches**: Client testimony alleges counseling information was leaked from YWCA channels into adversarial police and court settings.
* **Grant-Reporting Misrepresentation**: The October 2021 Meadowlark Shelter evictions and the Rapid Rehousing payment cap mismatch suggest that the service model reported to federal funders does not align with operational realities on the ground.

### Civil Rights Frames

These coordinated responses are analyzed under 42 U.S.C. Section 1985 (Civil Rights Conspiracy) and RICO enterprise theories. The documented pattern suggests a system where grievances are converted into "prosecution-supporting narratives" to suppress protected speech and dissent.

## 6. Remediation Pathways and Policy Recommendations

Shifting from a self-investigatory model to a transparent, independent oversight framework is a strategic necessity for restoring public confidence.

### Systemic Reform Requirements

* **Establish Genuine Independence**: Create a civilian-led intake and investigative mechanism entirely external to the MPD and the Mayor's Office.
* **Governance Decoupling**: Immediately remove all law enforcement personnel from the governance boards of victim advocacy organizations.
* **Immediate Confidentiality Protection**: Implement rigorous data-access controls to prevent the leakage of victim counseling information to adversarial parties.
* **Enhanced Financial Oversight**: Conduct independent audits of federal grant compliance and housing outcome reporting.

### Targeted FOIA Priorities

### Final Assessment

This evaluation concludes that the internal governance of the Missoula Police Department is structurally unable to resolve these conflicts. The current architecture is optimized for institutional protection rather than accountability. Without independent financial oversight and federal intervention, these patterns are unlikely to be corrected. These findings are empirically grounded in the statistical and procedural records of the 2016–2022 period.
