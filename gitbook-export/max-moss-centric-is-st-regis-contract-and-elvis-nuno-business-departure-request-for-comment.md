> **Record status:** The account below is principally Elvis Nuno's firsthand account. It is not a judicial finding. The present record does not establish that Mr. Moss originated the reported assault characterization, intended the consequences Mr. Nuno describes, or acted unlawfully. Those questions remain open.

## Why this inquiry matters

MISJustice Alliance has asked Mr. Moss to explain the reported connection among a late-2023 roadside arrest, restrictions on Mr. Nuno's access to St. Regis school facilities, the loss of school IT work in 2024, and Mr. Nuno's departure from the business associated with Centric IS.

According to Mr. Nuno, his truck struck a deer near St. Regis and became disabled. He states that he was off the roadway awaiting towing and family assistance when law enforcement stopped, questioned him, discovered an outstanding bench warrant arising from an earlier traffic matter, and arrested him.

Mr. Nuno alleges that the event was later described to people connected with the school as an arrest involving assault. The distinction is consequential. A traffic-related bench warrant and an allegation of violent conduct communicate fundamentally different things about a person's character and perceived fitness to enter a school, hold keys and access credentials, maintain information systems, and occupy a position of professional trust.

Mr. Nuno describes the warrant as traffic-related and states that no assault charge arose from the deer-strike roadside encounter. The source, wording, recipients, and path of the reported assault characterization have not yet been independently established through primary court or school records.

## Reported professional and economic consequences

Mr. Nuno states that, after the arrest information reached the St. Regis school community:

* his keys and door-access codes were revoked;
* he could return onsite only when accompanied by a business partner;
* the school work went to another IT contractor when the arrangement was reviewed in 2024; and
* the resulting loss of work and revenue was later used as leverage to pressure him out of the combined business.

He describes these events as career- and reputation-destroying. In his account, the harm was not limited to embarrassment from an arrest. The reported assault label allegedly affected the trust required for his work, removed his independent access to a major client, contributed to a substantial contract loss, weakened his position inside the business, and continued to damage his standing in a small professional community.

Mr. Nuno characterizes the circulation and use of the assault narrative as defamatory. That legal characterization has not been adjudicated. The factual questions come first: what was said, whether it was false, who communicated it, what each recipient knew, whether anyone verified it, and which decisions it influenced.

## Questions sent to Mr. Moss

The October 11 request asks Mr. Moss to address:

1. **The business relationship.** How were Centric IS, Mr. Nuno's nonprofit organization, and any combined enterprise legally structured? What responsibilities, compensation, governance authority, or ownership interests did each person have?
2. **The separation.** Was Mr. Nuno's departure voluntary, negotiated, or required? What changed in his role or economic interest, when, and at whose direction?
3. **Information about the arrest.** When did Mr. Moss first learn of the arrest, from whom, and what did he understand the warrant to involve?
4. **The reported assault characterization.** Did Mr. Moss receive or relay a statement suggesting assault, violence, drugs, or another matter beyond a traffic-related warrant? If so, what was the source, and did he attempt to verify or correct it?
5. **School access.** What role, if any, did Mr. Moss have in discussions about the background check, revoked access, or accompaniment requirement?
6. **The 2024 contract decision.** Why did the school work go to another provider, was a reason given in writing, and how was the financial effect assessed?
7. **The alleged use of leverage.** Did the arrest, access restrictions, reputational concerns, or contract loss enter discussions about Mr. Nuno leaving the business or relinquishing a role or interest?
8. **Records and corrections.** What agreements, correspondence, meeting records, financial materials, or other nonprivileged records support Mr. Moss's account?

## Legal context under Montana and federal law

This section identifies potentially relevant doctrine. It does not conclude that any claim is timely, that an exception or privilege does not apply, or that the evidence satisfies the elements of a cause of action.

Montana's slander statute requires a false and unprivileged publication and expressly addresses statements that charge a person with a crime or directly injure the person in an office, profession, trade, or business. [Mont. Code Ann. § 27-1-803 (2025)](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0270/chapter_0010/part_0080/section_0030/0270-0010-0080-0030.html). The Montana Supreme Court has explained that actionable language must be capable of tending to disgrace or degrade a person or cause the person to be shunned, and that courts evaluate whether the statement is capable of bearing a defamatory meaning. *[McConkey v. Flathead Electric Cooperative](https://erd.dli.mt.gov/_docs/labor-standards/McConkey-v-Flathead-Electric-Cooperative.pdf)*, 2005 MT 334, ¶¶ 44–49, 330 Mont. 48, 125 P.3d 1121.

Montana authority has also treated false statements charging criminal conduct or directly injuring a trade or business as potentially actionable without proof of special damages. *[McCusker v. Roberts](https://hallapproved.com/mt/cases/supreme/1969/1164497/)*, 152 Mont. 513, 523–24, 452 P.2d 408, 414 (1969). In *[Blue Ridge Homes, Inc. v. Thein](https://law.justia.com/cases/montana/supreme-court/2008/53626f2e-366c-42e4-97d9-bacf06090837.html)*, 2008 MT 264, ¶ 37, 345 Mont. 125, 191 P.3d 374, the court reiterated that defamation per se does not require proof of special damages. Those rules do not eliminate the need to prove falsity, publication, lack of privilege, fault, identity, and any other applicable elements or defenses.

Montana recognizes privileges for certain official and interest-based communications. *[Small v. McRae](https://law.justia.com/cases/montana/supreme-court/1982/b6e6ee6f-6730-4145-93d8-b67352a17fb1.html)*, 200 Mont. 497, 524, 651 P.2d 982, 998 (1982), applied Montana's official-duty privilege to an internal communication explaining a public-college personnel decision. By contrast, *[Sacco v. High Country Independent Press, Inc.](https://law.justia.com/cases/montana/supreme-court/1995/eafab6e0-f333-4c42-9370-110c4caabf74.html)*, 271 Mont. 209, 234–36, 896 P.2d 411, 426–27 (1995), explains that not every accusation made before an arrest or official proceeding falls within an official-proceeding privilege. The exact speaker, audience, purpose, and context therefore matter.

Federal constitutional law supplies additional limits in defamation cases. *[Gertz v. Robert Welch, Inc.](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/418/323/)*, 418 U.S. 323, 347–50 (1974), bars states from imposing liability without fault for defamatory falsehoods about private individuals and limits presumed or punitive damages in matters of public concern absent the required showing. *[Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co.](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/1/)*, 497 U.S. 1, 18–21 (1990), explains that labeling a statement “opinion” does not create categorical immunity when the statement can reasonably imply a provably false factual assertion. If a plaintiff is a public official or public figure, *[New York Times Co. v. Sullivan](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/376/254/)*, 376 U.S. 254, 279–80 (1964), requires proof of knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for truth.

The claimed business consequences may also raise a distinct interference question, but Montana requires more than temporal sequence. A plaintiff must establish intentional and willful conduct calculated to cause business damage, an unlawful purpose without right or justifiable cause, and resulting loss. *[Grenfell v. Anderson](https://openjurist.org/2002/mt/225/grenfell-v-anderson-886036)*, 2002 MT 225, ¶¶ 64, 75, 311 Mont. 385, 56 P.3d 326. *Grenfell* rejected the claim where the relevant sublease had already ended and the challenged action rested on a good-faith belief that it was justified. *Id.* ¶¶ 66, 75. That limitation is important here: causation, purpose, justification, and the division of responsibility among the school and business actors require evidence rather than inference.

## The unresolved accountability issue

If the record confirms that decision-makers treated a traffic-warrant arrest as evidence of assault or violent conduct without checking the underlying records, the resulting damage would be extreme in relation to the event actually at issue. In a small community, a violent-crime label can become self-reinforcing: suspicion produces restricted access; restricted access damages the client relationship; the damaged relationship contributes to contract loss; and the contract loss is then used to weaken the individual's standing inside the business.

That sequence is Mr. Nuno's allegation, not a finding. It remains possible that the contract changed for independent reasons, that the business separation had unrelated causes, that Mr. Moss did not repeat the reported misinformation, or that he opposed rather than advanced the restrictions. The request for comment gives him an opportunity to document those possibilities.
