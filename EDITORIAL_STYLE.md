# Editorial Style and Accountability Policy

Required for drafting, rewriting, summarizing, translating, titling, or reviewing public-facing bodies, headlines, captions, metadata, structured data, and derivatives. Read with root [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) and nested instructions. It supplements source, privacy, legal, independence, and authorization safeguards; it grants no external-action permission. If inaccessible, report the blocker and do not mark content publication-ready. This is not an automated control.

## Mission, fairness, and direct accountability

This independent civil-rights advocacy publication documents alleged misconduct, exposes supported abuses, explains barriers to redress, and makes the strongest evidence-supported case for people harmed. It is not a neutral platform for laundering institutional talking points or manufacturing equivalence between documented harm and unsupported denial. It is not affiliated with YWCA of Missoula; preserve non-affiliation and other applicable disclaimers.

Take a position for civil rights, due process, accountability, and meaningful remedies without presuming every allegation true. State supported criticism plainly; revise, narrow, or withdraw it when adverse evidence requires. Neither official admission nor final judgment is a universal prerequisite. Evidence may establish misconduct, weaken an allegation, support an alternative explanation, or leave the issue unresolved.

Give no editorial quarter to substantiated misconduct: do not excuse, sanitize, euphemize, minimize, or normalize supported abuses of power. Apply that standard through evidence, specificity, and accountability—not unsupported accusations, abuse, or prejudgment. Do not soften supported criticism for reputation, insert speculative good intentions, or call serious supported conduct a “misunderstanding” or “communication issue” without evidence. Do not defer to status, credentials, or PR language. Explain material context, not automatic excuses. Scrutinize conduct, not irrelevant personal traits. “No quarter” does not permit threats, harassment, doxing, dehumanization, fabrication, suppression of exculpatory material, or unsupported liability claims.

Do not flatten the account into ‘both sides disagree.’ Fairness is accuracy, material completeness, and honest treatment of contrary evidence—not equal protection for powerful actors. Neither symmetrical attribution, equal word counts, nor a manufactured midpoint substitutes for evidence. An allegation establishes that an allegation was made; a denial establishes that a denial was made. Neither settles the proposition or makes a generic denial a rebuttal of records. Presume neither side correct because of status, identity, sympathy, repetition, or advocacy. Do not let official messaging displace chronology, conclude vaguely that truth lies in the middle, or cushion criticism with unrelated praise. Scrutinize institutional and complainant claims alike; do not demand impossible proof from one while accepting unsupported assertions from the other.

## Affected person, agency, and chronology

Organize the account around conduct; the affected person's attributed words; supporting and limiting evidence; institutional power; implicated rights and duties; documented versus alleged consequences; attempts at protection or redress and responses; unresolved questions; and proportionate requested action. Preserve agency, chronology, and documented harm rather than reducing a person to a case number. Do not invent emotions, diagnoses, motives, or consequences. Choose “victim,” “survivor,” “complainant,” “plaintiff,” or another descriptor by context, support, and known preference. A label must not settle disputed facts; qualify the conduct, not mechanically the person's identity.

Name supported decision-makers, authority, notice, actions or omissions, dates, explanations, and consequences. Prefer concrete verbs to “controversy arose,” “communication broke down,” or “both parties felt unheard.” Passive voice may protect a source or acknowledge an unknown actor, not hide known responsibility. Attribute another's actual view; state the publication's supported analysis without reflexive “some might argue.” Do not infer intent, causation, coordination, illegality, or breach of duty beyond evidence and law.

## Classify and weight every central contested proposition

Distinguish: (1) what underlying records directly establish; (2) what a named source alleges or reports; (3) what a person or institution denies, acknowledges, or explains; (4) what a court, agency, or other adjudicative body actually decided, including scope and procedural posture; (5) the author's inference and supporting evidence; and (6) what remains unresolved and which missing records could help resolve it. Do not collapse these categories into “conflicting accounts.” Distinguish a document's existence and contents from the truth of each assertion within it. A complaint is not a finding; a sworn declaration is evidence but not automatically an adjudicated finding.

For materially contested facts, make an internal claim–evidence matrix: proposition, source and pinpoint/date, category, contrary material, limitations, permissible wording. Assess relevance, specificity, provenance, firsthand knowledge, contemporaneity, completeness, authenticity where ascertainable, and independent corroboration. Repetitions of one originating account—including prior articles—are not corroboration. Official status does not establish truth; firsthand testimony is not automatically conclusive. Read [DATA_ISSUES.md](DATA_ISSUES.md) before reusing affected MPD statistics and apply unresolved interim limits. Use the [source and record index](gitbook-export/overview/sources-and-record-index.md) and relevant complaint indexes to find artifacts; an index is not proof its links were inspected. Cite the source actually read and pinpoint. Preserve originals; keep matrices out of public copy.

## Denials, adverse evidence, and calibrated certainty

Include material denials, explanations, contrary records, adverse findings, and plausible alternatives near relevant claims. Assess what a response answers and supports. Distinguish supported rebuttal, unsupported denial, procedural disposition, refusal or inability to comment, no response to documented outreach, and no response located in reviewed materials. Silence is not admission; do not invent outreach. If adverse evidence weakens the thesis, change or abandon it, not merely add a cosmetic caveat.

State established facts directly even if disputed; attribute allegations; label inference and uncertainty proposition by proposition. Do not delete necessary qualifications or bury established facts in blanket disclaimers. Missing records do not prove nonoccurrence; absence-of-record conclusions must state search scope, expected recordkeeping, and limits. Do not claim independent corroboration without independent sources.

## Legal and publication-surface accuracy

Distinguish documented conduct, alleged misconduct, supported factual criticism, policy departures, and adjudicated legal violations. Address abuse of power, retaliation, civil-rights violations, and malicious prosecution directly where relevant, but explain the factual basis and calibrate to procedural posture. A dismissal, acquittal, withdrawn charge, or overturned conviction does not by itself prove factual innocence, knowing fabrication, or malicious prosecution; absence of a conviction or civil judgment against an official does not prove no misconduct occurred. Distinguish a person's assertion of innocence, evidence supporting it, and any adjudicated determination. Verify jurisdiction-specific law and applicable dates before stating legal elements, duties, or available remedies. Do not equate procedural disposition with a merits finding without reading the decision.

Apply the same evidentiary limits to headlines, introductions, excerpts, summaries, SEO/GEO/AEO metadata, captions, and structured data: neither elevate allegations into findings, flatten documented conduct, imply equal evidence, nor overstate exoneration or liability. Protect private identifiers. When supported, end with the accountability issue, actor positioned to answer, outstanding record or remedy sought, and its significance. Do not substitute generic reconciliation for unresolved harm; distinguish requested action from adjudicated duty or legal entitlement.

## Manual publication-readiness gate

Before marking a draft publication-ready, use a reviewer other than the drafting agent when available. Otherwise disclose the limitation, make a separate review pass, and do not claim independent validation. Mark **REVISE** or **BLOCKED**, with reasons, until corrected or supported if the draft:

- Summarizes a central dispute without examining available underlying evidence; flattens supported misconduct into generic disagreement; treats a denial as documentary rebuttal; or centers official messaging over the affected person's material account and underlying evidence.
- Conceals material contrary evidence, supported agency or chronology; sanitizes conduct; adds unsupported institutional excuses or reputation cushioning; or substitutes rhetoric for a documented chain of reasoning.
- Overstates a complaint, declaration, official response, or procedural ruling; infers guilt, intent, causation, innocence, liability, or malicious prosecution without adequate support; or lacks an adequate source for a material factual assertion.
- Publishes a headline, caption, metadata, or structured-data claim beyond the supported body; or asserts missing records, outreach, independent corroboration, or adjudicated findings without support.

The reviewer must answer: What is the strongest supported conclusion, and is it stated plainly? What materially contradicts it, and is that addressed? Has caution improved precision or merely softened accountability? Has advocacy improved focus or exceeded the record? A negative answer needs a specific correction, not “add balance.” Report **PASS / REVISE / BLOCKED**, each defect and location, needed evidence or qualification, and remaining uncertainties. This manual gate grants no publication approval; claim automated controls or actual review only after inspection and testing or observation, respectively.

## Hypothetical reasoning examples (invented; not repository findings or templates)

1. Unacceptable: “The complainant says the agency ignored the complaint; the agency disagrees.” Acceptable only if the described records exist: “Delivery records show the agency received the complaint on [date]. Its response addresses [issue A] but does not address [issue B]. The agency denies mishandling the complaint. The available records establish receipt and the response's contents; they do not establish why issue B was omitted.” Receipt and omission are record-bounded; motive is not invented.
2. Unacceptable: “The court dismissed the case, proving the allegations were false.” Acceptable only if supported by the ruling: “The court dismissed the case on [procedural ground]. The ruling did not decide whether the alleged conduct occurred.”
3. Unacceptable: “The complainant and the agency have different perspectives on whether the complaint was handled appropriately.” Preferred only if supported: “The agency acknowledged receiving the complaint on [date]. Its closure letter did not address the attached [record] or explain whether [investigative step] occurred. The agency denies mishandling the matter, but that denial does not answer those specific omissions. The available records support scrutiny of the complaint-handling process; they do not, by themselves, establish the decision-maker's motive.” This identifies institutional action, evaluates the response against evidence, preserves uncertainty about motive, and keeps accountability visible.

These invented examples illustrate reasoning, not mandatory prose.
